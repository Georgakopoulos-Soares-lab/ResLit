                     Pseudomonas aeruginosa is an environmental bacterium involved in mineralization of organic matter. It is also an opportunistic pathogen able to cause serious infections in immunocompromised hosts. As such, it is exposed to xenobiotics including solvents, heavy metals, and antimicrobials. We studied the response of P. aeruginosa upon exposure to heavy metals or antibiotics to investigate whether common regulatory mechanisms govern resistance to both types of compounds. We showed that sublethal zinc concentrations induced resistance to zinc, cadmium, and cobalt, while lethal zinc concentrations selected mutants constitutively resistant to these heavy metals. Both zinc-induced and stable zinc-resistant strains were also resistant to the carbapenem antibiotic imipenem. On the other hand, only 20% of clones selected on imipenem were also resistant to zinc. Heavy metal resistance in the mutants could be correlated by quantitative real time PCR with increased expression of the heavy metal efflux pump CzcCBA and its cognate two-component regulator genes czcR-czcS. Western blot analysis revealed reduced expression of the basic amino acid and carbapenem-specific OprD porin in all imipenem-resistant mutants. Sequencing of the czcR-czcS DNA region in eight independent zinc- and imipenem-resistant mutants revealed the presence of the same V194L mutation in the CzcS sensor protein. Overexpression in a susceptible wild type strain of the mutated CzsS protein, but not of the wild type form, resulted in decreased oprD and increased czcC expression. We further show that zinc is released from latex urinary catheters into urine in amounts sufficient to induce carbapenem resistance in P. aeruginosa, possibly compromising treatment of urinary tract infections by this class of antibiotics.

            <ce:abstract id="ceab10" xml:lang="en" view="all" class="author"><ce:abstract-sec id="ceabs10" view="all">
                  <ce:simple-para id="spara140" view="all">
                     <ce:italic>Pseudomonas aeruginosa</ce:italic> is an environmental bacterium involved in mineralization of organic matter. It is also an opportunistic pathogen able to cause serious infections in immunocompromised hosts. As such, it is exposed to xenobiotics including solvents, heavy metals, and antimicrobials. We studied the response of <ce:italic>P. aeruginosa</ce:italic> upon exposure to heavy metals or antibiotics to investigate whether common regulatory mechanisms govern resistance to both types of compounds. We showed that sublethal zinc concentrations induced resistance to zinc, cadmium, and cobalt, while lethal zinc concentrations selected mutants constitutively resistant to these heavy metals. Both zinc-induced and stable zinc-resistant strains were also resistant to the carbapenem antibiotic imipenem. On the other hand, only 20% of clones selected on imipenem were also resistant to zinc. Heavy metal resistance in the mutants could be correlated by quantitative real time PCR with increased expression of the heavy metal efflux pump CzcCBA and its cognate two-component regulator genes <ce:italic>czcR-czcS</ce:italic>. Western blot analysis revealed reduced expression of the basic amino acid and carbapenem-specific OprD porin in all imipenem-resistant mutants. Sequencing of the <ce:italic>czcR-czcS</ce:italic> DNA region in eight independent zinc- and imipenem-resistant mutants revealed the presence of the same V194L mutation in the CzcS sensor protein. Overexpression in a susceptible wild type strain of the mutated CzsS protein, but not of the wild type form, resulted in decreased <ce:italic>oprD</ce:italic> and increased <ce:italic>czcC</ce:italic> expression. We further show that zinc is released from latex urinary catheters into urine in amounts sufficient to induce carbapenem resistance in <ce:italic>P. aeruginosa</ce:italic>, possibly compromising treatment of urinary tract infections by this class of antibiotics.</ce:simple-para>

            <ce:sections>
               <ce:para id="para10" view="all">
                  <ce:italic>Pseudomonas aeruginosa</ce:italic> is a Gram-negative bacterium thriving in environments polluted with organic matter. It is also an opportunistic pathogen frequently encountered in the hospital, causing morbidity and mortality in immunocompromised and cystic fibrosis patients (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref220" refid="bib1">1</ce:cross-ref>). <ce:italic>P. aeruginosa</ce:italic> is characterized by an intrinsically high level of resistance to xenobiotics including antimicrobial agents, solvents, and heavy metals (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref230" refid="bib2">2</ce:cross-ref>), which can be accounted for by a combination of its low outer membrane permeability and the presence of multiple efflux pumps (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref240" refid="bib3">3</ce:cross-ref>). These pumps belong to the resistance, nodulation, cell division (RND)<ce:cross-ref id="cecref250" refid="FN2">
                     <ce:sup loc="post">1</ce:sup>
                  </ce:cross-ref>
                  <ce:footnote id="FN2">
                     <ce:label>1</ce:label>
                     <ce:note-para id="cenpara90" view="all">The abbreviations used are: RND, resistance, nodulation, cell division; TYG, tryptone-yeast extract-glucose; MTC, maximal tolerable concentration; qRT-PCR, quantitative real time PCR; LUC, latex urinary catheter.</ce:note-para>
                  </ce:footnote> transporter family, present in many Gram-negative bacteria (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref260" refid="bib4">4</ce:cross-ref>). To extrude substrates from the cytoplasm across the two membranes, these systems are composed of a proton antiporter located in the cytoplasmic membrane, a membrane fusion protein spanning the periplasmic space, and an outer membrane protein (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref270" refid="bib5">5</ce:cross-ref>). Members of the RND family, namely the Mex pumps, have recently gained increasing interest. In particular, the constitutively expressed MexAB-OprM (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref280" refid="bib6">6</ce:cross-ref>, <ce:cross-ref id="cecref290" refid="bib7">7</ce:cross-ref>) and the inducible MexXY (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref300" refid="bib8">8</ce:cross-ref>) efflux pumps endow the PAO1 reference strain and other clinical isolates (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref310" refid="bib9">9</ce:cross-ref>) with a natural resistance to a wide range of antimicrobial agents. Proton-driven RND type efflux pumps conferring heavy metal resistance have been described in <ce:italic>Ralstonia metallidurans</ce:italic> (for a recent review, see Ref. <ce:cross-ref id="cecref320" refid="bib10">10</ce:cross-ref>) and include the Cnr system (nickel/cobalt) (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref330" refid="bib11">11</ce:cross-ref>), the Ncc system (nickel/cobalt/cadmium) (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref340" refid="bib12">12</ce:cross-ref>), and the Czc system (cobalt/zinc/cadmium) (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref350" refid="bib13">13</ce:cross-ref>, <ce:cross-ref id="cecref360" refid="bib14">14</ce:cross-ref>). In <ce:italic>P. aeruginosa</ce:italic>, an RND type efflux pump called CzrCBA was recently described in an environmental isolate where it contributes to the intrinsic resistance to zinc and cadmium (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref370" refid="bib15">15</ce:cross-ref>). Cross-resistance between heavy metal and antibiotic pumps has not been reported so far. In the few cases where associations have been observed they were either plasmid-mediated (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref380" refid="bib16">16</ce:cross-ref>) or resulted from uncharacterized multiple resistance mechanisms (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref390" refid="bib17">17</ce:cross-ref>, <ce:cross-ref id="cecref400" refid="bib18">18</ce:cross-ref>). In <ce:italic>P. aeruginosa</ce:italic>, the question of heavy metal and antibiotic resistance is of particular concern since this organism is a possible candidate for bioremediation processes where selection of antibiotic resistance upon heavy metal exposure is undesirable. On the other hand, zinc was found to be released from urinary catheters resulting in antibiotic resistance (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref410" refid="bib19">19</ce:cross-ref>, <ce:cross-ref id="cecref420" refid="bib20">20</ce:cross-ref>). Therefore the possibility of cross-resistance selection by either heavy metals or antibiotics is of concern for both environmental and clinical issues. In the present study, we addressed this question by exposing the <ce:italic>P. aeruginosa</ce:italic> reference strain PAO1 to either zinc or to the antibiotic imipenem. Surprisingly exposure to zinc selected strains that were resistant to both heavy metals (zinc, cadmium, and cobalt) and to the carbapenem antibiotic imipenem. Analysis of the underlying mechanism revealed a co-regulation between carbapenem influx and heavy metal efflux. A single amino acid change located in the two-component sensor protein CzcS, regulating heavy metal efflux pump expression, was found to be responsible for the observed cross-resistance in <ce:italic>P. aeruginosa</ce:italic>.</ce:para>
               <ce:section id="cesec10" view="all">
                  <ce:section-title id="cestitle10">EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURES</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:para id="para20" view="all">
                     <ce:italic>Bacterial Strains and Growth Conditions—</ce:italic>Bacterial strains used in this study are listed in <ce:cross-ref id="cecref430" refid="tbl1">Table I</ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:float-anchor refid="tbl1"/>. Strain BdB4 was isolated from soil of a heavy metal-contaminated site (zinc and lead) near Geneva (Bois de Bay) by successive subculturing in tryptone-yeast extract-glucose (TYG) medium (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref440" refid="bib21">21</ce:cross-ref>) provided with increasing ZnCl<ce:inf loc="post">2</ce:inf> concentrations (10–35 m<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps>; dose increase, 5 m<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps>). Strain BdB4 was confirmed as <ce:italic>P. aeruginosa</ce:italic> using an API20NE gallery (Biomérieux, Marcy l’Etoile, France). Luria-Bertani (LB) (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref450" refid="bib22">22</ce:cross-ref>) and TYG medium were used as rich media. Some experiments were performed in liquid mineral medium (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref460" refid="bib13">13</ce:cross-ref>) supplemented with 0.4% glucose and 50 m<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps> sodium Hepes, pH 7.0, instead of Tris buffer. The phosphate content (0.64 m<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps>) of this Hepes-buffered minimal medium minimized the interference of phosphate with heavy metals. Cultures were grown at 37 °C on a rotary shaker in 200-ml Erlenmeyer flasks containing 25 ml of minimal medium.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="para30" view="all">
                     <ce:italic>Isolation of Spontaneous Mutants Resistant to Heavy Metals or Imipenem—</ce:italic>For selection of zinc-resistant mutants, PT5 was inoculated at a final concentration of about 2 × 10<ce:sup loc="post">7</ce:sup> cells/ml in 5 ml of liquid TYG medium in test tubes that were incubated for 5 days at 30 °C. The medium was supplemented with 15 (maximal tolerable concentration (MTC)), 20, 25, or 30 m<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps> ZnCl<ce:inf loc="post">2</ce:inf>. From each condition, clones were isolated on TYG medium by serial dilutions. 50 clones were tested for zinc resistance after growth for 48 h at 30 °C on TYG plates containing 20 m<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps> ZnCl<ce:inf loc="post">2</ce:inf>. 16 independent clones were then tested for antibiotic resistance and tolerance to other heavy metals.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="para40" view="all">PT5 was also inoculated on Mueller-Hinton agar plates and exposed to imipenem-impregnated disks (10 μg, Biomérieux). After overnight incubation at 37 °C, 42 imipenem-resistant clones appearing inside the inhibition zone were picked, streaked on TYG medium, and tested for zinc resistance. Resistant mutants were then tested for tolerance to other metals (cadmium, cobalt, copper, and nickel) and antibiotics (imipenem, ticarcillin, carbenicillin, nalidixic acid, ciprofloxacin, norfloxacin, tetracycline, amikacin, chloramphenicol, and polymyxin B).</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="para50" view="all">
                     <ce:italic>Determination of Heavy Metal and Antibiotic Resistance—</ce:italic>The MTCs of heavy metals were determined on solidified TYG medium containing different concentrations of heavy metal salts. The MTCs were scored in 10 × 10 × 2-cm Sterilin plates (Bibby Sterilin Ltd., Stone, Staffs, UK) provided with 25 compartments. 2-ml of medium were poured into each compartment, plates were air-dried, and subdivisions were inoculated with 10 μl of an overnight culture. The MTC is defined as the highest metal concentration at which growth was still observed after 48 h of incubation at 30 °C.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="para60" view="all">Resistance to antibiotics was determined by the Kirby-Bauer method on Mueller-Hinton agar plates using antibiotic-impregnated disks (Biomérieux). An overnight culture grown at 37 °C in TYG medium was diluted 1:500 in 0.9% NaCl, and 2 ml of the suspension were spread on Mueller-Hinton agar plates. The plate was air-dried, and antibiotic disks were applied. After 1 h of diffusion at room temperature, plates were incubated overnight at 37 °C. Inhibition zones were measured and compared with those of the reference strain PT5.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="para70" view="all">
                     <ce:italic>Killing Curves in Presence of Zinc—</ce:italic>PT5 was grown for 48 h at 37 °C in phosphate-deficient (0.1 m<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps>) minimal medium to increase bioavailability of heavy metals. Cultures were performed in the absence (uninduced control cells) or in the presence of 1 m<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps> ZnCl<ce:inf loc="post">2</ce:inf> (metal-induced cells). Treated cultures were found to exhibit a lag phase of about 24 h followed by a period of active growth. Both cultures were then diluted 1:200 in fresh medium containing either no zinc or a lethal zinc concentration of 5 m<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps>. Cultures were incubated at 37 °C with shaking (150 rpm) for 5 h. Kinetics of killing of uninduced <ce:italic>versus</ce:italic> metal-induced cells was then followed. At defined intervals, samples were removed, diluted in TYG medium, and plated on the same medium for viable counts.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="para80" view="all">
                     <ce:italic>DNA Manipulations—</ce:italic>Standard techniques were used for DNA manipulation (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref470" refid="bib22">22</ce:cross-ref>). The gene encoding the CzcR protein was amplified by PCR from PT5 genomic DNA (<ce:italic>Pfu</ce:italic> DNA polymerase, Promega) with primers czcR-F and czcR-R (see below). The 900-bp product was cloned into the SmaI site of vector pMMB66EH (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref480" refid="bib23">23</ce:cross-ref>) under the <ce:italic>tac</ce:italic> promoter, yielding plasmid pRWT. The <ce:italic>czcS</ce:italic> gene from PT5 and PT1105 was amplified by PCR using primers S58 (5′-cg<ce:underline>gaattc</ce:underline>gcggcgtcggctacgtcc) and S59 (5′-cg<ce:underline>ggatcc</ce:underline>tgcggcgagtaccggctgtggc) containing an EcoRI and a HindIII site, respectively.                   <ce:para id="para140" view="all">
                     <ce:italic>Characterization of the CzcCBA Heavy Metal Efflux Pump from PAO1—</ce:italic>An efflux pump of the RND transporter family, called CzrCBA, was recently shown to produce tolerance to cadmium and zinc in the <ce:italic>P. aeruginosa</ce:italic> strain CMG103 isolated from a metal-polluted river in Pakistan (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref580" refid="bib15">15</ce:cross-ref>). A highly similar efflux pump, annotated as Cz<ce:underline>c</ce:underline>CBA,<ce:cross-ref id="cecref590" refid="FN4">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">3</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:footnote id="FN4">
                        <ce:label>3</ce:label>
                        <ce:note-para id="cenpara110" view="all">See <ce:inter-ref id="interrefs30" xlink:href="http://www.pseudomonas.com" xlink:type="simple">www.pseudomonas.com</ce:inter-ref>.</ce:note-para>
                     </ce:footnote> is present on the completely sequenced genome of the <ce:italic>P. aeruginosa</ce:italic> reference strain PAO1 (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref600" refid="bib26">26</ce:cross-ref>). Sequence alignment between the <ce:italic>czrCBA</ce:italic> genes from strain CMG103 (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref610" refid="bib15">15</ce:cross-ref>) and the <ce:italic>czcCBA</ce:italic> genes in the PAO1 genome (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref620" refid="bib26">26</ce:cross-ref>) revealed a &gt;99% amino acid identity between the CzrA and CzcA efflux pump proteins. However, sequence variations were present in the N termini of the outer membrane proteins CzrC (CMG103) and CzcC (PAO1). Furthermore the membrane fusion protein CzrB from strain CMG103 lacks a stretch of 31 amino acids present in CzcB from PAO1. This 31-amino acid stretch is present in the CzcB protein from <ce:italic>R. metallidurans</ce:italic> and is 70% identical to that of PAO1 (data not shown). To analyze the contribution of the CzcCBA system to heavy metal resistance, we inactivated by homologous recombination the efflux pump gene, annotated as <ce:italic>czcA</ce:italic> (PA2520) in our PAO1 strain called PT5 (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref630" refid="tbl1">Table I</ce:cross-ref>). The resulting mutant, termed PT1173, was indeed more susceptible than its parent to zinc and cadmium as well as to cobalt (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref640" refid="tbl2">Table II</ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:float-anchor refid="tbl2"/>). A similar increase in susceptibility to these three heavy metals was observed in strain CMG103-13 containing a transposon insertion in the <ce:italic>czrCBA</ce:italic> region of strain CMG103 (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref650" refid="bib15">15</ce:cross-ref>). With respect to the hypersusceptibility of the <ce:italic>czcA</ce:italic> deletion mutant PT1173 to zinc, cadmium, and cobalt, we decided to comply with the current annotation of the PAO1 genome<ce:cross-ref id="cecref660" refid="FN4">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">3</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref> and adopted the designation Cz<ce:underline>c</ce:underline>CBA for this heavy metal efflux pump.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="para150" view="all">To further characterize the CzcCBA pump, we tested its inducibility by heavy metals. Without induction, the PT5 wild type strain reached a MTC of 15, 6, and 2–3 m<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps> for zinc, cadmium, and cobalt, respectively (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref670" refid="tbl2">Table II</ce:cross-ref>). After preculturing PT5 in the presence of zinc (1 m<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps>) or cadmium (0.4 m<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps>) the MTCs increased to 30, 8, and 3 m<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps> for zinc, cadmium, and cobalt, respectively (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref680" refid="tbl2">Table II</ce:cross-ref>). MTCs of copper and nickel were not affected. A killing rate experiment showed that uninduced PT5 cells were killed after 3 h in the presence of 5 m<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps> ZnCl<ce:inf loc="post">2</ce:inf>, while about 50% of cells, preincubated in the presence of 1 m<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps> ZnCl<ce:inf loc="post">2</ce:inf>, stayed viable after 5 h of incubation (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref690" refid="fig1">Fig. 1</ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:float-anchor refid="fig1"/>). Therefore, the induced cells are strongly protected by the CzcCBA efflux pump against a challenge with lethal heavy metal concentrations.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="para160" view="all">
                     <ce:italic>Selection of Mutants Resistant to Either Zinc or Imipenem—</ce:italic>We attempted to select stable, heavy metal-resistant mutants from our wild type strain. We therefore exposed PT5 to elevated zinc concentrations (20–25 m<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps>). Sixteen independent clones displayed resistance to zinc, cadmium, and cobalt with MTCs comparable to those of the induced PT5 strain (data not shown). This resistance phenotype was stable even after several passages in the absence of zinc. Since the CzcCBA pump belongs to the RND transporter family that also includes all the Mex multidrug efflux pumps, we tested the susceptibilities of the 16 zinc-selected mutants to various antibiotics (see “Experimental Procedures”). Surprisingly all the tested mutants displayed increased imipenem resistance, while susceptibility to the other antibiotics was unaffected (data not shown). One zinc- and imipenem-resistant mutant, called PT1108, was selected for further experiments (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref700" refid="tbl2">Table II</ce:cross-ref>).</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="para170" view="all">To investigate a possible link between heavy metal and carbapenem resistance, the reciprocal experiment was performed by exposing strain PT5 to imipenem. Spontaneous mutants appearing after 24 h in the inhibition zone around the imipenem disk were picked, and their resistance profiles were analyzed. Two groups of mutants were identified. Members of the first group were resistant only to imipenem (24 of 30 mutants), while those of the second group were resistant to both heavy metals (zinc, cadmium, and cobalt) and imipenem (6 of 30 mutants). One mutant from the first group (PT1102) and one from the second (PT1105) were selected for subsequent experiments. All imipenem-resistant mutants showed resistance levels between those of the wild type and the <ce:italic>oprD</ce:italic> knock-out strain PT364 (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref710" refid="tbl2">Table II</ce:cross-ref>). <ce:underline>Z</ce:underline>inc- and <ce:underline>i</ce:underline>mipenem-<ce:underline>r</ce:underline>esistant mutants, like PT1105 and PT1108, were termed <ce:italic>zir</ce:italic>. The fact that mutants selected independently on either zinc or imipenem may display the same resistance profile suggested the existence of a common regulatory mechanism in <ce:italic>P. aeruginosa.</ce:italic>
                  </ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="para180" view="all">
                     <ce:italic>Expression of OprD in Zinc-induced Strains and in Heavy Metal-resistant Mutants—</ce:italic>In <ce:italic>P. aeruginosa</ce:italic> carbapenem resistance is known to result mainly from mutations affecting expression of the OprD porin (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref720" refid="bib30">30</ce:cross-ref>) involved in the facilitated diffusion of basic amino acids (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref730" refid="bib31">31</ce:cross-ref>) and carbapenem antibiotics (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref740" refid="bib32">32</ce:cross-ref>). We therefore investigated OprD expression in the wild type strain PT5 in the presence of zinc and subsequently in the mutants obtained under zinc or imipenem selection. Western blot analysis revealed that in the wild type strain OprD expression strongly decreased at 3 μ<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps> ZnCl<ce:inf loc="post">2</ce:inf> and was undetectable at 10 μ<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps> ZnCl<ce:inf loc="post">2</ce:inf> (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref750" refid="fig2">Fig. 2<ce:italic>A</ce:italic>
                     </ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:float-anchor refid="fig2"/>). Expression of the major outer membrane protein OprF was not affected. The amount of <ce:italic>oprD</ce:italic> mRNA was determined by quantitative real time PCR (qRT-PCR). In the presence of 1 μ<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps> ZnCl<ce:inf loc="post">2</ce:inf>, <ce:italic>oprD</ce:italic> mRNA levels dropped to 60% and decreased to 20% when 10 μ<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps> ZnCl<ce:inf loc="post">2</ce:inf> was added. These results demonstrate that ZnCl<ce:inf loc="post">2</ce:inf> has a negative effect on <ce:italic>oprD</ce:italic> transcription in a dose-dependent manner, resulting in carbapenem resistance in <ce:italic>P. aeruginosa</ce:italic>. However, heavy metal resistance is not caused by OprD down-regulation <ce:italic>per se</ce:italic> since PT364, an <ce:italic>oprD</ce:italic> knock-out mutant, displayed the same heavy metal susceptibility as its parent strain PT5 (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref760" refid="tbl2">Table II</ce:cross-ref>).</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="para190" view="all">We further investigated by Western blot analysis the amount of OprD in the three strains obtained by either zinc or imipenem selection. A strongly reduced but still detectable amount of OprD was observed in all three strains (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref770" refid="fig2">Fig. 2<ce:italic>B</ce:italic>
                     </ce:cross-ref>). These results were confirmed by <ce:italic>oprD</ce:italic> mRNA transcript analysis using qRT-PCR. The level of <ce:italic>oprD</ce:italic> mRNA in strains PT1102, PT1105, and PT1108 dropped, respectively, to 30, 2, and 24% of those of the wild type. From these data, we conclude that imipenem resistance in the three mutant strains can be directly attributed to decreased <ce:italic>oprD</ce:italic> expression.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="para200" view="all">
                     <ce:italic>Involvement of CzcCBA Efflux Pump in Heavy Metal Resistance—</ce:italic>We investigated the possible involvement of the CzcCBA efflux system in the heavy metal resistance by qRT-PCR. When wild type strain PT5 was grown in the presence of 5 m<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps> zinc, the amount of <ce:italic>czcC</ce:italic> mRNA was increased 215-fold (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref780" refid="tbl3">Table III</ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:float-anchor refid="tbl3"/>). Thus, zinc is able to strongly induce the <ce:italic>czcCBA</ce:italic> efflux system. Cadmium was less effective than zinc as an inducer, while, in contrast to <ce:italic>R. metallidurans</ce:italic> (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref790" refid="bib33">33</ce:cross-ref>), cobalt and nickel had no measurable effect on transcription of <ce:italic>czcC</ce:italic> (data not shown). Interestingly zinc not only induced transcription of the <ce:italic>czcCBA</ce:italic> structural genes but also induced transcription of the two-component regulator genes <ce:italic>czcR</ce:italic> (20-fold) and <ce:italic>czcS</ce:italic> (4.5-fold), suggesting an autoamplification mechanism for the pump gene expression. As expected, <ce:italic>czcC, czcR</ce:italic>, and <ce:italic>czcS</ce:italic> expressions in mutant PT1102, which is resistant to imipenem only, were comparable to those in the wild type (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref800" refid="tbl3">Table III</ce:cross-ref>). In contrast, the <ce:italic>zir</ce:italic> mutants PT1105 and PT1108 showed strongly increased amounts of <ce:italic>czcC, czcR</ce:italic>, and <ce:italic>czcS</ce:italic> mRNAs in the absence of zinc. The expression levels for <ce:italic>czcC</ce:italic> in the mutants were comparable to those resulting from zinc induction in the wild type (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref810" refid="tbl3">Table III</ce:cross-ref>). Hence overexpression of <ce:italic>czcCBA</ce:italic> upon zinc induction in the wild type and in the <ce:italic>zir</ce:italic> mutants seems to result in both cases from increased expression of the two-component <ce:italic>czcR-czcS</ce:italic> regulator genes.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="para210" view="all">
                     <ce:italic>CzcS Protein Is Altered in zir Mutants—</ce:italic>The similar phenotype of mutants obtained from either zinc or imipenem selection prompted us to examine the regulatory region of the <ce:italic>czcCBA</ce:italic> operon. We thus sequenced, in the two independently obtained <ce:italic>zir</ce:italic> mutants PT1105 and PT1108, the <ce:italic>czcR</ce:italic> and <ce:italic>czcS</ce:italic> genes as well as the intergenic region between <ce:italic>czcR</ce:italic> and <ce:italic>czcC</ce:italic>, which should encode the promoter of these two genes. A unique <ce:bold>G</ce:bold>TG to <ce:bold>T</ce:bold>TG transversion was detected in the <ce:italic>czcS</ce:italic> gene from both strains, resulting in a V194L substitution in the sensor protein. This mutation was also found in six other independently obtained <ce:italic>zir</ce:italic> mutants. Furthermore sequencing of the <ce:italic>czcS</ce:italic> gene in the environmental derivative BdB4-3, obtained upon zinc exposure of BdB4 (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref820" refid="tbl2">Table II</ce:cross-ref>), revealed a G<ce:bold>G</ce:bold>C to G<ce:bold>A</ce:bold>C transition, resulting in a G197D substitution in the CzcS kinase protein. Analysis of the CzcS protein with the transmembrane prediction program TMHMM-2.0<ce:cross-ref id="cecref830" refid="FN5">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">4</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref> suggested the presence of two membrane-spanning segments (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref840" refid="fig3">Fig. 3</ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:float-anchor refid="fig3"/>). According to this program, residues Val-194 and Gly-197 are located just outside the second transmembrane segment on the cytosolic side.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="para220" view="all">To verify that the V194L mutation in the CzcS sensor was responsible for the observed <ce:italic>zir</ce:italic> phenotype, we cloned the mutated <ce:italic>czcS</ce:italic> gene as well as the wild type <ce:italic>czcS</ce:italic> and <ce:italic>czcR</ce:italic> genes under the control of the inducible <ce:italic>tac</ce:italic> promoter in plasmid pMMB66EH. When the mutated CzcS protein was overexpressed in the wild type strain PT5, the level of OprD decreased significantly, while overexpression of the wild type CzcS protein had no effect (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref850" refid="fig4">Fig. 4</ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:float-anchor refid="fig4"/>). In parallel, an increase in heavy metal resistance occurred upon expression of the CzcS protein carrying the V194L mutation, while expression of the normal CzcS protein had no influence (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref860" refid="tbl4">Table IV</ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:float-anchor refid="tbl4"/>). MTCs of nickel were not affected. In agreement with decreased OprD expression, resistance to imipenem increased when CzcS(V194L) was expressed in the wild type strain, leading to a decrease of the inhibition zone from 26 to 19 mm (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref870" refid="tbl4">Table IV</ce:cross-ref>). Heavy metal and imipenem resistance levels obtained upon plasmid-directed expression of the mutated CzcS protein were lower than those in the mutant strains (PT1105 and PT1108), a fact that can be explained by competition with the wild type CzcS protein encoded on the chromosome. These results clearly demonstrate that the resistance to both heavy metals and imipenem in the selected mutants is due to the V194L mutation in CzcS (<ce:italic>zirS</ce:italic> mutants).</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="para230" view="all">
                     <ce:italic>Effect of CzcR Overexpression—</ce:italic>Since <ce:italic>czcR</ce:italic> transcription was strongly increased in the <ce:italic>zirS</ce:italic> mutants and upon zinc induction in the wild type strain, we also cloned the wild type <ce:italic>czcR</ce:italic> gene in vector pMMB66EH. When overexpressed in strain PT5, CzcR reduced OprD expression (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref880" refid="fig4">Fig. 4</ce:cross-ref>) and rendered cells resistant to imipenem (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref890" refid="tbl4">Table IV</ce:cross-ref>). Surprisingly CzcR overexpression did not result in increased resistance to zinc, cadmium, or cobalt but instead rendered bacteria more susceptible to these heavy metals (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref900" refid="tbl3">Table III</ce:cross-ref>). Thus, overexpression of wild type CzcR <ce:italic>per se</ce:italic> allows repression of OprD but is not sufficient to cause increased expression of the CzcCBA efflux pump.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="para240" view="all">
                     <ce:italic>Imipenem Resistance Induced by Zinc Release from Urinary Catheters—</ce:italic>Recently Conejo <ce:italic>et al.</ce:italic> (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref910" refid="bib20">20</ce:cross-ref>) showed that zinc eluted from siliconized latex urinary catheters decreases OprD expression in <ce:italic>P. aeruginosa</ce:italic> causing carbapenem resistance. The authors carried out their experiments using segments of siliconized latex urinary catheters incubated in Mueller-Hinton broth (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref920" refid="bib34">34</ce:cross-ref>). To determine whether zinc was also released from latex urinary catheters (LUCs) into urine, we grew strain PT5 for 48 h at 37 °C in LUCs containing filter-sterilized urine. As determined by atomic absorption spectroscopy, zinc reached a concentration of about 1 m<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps> in urine (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref930" refid="tbl5">Table V</ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:float-anchor refid="tbl5"/>). This concentration was sufficient to induce wild type cells to resist a subsequent challenge by 20 m<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps> zinc (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref940" refid="tbl5">Table V</ce:cross-ref>). This tolerance was accompanied by a 33-fold increased expression of the CzcCBA efflux system as determined by qRT-PCR (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref950" refid="tbl5">Table V</ce:cross-ref>). As expected, zinc released from LUCs decreased OprD expression (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref960" refid="tbl5">Table V</ce:cross-ref>) causing carbapenem resistance, suggesting that zinc-induced imipenem resistance might persist as long as zinc is released from the LUCs. Using these growth conditions for 6 days at 37 °C, we did not detect appearance of any stable mutants resistant to both imipenem and zinc (98 clones tested, data not shown).</ce:para>
               </ce:section>
               <ce:section id="cesec30" view="all">
                  <ce:section-title id="cestitle30">DISCUSSION</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:para id="para250" view="all">In the present study, we report on the characterization of the CzcCBA efflux pump from the <ce:italic>P. aeruginosa</ce:italic> reference strain PT5. We demonstrate the role played by the CzcS-CzcR two-component system in the control of <ce:italic>czcCBA</ce:italic> expression as well as its surprising involvement in the expression of the OprD porin leading to carbapenem resistance. To our knowledge this is the first report on a co-regulation mechanism between heavy metal and antibiotic resistance.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="para260" view="all">Our results clearly show that the CzcCBA efflux system in PT5 confers resistance to zinc, cadmium, and cobalt. The reductions in MTCs of zinc, cadmium, and cobalt in the <ce:italic>czcA</ce:italic> deletion mutant were rather modest compared with the more than 10-fold differences in MTCs conferred by the CzcCBA system in <ce:italic>R. metallidurans</ce:italic> (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref970" refid="bib35">35</ce:cross-ref>). It cannot be excluded that other transporters present in PT5 may contribute to the intrinsic resistance of this organism to heavy metals. Overexpression of the heavy metal pumps could be obtained upon exposure to zinc and cadmium (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref980" refid="bib36">36</ce:cross-ref>) or by constitutive expression resulting, as we show here, from mutations in the two-component sensor CzcS. Zinc in the growth medium not only induced the <ce:italic>czcCBA</ce:italic> efflux pump operon but at the same time transcription of the two-component regulator genes <ce:italic>czcR</ce:italic> and <ce:italic>czcS</ce:italic>. This positive autoregulation loop could allow the cells to respond rapidly to the presence of small amounts of heavy metals in the environment.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="para270" view="all">Analysis of mutants constitutively expressing the CzcCBA pump surprisingly revealed that all of them were also resistant to carbapenem antibiotics. This resulted from decreased expression of the porin OprD mediating facilitated diffusion of this class of antibiotics. However, OprD is not directly involved in heavy metal resistance since an <ce:italic>oprD</ce:italic> knock-out mutant was as susceptible as the parent to heavy metals. Hence OprD decrease is a secondary effect linked to CzcCBA overexpression. While only 20% of the analyzed strains selected on imipenem were co-resistant to heavy metals, it nevertheless strongly suggests the existence of a common regulatory mechanism that connects <ce:italic>czcCBA</ce:italic> to <ce:italic>oprD</ce:italic> expression. This hypothesis is supported by the identification of two mutational events found in the same region of the CzcS sensor kinase in zinc-selected derivatives from PT5 and from an environmental isolate. Moreover the V194L mutation was selected in PT5 either upon zinc or imipenem exposure (<ce:italic>zirS</ce:italic> mutants). Therefore, the sensor kinase CzcS via its cognate regulator CzcR is involved in the regulation of both heavy metal and antibiotic resistance. How could the mutations in CzcS lead to constitutive expression of the CzcCBA efflux pump and reduced expression of OprD? According to sequence analysis, CzcS, like EnvZ in <ce:italic>Escherichia coli</ce:italic>, belongs to class I histidine kinases. In this class the catalytic and ATP-binding domain is separated in two regions (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref990" refid="bib37">37</ce:cross-ref>). The A domain is involved in the dimerization, phosphotransfer, and phosphatase activity, while the B domain binds ATP (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1000" refid="bib38">38</ce:cross-ref>, <ce:cross-ref id="cecref1010" refid="bib39">39</ce:cross-ref>). These histidine kinases transfer a phosphoryl group on an aspartate residue in the cognate regulator protein (OmpR in the case of EnvZ). The activated regulator protein then triggers expression of specific target genes. Many histidine kinases also possess phosphatase activities that allow dephosphorylation and hence inactivation of the regulator protein (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1020" refid="bib37">37</ce:cross-ref>). In the case of EnvZ/OmpR, the level of phosphorylation of OmpR is important for the tight control of osmoregulation (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1030" refid="bib40">40</ce:cross-ref>). Mutations in EnvZ that specifically affect either kinase or phosphatase activities were localized in separate structural domains (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1040" refid="bib41">41</ce:cross-ref>). This bifunctional enzymatic activity of EnvZ is further regulated by a linker region containing two helices (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1050" refid="bib42">42</ce:cross-ref>). Upon signal input, the interaction between these two helices is modified, thereby altering the relative position of domains A and B and hence the ratio of kinase/phosphatase activity of the catalytic and ATP-binding domain (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1060" refid="bib42">42</ce:cross-ref>). The mutated Val-194 and Gly-197 residues in CzcS are located in this linker region that connects the putative catalytic and ATP-binding domain to the second transmembrane segment (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1070" refid="fig4">Fig. 4</ce:cross-ref>). The V194L and the G197D mutations could therefore cause a conformational change in CzcS that is normally induced by the signal (heavy metals), causing CzcS to be permanently autophosphorylated. Alternatively the change in conformation could decrease the phosphatase activity of CzcS, thereby preventing or reducing dephosphorylation of CzcR. Since overexpression of wild type CzcR only reduced OprD expression without increasing heavy metal resistance, it can be concluded that at least the unphosphorylated form of CzcR negatively regulates OprD, while the amount of active phosphorylated CzcR is critical for triggering <ce:italic>czcCBA</ce:italic> transcription.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="para280" view="all">Simultaneous overexpression of an efflux pump and down-regulation of a porin pathway seems to be an efficient mechanism to prevent intracellular accumulation of toxic molecules. This type of co-regulation has been described in <ce:italic>nfxC</ce:italic> type mutants of <ce:italic>P. aeruginosa</ce:italic> (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1080" refid="bib43">43</ce:cross-ref>). These mutants overexpress the multidrug efflux pump MexEF-OprN (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1090" refid="bib44">44</ce:cross-ref>), conferring resistance to quinolones, chloramphenicol, and trimethoprim, and are resistant to carbapenems due to decreased OprD expression (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1100" refid="bib44">44</ce:cross-ref>, <ce:cross-ref id="cecref1110" refid="bib45">45</ce:cross-ref>). Interestingly this resistance phenotype can be obtained in the susceptible wild type PT5 upon plasmid-mediated overexpression of MexT, the transcriptional activator of the MexEF-OprN efflux pump (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1120" refid="bib46">46</ce:cross-ref>, <ce:cross-ref id="cecref1130" refid="bib47">47</ce:cross-ref>). MexT overexpression was shown to decrease both transcriptional (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1140" refid="bib46">46</ce:cross-ref>, <ce:cross-ref id="cecref1150" refid="bib47">47</ce:cross-ref>) and post-transcriptional expression of <ce:italic>oprD</ce:italic> (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1160" refid="bib46">46</ce:cross-ref>). Hence both CzcR and MexT are able to repress OprD expression, which could occur either directly or via another regulator (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1170" refid="fig5">Fig. 5</ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:float-anchor refid="fig5"/>). Since in PT5 as well as in other PAO1 strains the <ce:italic>mexT</ce:italic> gene is inactivated by insertion of a stretch of 8 bp, resulting in a frameshift and premature stop of translation (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1180" refid="bib48">48</ce:cross-ref>), one can exclude CzcR-mediated repression of OprD via MexT.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="para290" view="all">Another example of this type of co-regulation has been demonstrated in <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> where the <ce:italic>micF</ce:italic> regulatory RNA (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1190" refid="bib49">49</ce:cross-ref>) simultaneously decreases expression of the porin OmpF and increases expression of the multidrug efflux pump AcrAB (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1200" refid="bib50">50</ce:cross-ref>). Expression of <ce:italic>micF</ce:italic> in <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> is controlled by environmental and internal stress signals (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1210" refid="bib51">51</ce:cross-ref>). The presence of a <ce:italic>micF</ce:italic> homologue in <ce:italic>P. aeruginosa</ce:italic> was already suggested by Southern hybridization with the <ce:italic>E. coli micF</ce:italic> gene (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1220" refid="bib52">52</ce:cross-ref>). Exposure to heavy metals or antibiotics could therefore be considered as external stress signals and trigger a <ce:italic>micF</ce:italic>-like response in <ce:italic>P. aeruginosa</ce:italic>.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="para300" view="all">Co-regulation between heavy metal and antibiotic resistance might have important diagnostic and clinical implications. It was known for a long time that MICs of carbapenems for <ce:italic>P. aeruginosa</ce:italic> are increased in the presence of trace amounts of zinc in the medium (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1230" refid="bib53">53</ce:cross-ref>, <ce:cross-ref id="cecref1240" refid="bib54">54</ce:cross-ref>). While direct inactivation of imipenem by zinc compounds was hypothesized (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1250" refid="bib55">55</ce:cross-ref>), it seems clear from our results and those of Conejo <ce:italic>et al.</ce:italic> (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1260" refid="bib20">20</ce:cross-ref>) that zinc-mediated repression of OprD is mainly responsible for imipenem resistance. More importantly, zinc, which is used as a catalyst during production of LUCs (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1270" refid="bib56">56</ce:cross-ref>), is released into growth media (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1280" refid="bib19">19</ce:cross-ref>), and as we show here also into urine. The amount of zinc released from LUCs into urine (1.1 m<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps>) is higher than the zinc concentration released into Mueller-Hinton medium (0.56 m<ce:small-caps>m</ce:small-caps>) (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1290" refid="bib20">20</ce:cross-ref>). These concentrations are sufficient to induce imipenem resistance in a clinical isolate (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref1300" refid="bib34">34</ce:cross-ref>) and in the wild type strain PT5. Whether this transient increase in carbapenem resistance might compromise treatment of urinary tract infections by <ce:italic>P. aeruginosa</ce:italic> remains to be determined. While no stable, constitutively resistant mutants to zinc and imipenem could be obtained after 6 days of growth in LUCs, one cannot exclude that such mutants might appear at low frequency <ce:italic>in vivo</ce:italic>. Interestingly polyvinylchloride urinary catheters, which were used for comparison, released undetectable amounts of zinc (data not shown). Therefore, the choice of catheter used should take into account an eventual colonization by <ce:italic>P. aeruginosa</ce:italic> and subsequent infection.</ce:para>
