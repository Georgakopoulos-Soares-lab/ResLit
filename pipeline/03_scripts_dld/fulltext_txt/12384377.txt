Abstract 

Clinical isolate Escherichia coli BM4511 was resistant to broad-spectrum penicillins in the presence or in the absence of β-lactamase inhibitors but remained susceptible to cephalosporins. Resistance was due to production of a new TEM-type β-lactamase, designated TEM-103/IRT-28, characterized by the Arg 275 Leu substitution and encoded by the ca. 62-kb pIP845 conjugative plasmid of the IncI1 incompatibility group. 

Among the mechanisms responsible for resistance of Escherichia coli to β-lactam-β-lactamase inhibitor combinations, hyperproduction of TEM-1 penicillinase and alteration in the outer membrane proteins limiting the entry of the drugs were reported first ( 16 , 19 ). Inhibitor-resistant TEM (IRT) β-lactamases have been isolated since the beginning of the 1990s and are mostly produced by strains of E. coli but also by clinical isolates of Klebsiella spp. and Proteus mirabilis ( 2 ). These enzymes confer resistance to penicillins and their combinations with β-lactamase inhibitors ( 3 , 6 , 13 ). IRT variants are derivatives of TEM β-lactamases, with mutations at various amino acid positions (69, 130, 244, 275, and 276, according to Ambler's numbering [ 1 ]) that have been shown, or postulated, to play a role in determining resistance to inhibitors. These substitutions decrease the affinity for β-lactams and alter the way in which the enzymes interact with suicide inactivators ( 9 ). We report the genetic characterization of a new TEM variant, TEM-103/IRT-28, in a clinical isolate of E. coli resistant to amoxicillin-clavulanate and susceptible to cephalosporins. 

E. coli BM4511 was isolated from the surgery wound of an 82-year-old woman suffering from colon cancer and hospitalized in Hospital de Basurto, Bilbao, Spain. The MICs of various β-lactams, alone or in combination with β-lactamase inhibitors used at fixed concentrations (clavulanic acid, 2 μg/ml; tazobactam, 4 μg/ml; sulbactam, 8 μg/ml), were determined by the microdilution method in Mueller-Hinton broth (Difco) with an inoculum of approximately 5 × 10 4 cells/well. BM4511 was resistant to penicillins (MICs ≥ 1,024 μg/ml) but susceptible to cephalosporins (MICs ≤ 4 μg/ml). Clavulanic acid was unable to restore susceptibility of the strain to penicillins, but tazobactam reduced significantly the piperacillin MIC (Table 1 ). These data were compatible with the production of an IRT β-lactamase. The presence of a bla TEM gene in BM4511 was revealed by PCR amplification (data not shown) using primers TEM-C and TEM-H ( 12 ). To determine the pI of the enzyme, crude sonic bacterial extracts were applied to Phast-gels with different pH gradients in a Phast System (Pharmacia) and compared with TEM-30/IRT-2 (pI 5.2), TEM-1 (pI 5.4), and TEM-2 (pI 5.6). The strain was shown to produce a β-lactamase with an isoelectric point of 5.2 (data not shown). All IRT-type β-lactamases reported so far, except for TEM-59/IRT17 (pI 5.6), have a pI of either 5.2 or 5.4.

Resistance to amoxicillin-clavulanate was transferred by conjugation from BM4511 to E. coli JM83 at an approximate frequency of 10 −7 per donor. Analysis of the donor and of a transconjugant after digestion with Eco RI revealed the presence of a single plasmid, pIP845, with a size of approximately 62 kb. By reciprocal conjugation with E. coli BM21 ( 5 ) containing plasmids belonging to various incompatibility groups, pIP845 was found to be of incompatibility group I1; the size of these plasmids ranges between 55 and 80 kb (A. Labigne, personal communication). 

Fragments of pIP845 DNA generated by double digestion with Eco RI and Bam HI were inserted into pBGS18+ ( 17 ), and the resulting recombinant plasmids were introduced into E. coli TOP10 (Invitrogen) with selection on ampicillin at 100 μg/ml. The smallest plasmid able to confer resistance to amoxicillin-clavulanate to the new host, pAT779, had a 1.3-kb insert. Direct sequencing of the resistance gene, designated bla TEM-103 , using an automated sequencer (CEQ 2000 DNA analysis system; Beckman Coulter) revealed that it was identical to bla TEM1-B except for a single G-to-T change at position 1020 (according to Sutcliffe's numbering [ 18 ]) (Table 2 ), which leads to the amino acid substitution of Arg to Leu at position 275 (according to Ambler's numbering [ 1 ]). The change of arginine, a basic positively charged amino acid, to leucine, an uncharged amino acid, accounts for the decrease in pI from 5.4 to 5.2 ( 9 ). Substitutions that lead to an inhibitor-resistant phenotype occur at a few specific sites within the TEM enzymes: Met-69, Arg-244, Arg-275, and Asn-276 ( 14 ). To the best of our knowledge, the Arg 275 Leu substitution has not yet been observed alone in TEM-type β-lactamases, and the enzyme was designated TEM-103/IRT-28 according to the TEM and IRT nomenclature (G. Jacoby and K. Bush, Amino acid sequences for TEM, SHV, and OXA extended-spectrum β-lactamases [ http://www.lahey.org/studies/webt.html ; last accessed 10 July 2002]).

The Arg 275 Leu substitution has been detected in TEM-38/IRT-9 associated with Met 69 Val ( 9 ) and in TEM-68 associated with Gly 238 Ser, Glu 240 Lys, and Thr 265 Met ( 7 ). Another mutation at this position, Arg 275 Gln, was found in TEM-45/IRT-14 ( 4 ), TEM-82/IRT-24 ( 11 ), and TEM-83/IRT-25 ( 11 ) enzymes associated with Met 69 Leu, Met 69 Val, and Met 69 Ile substitutions, respectively; TEM-83 has, in addition, a Trp 165 Cys substitution. These β-lactamases, except for TEM-68, are enzymes with strong inhibitor-resistant activity because the Arg275 substitution is associated with substitutions at position 69 known to confer resistance to inhibitors ( 10 ). Therefore, the contribution of the Arg275 mutation to the IRT phenotype was not known. 

Argnine 275 is located at the beginning of the C terminus of the α-11 helix, and its side chain is close to the guanidinium group of Arg244. A kinetic study of the Arg 275 Leu variant has shown the involvement of this change in resistance of the TEM β-lactamases to inactivation by clavulanic acid ( 15 ). This could be related to electrostatic interactions with Arg244 and/or to a possible displacement of the water molecule involved in the inactivation process of clavulanic acid ( 6 ). Variant TEM-68 ( 7 ) combines mutations responsible for the enlargement of the substrate profile (Gly 238 Ser, Glu 240 Lys, and Thr 265 Met) with a change (Arg 275 Leu) conferring resistance to inhibitors. Fiett et al. ( 7 ) attributed the inhibitor-resistant activity of TEM-68 to the Arg 275 Leu substitution. 

Functional characterization of this new inhibitor-resistant β-lactamase should provide further insight into the understanding of the heterogeneity of IRT-type enzymes and into structure-activity relationships of the expanding family of TEM proteins.
