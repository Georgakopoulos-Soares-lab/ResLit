         <body view="all">
            <ce:sections>
               <ce:para id="para10" view="all">Class D OXA-51-like carbapenemases are chromosomally encoded and appear to be intrinsic to <ce:italic>Acinetobacter baumannii</ce:italic> [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref30" refid="bib1">1</ce:cross-ref>]. These enzymes are weak carbapenemases, and it has been suggested that they only confer carbapenem resistance if an additional promoter is provided by the insertion of IS<ce:italic>Aba1</ce:italic> upstream of the structural gene [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref40" refid="bib2">2</ce:cross-ref>]. Following recent reports in <ce:italic>CMI</ce:italic> of new variants of the OXA-51-like subgroup of class D carbapenemases [<ce:cross-refs id="cecrefs10" refid="bib3 bib4">3,4</ce:cross-refs>], we wish to report the identification of 11 more novel <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-51</ce:inf>-like variants in clinical isolates of <ce:italic>A. baumannii.</ce:italic>
               </ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para20" view="all">A collection of 60 clinical <ce:italic>A. baumannii</ce:italic> isolates of diverse worldwide origin was screened for <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-51</ce:inf>-like genes using primers OXA-69A and OXA-69B, which amplify a 975-bp product containing the entire <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-51</ce:inf>-like coding sequence [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref50" refid="bib5">5</ce:cross-ref>]. Amplification products were sequenced on a 3730 DNA Analyzer (Applied Biosystems, Warrington, UK) and were compared using BLAST and Multalin software. Isolates producing a band of <ce:italic>c.</ce:italic> 2000 bp, as the result of an insertion upstream of the <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-51</ce:inf>-like gene, were re-analysed using primers preABprom+ [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref60" refid="bib6">6</ce:cross-ref>] and OXA-69B to produce a 1189-bp product, which was then sequenced as above.</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para30" view="all">Sequence comparison revealed 11 novel <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-51</ce:inf>-like sequences, encoding 11 novel enzymes, designated OXA-79, OXA-80, OXA-82, OXA-104 and OXA-106–OXA-112 (<ce:inter-ref id="interrefs10" xlink:href="http://www.lahey.org/studies/webt.asp" xlink:type="simple">http://www.lahey.org/studies/webt.asp</ce:inter-ref>). The origins of the isolates encoding these enzymes are listed in <ce:cross-ref id="cecref70" refid="tbl1">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>
                  <ce:float-anchor refid="tbl1"/>. These novel enzymes differ from all currently known enzymes by between one and four amino-acids (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref80" refid="tbl1">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>), sharing 99% amino-acid identity. The <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-79</ce:inf>, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-80</ce:inf>, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-106</ce:inf>, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-107</ce:inf>, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-109</ce:inf>, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-110</ce:inf> and <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-112</ce:inf> genes each differ from the gene encoding the closest related enzyme by a single nucleotide, resulting in a single amino-acid change. The nucleotide sequence of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-82</ce:inf> differs from <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-66</ce:inf> by two nucleotides, one of which is silent (T<ce:inf loc="post">435</ce:inf> → A), and <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-111</ce:inf> has three silent substitutions compared to <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-51</ce:inf> (C<ce:inf loc="post">90</ce:inf> → T, T<ce:inf loc="post">216</ce:inf> → A and T<ce:inf loc="post">507</ce:inf> → C). The sequences of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-104</ce:inf> and <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-108</ce:inf> differ from that of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-95</ce:inf> by 11 and five nucleotides, respectively, eight (C<ce:inf loc="post">54</ce:inf> → T, C<ce:inf loc="post">369</ce:inf> → T, A<ce:inf loc="post">390</ce:inf> → G, G<ce:inf loc="post">426</ce:inf> → A, C<ce:inf loc="post">549</ce:inf> → T, C<ce:inf loc="post">610</ce:inf> → T, G<ce:inf loc="post">720</ce:inf> → A and G<ce:inf loc="post">732</ce:inf> → A) and one (C<ce:inf loc="post">328</ce:inf> → T) of which are silent. All amino-acid changes were located outside the class D carbapenemase motifs [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref90" refid="bib7">7</ce:cross-ref>]. All isolates producing the larger <ce:italic>c.</ce:italic> 2000-bp product with primers OXA-69A and OXA-69B also yielded a 1189-bp product with the second set of primers, indicating that these isolates had an IS<ce:italic>Aba1</ce:italic> insertion upstream of the <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-51</ce:inf>-like gene. Sequencing confirmed that the <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-79</ce:inf>, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-80</ce:inf>, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-82</ce:inf> (in both isolates), <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-107</ce:inf> (in all isolates), <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-108</ce:inf>, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-109</ce:inf> and <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-110</ce:inf> (in both isolates) genes each had an IS<ce:italic>Aba1</ce:italic> insertion 7 bp upstream of the structural gene. A detailed analysis of the genotypic relationships among these isolates and the influence of the IS<ce:italic>Aba1</ce:italic> insertions on the expression of carbapenem resistance will be published separately.</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para40" view="all">Since the discovery of OXA-51 in 2004, the number of related enzymes in this group has now increased to at least 39, and they appear to be ubiquitous in <ce:italic>A. baumannii</ce:italic> [<ce:cross-refs id="cecrefs20" refid="bib3 bib5 bib7 bib8">3,5,7,8</ce:cross-refs>]. While the insertion of IS<ce:italic>Aba1</ce:italic> upstream of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-51</ce:inf>-like genes has been associated with resistance to carbapenems, not all isolates contain such an insertion [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref100" refid="bib2">2</ce:cross-ref>]. OXA-51 and OXA-69 have been shown to hydrolyse carbapenems poorly [<ce:cross-refs id="cecrefs30" refid="bib5 bib7">5,7</ce:cross-refs>], and it would be of interest to determine whether the sequence diversity of this group is a result of selection for greater activity against the carbapenems.</ce:para>
