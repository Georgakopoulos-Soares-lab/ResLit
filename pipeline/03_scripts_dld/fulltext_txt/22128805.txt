                  Carbapenem resistance is increasingly being reported among Acinetobacter species, and results mostly from the expression of acquired carbapenem-hydrolysing oxacillinases (CHDLs). Several Acinetobacter species intrinsically possess chromosomal CHDL genes: Acinetobacter baumannii (blaOX
                     
                     A-51), Acinetobacter radioresistens (blaOX
                     
                     A-23), and Acinetobacter Iwoffii (bla
                     OXA-134) We aimed to identify the progenitors of novel CHDL-encoding genes for identification of potential reservoirs. We performed PCR screening using degenerated internal primers designed from a sequence alignment of the known CHDLs (OXA-23, OXA-40, OXA-51, OXA-58, OXA-134, and OXA-143) applied to a collection of 50 Acinetobacter strains belonging to 23 different species. Two strains of Acinetobacter johnsonii, one strain of Acinetobacter calcoaceticus and two strains of Acinetobacter haemolyticus were found to harbour, respectively, the totally novel bla
                     OXA-211-like, bla
                     OXA-213-like and bla
                     OXA-214-like genes. In addition, the complete genomes of those three species available in GenBank, i.e. one A. johnsonii genome, four A. calcoaceticus genomes, and one A. haemolyticus genome, were analysed and found to be positive for the presence of bla
                     OXA11 -like, bla
                     OXA-213-like and bla
                     OXA-214-like genes, respectively. The β-lactamases OXA-211, OXA-213 and OXA-214 are diverse, with amino acid identities ranging from 53% to 76%, as compared with the naturally occurring OXA-51-like CHDL from A. baumannii. These β-lactamases showed a peculiar hydrolysis profile, including mostly penicillins and carbapenems. Regarding bla
                     OXA-23 in A. radioresistens and bla
                     OXA-134 in A. Iwoffii, these genes were not expressed (or expressed at a non-significant level) in their host. Detection of these β-lactamase genes might be used as a useful tool for accurate identification of these Acinetobacter species.

            <ce:abstract id="ceab10" view="all" class="author"><ce:abstract-sec id="ceabs10" view="all">
                  <ce:simple-para id="spara30" view="all">Carbapenem resistance is increasingly being reported among <ce:italic>Acinetobacter</ce:italic> species, and results mostly from the expression of acquired carbapenem-hydrolysing oxacillinases (CHDLs). Several <ce:italic>Acinetobacter</ce:italic> species intrinsically possess chromosomal CHDL genes: <ce:italic>Acinetobacter baumannii</ce:italic> (<ce:italic>bla<ce:inf loc="post">OX</ce:inf>
                     </ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">A-51</ce:inf>), <ce:italic>Acinetobacter radioresistens</ce:italic> (<ce:italic>bla<ce:inf loc="post">OX</ce:inf>
                     </ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">A-23</ce:inf>), and <ce:italic>Acinetobacter Iwoffii</ce:italic> (<ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-134</ce:inf>) We aimed to identify the progenitors of novel CHDL-encoding genes for identification of potential reservoirs. We performed PCR screening using degenerated internal primers designed from a sequence alignment of the known CHDLs (OXA-23, OXA-40, OXA-51, OXA-58, OXA-134, and OXA-143) applied to a collection of 50 <ce:italic>Acinetobacter</ce:italic> strains belonging to 23 different species. Two strains of <ce:italic>Acinetobacter johnsonii,</ce:italic> one strain of <ce:italic>Acinetobacter calcoaceticus</ce:italic> and two strains of <ce:italic>Acinetobacter haemolyticus</ce:italic> were found to harbour, respectively, the totally novel <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-211</ce:inf>-like, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-213</ce:inf>-like and <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-214</ce:inf>-like genes. In addition, the complete genomes of those three species available in GenBank, i.e. one A. <ce:italic>johnsonii</ce:italic> genome, four <ce:italic>A. calcoaceticus</ce:italic> genomes, and one <ce:italic>A. haemolyticus</ce:italic> genome, were analysed and found to be positive for the presence of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">OXA11</ce:inf> -like, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-213</ce:inf>-like and <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-214</ce:inf>-like genes, respectively. The β-lactamases OXA-211, OXA-213 and OXA-214 are diverse, with amino acid identities ranging from 53% to 76%, as compared with the naturally occurring OXA-51-like CHDL from <ce:italic>A. baumannii.</ce:italic> These β-lactamases showed a peculiar hydrolysis profile, including mostly penicillins and carbapenems. Regarding <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-23</ce:inf> in <ce:italic>A. radioresistens</ce:italic> and <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-134</ce:inf> in <ce:italic>A. Iwoffii,</ce:italic> these genes were not expressed (or expressed at a non-significant level) in their host. Detection of these β-lactamase genes might be used as a useful tool for accurate identification of these <ce:italic>Acinetobacter</ce:italic> species.</ce:simple-para>

                  <ce:section-title id="cestitle30">Introduction</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:para id="para10" view="all">Although <ce:italic>Acinetobacter baumannii</ce:italic> is considered to be the most clinically relevant <ce:italic>Acinetobacter</ce:italic> species, there are increasing reports of community and nosocomial infections caused by non<ce:italic>-baumannii Acinetobacter</ce:italic> species [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref110" refid="bib1">1</ce:cross-ref>]. This is probably a consequence of the development of genetic tools for identification of the different <ce:italic>Acinetobacter</ce:italic> species. <ce:italic>Acinetobacter haemolyticus</ce:italic> was found to be a causative agent of endocarditis [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref120" refid="bib2">2</ce:cross-ref>] and bloody diarrhoea mediated by Shiga toxin production [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref130" refid="bib3">3</ce:cross-ref>]. <ce:italic>Acinetobacter johnsonii</ce:italic> was found to be associated with catheter-related bloodstream infections [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref140" refid="bib4">4</ce:cross-ref>]. As there is a close relationship between <ce:italic>A. baumannii</ce:italic> and <ce:italic>Acinetobacter calcoaceticus,</ce:italic> the term <ce:italic>A. baumannii-A. calcoaceticus</ce:italic> complex, or Abc complex, has been used, and the exact clinical relevance of <ce:italic>A. calcoaceticus</ce:italic> therefore remains unclear [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref150" refid="bib5">5</ce:cross-ref>]. Although antimicrobial susceptibility in all other <ce:italic>Acinetobacter</ce:italic> species is usually much higher than in <ce:italic>A. baumannii,</ce:italic> antimicrobial resistance in non<ce:italic>-baumannii Acinetobacter</ce:italic> species raises concern, as multi-drug-resistant isolates, including carbapenem-resistant isolates, are being reported worldwide [<ce:cross-refs id="cecrefs10" refid="bib6 bib7">6,7</ce:cross-refs>]. Furthermore, some <ce:italic>Acinetobacter</ce:italic> species have been shown to intrinsically possess chromosomal genes encoding carbapenem-hydrolysing oxacillinases (CHDLs). Thus, A. <ce:italic>baumannii</ce:italic> carries <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-51</ce:inf>-like, <ce:italic>Acinetobacter radioresistens bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-23</ce:inf>-like and <ce:italic>Acinetobacter Iwoffii bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-134</ce:inf>-like genes [<ce:cross-refs id="cecrefs20" refid="bib8 bib9 bib10">8–10</ce:cross-refs>]. Characterization of these naturally occurring CHDL-encoding genes seems important for two reasons. First, these genes may spread into other <ce:italic>Acinetobacter</ce:italic> species, such as <ce:italic>A. baumannii,</ce:italic> causing outbreaks of carbapenem-resistant strains, as has been reported with <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-23</ce:inf>-carrying <ce:italic>A. baumannii</ce:italic> isolates [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref160" refid="bib11">11</ce:cross-ref>]. Second, detection of these β-lactamase genes might be used as an additional tool for rapid identification of <ce:italic>Acinetobacter</ce:italic> species.</ce:para>
               </ce:section>

                     <ce:para id="para30" view="all">The antimicrobial agents and their sources have been described previously [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref210" refid="bib14">14</ce:cross-ref>]. Antibiotic-containing disks were used for detection of antibiotic susceptibility, with Mueller-Hinton agar plates (Sanofi Diagnostics Pasteur, Marnes-La-Coquette, France) and a disk diffusion assay (<ce:inter-ref id="interrefs10" xlink:href="http://www.sfm.fr" xlink:type="simple">http://www.sfm.fr</ce:inter-ref>).                   <ce:section id="cesec50" view="all">
                     <ce:section-title id="cestitle70">PCR amplification and cloning experiments</ce:section-title>
                     <ce:para id="para40" view="all">Whole cell DNA of <ce:italic>Acinetobacter</ce:italic> species was extracted as described previously [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref240" refid="bib10">10</ce:cross-ref>]. PCRs were performed in standard conditions with Taq DNA polymerase (Applied Biosystems, Courtaboeuf, France), as described previously [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref250" refid="bib10">10</ce:cross-ref>]. Screening for CHDL-encoding genes was performed by PCR with the degenerate internal primers OXA-CHDL A and OXA-CHDL B (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref260" refid="tbl1">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>
                        <ce:float-anchor refid="tbl1"/>). Degenerate internal primers were designed in the corresponding nucleotide regions of conserved protein sequences after complete OXA-23, OXA-40, OXA-58, OXA-51, OXA-134 and OXA-143 protein sequence alignment. Complete available genome sequences of <ce:italic>A. haemolyticus</ce:italic> ATCC 19194 (GenBank accession number NZ_ADMT00000000.1), <ce:italic>A. johnsonii</ce:italic> SH046 (Genbank accession number ACPL01000000) and <ce:italic>A. calcoaceticus</ce:italic> RUH2202 (GenBank accession number ZP_06056651.1) were screened in order to determine genomic sequences flanking the internal fragments of the novel genes temporarily named <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-H</ce:inf>, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-J</ce:inf>, and <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-C</ce:inf>, respectively. External primers specific for each gene were designed and used for PCR, allowing amplification of the entire sequences of these genes (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref270" refid="tbl1">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>). OXA-calcoaceticus A-B, OXA-haemolyticus A-B and OXA-johnsonii A-B (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref280" refid="tbl1">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>) were used to amplify fragments containing the entire oxacillinase-encoding gene, with whole cell DNA from <ce:italic>A. calcoaceticus, A. haemolyticus</ce:italic> and <ce:italic>A. johnsonii</ce:italic> as the template.</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="para50" view="all">The obtained PCR fragment was purified with a QIAquick column (Qiagen, Courtaboeuf, France) and cloned into the pTOPO vector. Recombinant plasmids were selected on trypticase soy agar plates containing amoxycillin (25 mg/mL) and kanamycin (30 mg/mL). The cloned DNA fragments inserted into one of the recombinant plasmids were sequenced each time from two clones on both strands with an Applied Biosystems sequencer (ABI 377).</ce:para>

                  <ce:section id="cesec100" view="all">
                     <ce:section-title id="cestitle120">Sequencing of CHDL-encoding genes</ce:section-title>
                     <ce:para id="para90" view="all">Sequencing of the amplicons obtained from <ce:italic>A. haemolyticus, A. johnsonii</ce:italic> and <ce:italic>A. calcoaceticus</ce:italic> identified three genes encoding novel OXA-type β-lactamases. OXA-H was named OXA-214, and shared 53% amino acid identity with OXA-23, 55% with OXA-51, 53% with OXA-40, and 48% with OXA-58. OXA-J was named OXA-211, and shared 55% amino acid identity with OXA-23, 58% with OXA-51, 56% with OXA-40, and 52% with OXA-58. OXA-C was named OXA-213, and shared 57% amino acid identity with OXA-23, 76% with OXA-51, 62% with OXA-40, and 46% with OXA-58.</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="para100" view="all">OXA-211, OXA-213 and OXA-214 possessed the typical features of class D β-lactamases (DBLs), including the STFK tetrad at positions 70–73 according to DBL numbering (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref300" refid="fig1">Fig. 1</ce:cross-ref>
                        <ce:float-anchor refid="fig1"/>) [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref310" refid="bib16">16</ce:cross-ref>]. Also, in OXA-213 and OXA-214, an FGN motif at DBL positions 144–146 replaced the usual YGN motif of classical DBLs, as observed for other CHDLs except for OXA-58, OXA-51, and OXA-211 [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref320" refid="bib17">17</ce:cross-ref>]. Finally, a KSG element was identified at DBL positions 216–218, as observed in the CHDLs OXA-40 and OXA-51, whereas a KTG motif is present in most DBLs, including the CHDL OXA-23 [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref330" refid="bib17">17</ce:cross-ref>]. A phylogenetic analysis showed that OXA-211, OXA-213 and OXA-214 constituted separate subgroups of CHDLs, but that these subgroups were more closely related to the identified DBLs from <ce:italic>Acinetobacter</ce:italic> species than to other known CHDLs (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref340" refid="fig2">Fig. 2</ce:cross-ref>
                        <ce:float-anchor refid="fig2"/>).</ce:para>

                  <ce:section id="cesec130" view="all">
                     <ce:section-title id="cestitle150">Biochemical properties of OXA-21 I, OXA-213, OXA-214</ce:section-title>
                     <ce:para id="para150" view="all">To further characterize the activity of OXA-211, OXA-213 and OXA-214, the enzymes were purified from £ <ce:italic>coli</ce:italic> cultures containing recombinant plasmid expressing these CHDLs, as described previously [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref370" refid="bib17">17</ce:cross-ref>]. OXA-211, OXA-213 and OXA-214 showed a peculiar hydrolysis profile. These enzymes were able to hydrolyse penicillins and carbapenems. However, as observed for most of the CHDLs, broad-spectrum cephalosporins and monobactams were spared [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref380" refid="bib20">20</ce:cross-ref>]. The rates of carbapenem hydrolysis were similar to those of OXA-23 and OXA-51 (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref390" refid="tbl3">Table 3</ce:cross-ref>
                        <ce:float-anchor refid="tbl3"/>).</ce:para>
                  </ce:section>
                  <ce:section id="cesec140" view="all">
                     <ce:section-title id="cestitle160">Genetic context of OXA-211, OXA-213 and OXA-214 CHDL-encoding genes</ce:section-title>
                     <ce:para id="para160" view="all">
                        <ce:italic>In silico</ce:italic> analysis of whole genome sequences of <ce:italic>A. johnsonii, A. haemolyticus</ce:italic> and <ce:italic>A. calcoaceticus</ce:italic> led to the identification of genes surrounding these CHDL-encoding genes. These genes were chromosomally located. Upstream of the <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-211</ce:inf> gene from <ce:italic>A. johnsonii,</ce:italic> the <ce:italic>dnaK</ce:italic> gene was identified, encoding a molecular chaperone that modulates the heat shock response in £ <ce:italic>coli.</ce:italic> Downstream of the <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-21</ce:inf> gene, there was a hypothetical open reading frame followed by a matrixin super-family protein-encoding gene. Upstream of the <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-213</ce:inf> gene from <ce:italic>A. calcoaceticus,</ce:italic> a gene encoding a putative phage protein was identified, whereas a sortase family protein-encoding gene was identified downstream. Upstream of the <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-214</ce:inf> gene from <ce:italic>A. haemolyticus,</ce:italic> a gene encoding an outer membrane protein was identified, and a GGDEF family protein-encoding gene was identified downstream. This latter configuration has already been observed for the <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">OxA-23</ce:inf> gene from <ce:italic>A. radioresistens,</ce:italic> which was identified upstream of an AAA ATPase family protein-encoding gene [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref400" refid="bib10">10</ce:cross-ref>].</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="para170" view="all">In conclusion, this study identified naturally occurring CHDL-encoding genes in <ce:italic>A. haemolyticus, A. johnsonii,</ce:italic> and <ce:italic>A. calcoaceticus.</ce:italic> None of them corresponded to the acquired CHDL-encoding genes reported in <ce:italic>A. baumannii</ce:italic> or in any other Gram-negative rods. As these genes remained highly specific to these species, detection of these genes might be used as an additional tool for rapid identification of these <ce:italic>Acinetobacter</ce:italic> species. Finally, this work further indicates that <ce:italic>Acinetobacter</ce:italic> species are the reservoirs of peculiar β-lactamase genes, actually CHDL genes, whose distribution seems to be restricted to Gram-negative rods <ce:italic>(Pseudomonas, Aeromonas,</ce:italic> etc.). Their natural function remains to be identified.</ce:para>
