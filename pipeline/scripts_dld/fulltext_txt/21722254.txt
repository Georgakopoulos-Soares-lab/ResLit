                     Klebsiella pneumoniae Kp1 carrying a novel complex class 1 integron was isolated from an inanimate surface of a female ward sanitary facility in the Hospital Infante D.             <ce:abstract id="ceab10" view="all" class="author"><ce:abstract-sec id="ceabs10" view="all">
                  <ce:simple-para id="spara30" view="all">
                     <ce:italic>Klebsiella pneumoniae</ce:italic> Kp1 carrying a novel complex class 1 integron was isolated from an inanimate surface of a female ward sanitary facility in the Hospital Infante D. Pedro, Aveiro, central Portugal. The integron consists of two variable regions (VRs); VR1 was previously described in <ce:italic>Escherichia coli</ce:italic> and <ce:italic>Vibrio cholerae</ce:italic>, and VR2 contains an ***ln<ce:italic>37</ce:italic>-like structure and is located downstream of an IS<ce:italic>CR1</ce:italic> element. The integron was found on a plasmid of 225 kb. The <ce:italic>qnrB10</ce:italic> gene, although present, is not associated with the complex class 1 integron.</ce:simple-para>
               </ce:abstract-sec></ce:abstract>

            <ce:sections>
               <ce:para id="para10" view="all">Klebsiellae are widespread in the environment and in the intestinal flora of humans and other mammals. <ce:italic>Klebsiella pneumoniae</ce:italic> is an important opportunistic pathogen causing urinary tract and intra-abdominal infections, neonatal meningitis and pneumonia in immunocompromised patients [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref160" refid="bib1">
                     <ce:sup loc="post">1</ce:sup>
                  </ce:cross-ref>].</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para20" view="all">The intensive use of broad-spectrum antibiotics in hospitalized patients led to the development of multidrug-resistant (MDR) strains. In <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic>, resistance is associated with the production of <ce:italic>β</ce:italic>-lactamase enzymes, mainly extended-spectrum <ce:italic>β</ce:italic>-lactamases [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref170" refid="bib2">
                     <ce:sup loc="post">2</ce:sup>
                  </ce:cross-ref>]. However, more recently, metallo-<ce:italic>β</ce:italic>-lactamases have also been described [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref180" refid="bib3">
                     <ce:sup loc="post">3</ce:sup>
                  </ce:cross-ref>].</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para30" view="all">
                  <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> strains carrying complex class 1 integrons are becoming more common. Usually, within the second variable region (VR2), only one or two genes conferring resistance to <ce:italic>β</ce:italic>-lactamases, fluoroquinolones, aminoglycosides, chloramphenicol or trimethoprim are found [<ce:cross-refs id="ceref10" refid="bib4 bib5 bib6 bib7">
                     <ce:sup loc="post">4–7</ce:sup>
                  </ce:cross-refs>]. Thus, the use of antibiotics that are commonly used to treat infections caused by <ce:italic>Klebsiella</ce:italic> MDR strains, such as cephalosporins, <ce:italic>β</ce:italic>-lactamase-stable penicillins and aminoglycosides, can be compromised [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref190" refid="bib8">
                     <ce:sup loc="post">8</ce:sup>
                  </ce:cross-ref>].</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para40" view="all">Herein, we report and characterize a novel complex class 1 integron found in a <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> isolate collected from an inanimate surface within the Hospital Infante D. Pedro, Aveiro, Central Portugal.</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para50" view="all">
                  <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> Kp1 was isolated from the sink of a female ward sanitary facility during an infection control study in 2005. The strain was identified with the automatic VITEK 2 Advanced Expert System (BioMérieux, Marcy L'étoile, France). The identification was confirmed by 16S rDNA (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref200" refid="tbl1">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>
                  <ce:float-anchor refid="tbl1"/>) nucleotide sequence determination. Antibiotic susceptibilities were determined according to CLSI guidelines [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref210" refid="bib9">
                     <ce:sup loc="post">9</ce:sup>
                  </ce:cross-ref>]. In the Kp1 isolate, an unusual class 1 integron structure was detected by PCR, with the specific primers described in <ce:cross-ref id="cecref220" refid="tbl1">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>, and is described herein.</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para60" view="all">The first variable region (VR1) was approximately 2500 bp in length. By primer walking (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref230" refid="tbl1">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>), the complete array of gene cassettes was determined. Nucleotide sequence analysis confirmed an integron structure that was previously found and described in <ce:italic>Escherichia coli</ce:italic> [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref240" refid="bib10">
                     <ce:sup loc="post">10</ce:sup>
                  </ce:cross-ref>] and in a <ce:italic>Vibrio cholerae</ce:italic> isolate from China (unpublished: EU678897). So far, this structure has never been described in <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae.</ce:italic> VR1 of the Kp1 isolate comprises three genes: at the first position, close to the <ce:italic>intI1</ce:italic> gene, is a rifampin ADP-ribosyl transferaseencoding gene (<ce:italic>arr-3</ce:italic>), and this is followed by a dihydrofolate reductase gene (<ce:italic>dfrA27</ce:italic>), and an <ce:italic>aadA16</ce:italic> gene, encoding an aminoglycoside-(3′′)(9)-adenylyltransferase. An IS<ce:italic>CR1</ce:italic> element linked to the class 1 integron was also amplified. Specific primers for IS<ce:italic>CR1</ce:italic> and <ce:italic>qacE</ce:italic>Δ1 (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref250" refid="tbl1">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>) were employed to access the genes that compose VR2, resulting in an amplicon of approximately 6000 bp. Further nucleotide sequence determination and analysis revealed the presence of an array consisting of four gene cassettes, constituting the variable region of In<ce:italic>37</ce:italic> (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref260" refid="fig1">Fig. 1</ce:cross-ref>
                  <ce:float-anchor refid="fig1"/>a).</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para70" view="all">In<ce:italic>37</ce:italic> was previously described in a <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> clinical isolate from Argentina and in an <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> isolate from China [<ce:cross-refs id="cecrefs20" refid="bib6 bib11">
                     <ce:sup loc="post">6,11</ce:sup>
                  </ce:cross-refs>]. This integron appears to be associated with IS<ce:italic>CR1</ce:italic> and <ce:italic>qnr</ce:italic> genes (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref270" refid="fig1">Fig. 1</ce:cross-ref>a). It is noteworthy that the cassette array of In<ce:italic>37</ce:italic> is always found associated with VR1, and never in VR2.</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para80" view="all">Herein, we report, for the first time, the association of In<ce:italic>37</ce:italic> with VR2 in this isolate, highlighting the fact that here it is not associated with the <ce:italic>intI1</ce:italic> gene. The location of the resistance genes corresponding to the In<ce:italic>37</ce:italic> array may be explained by a homologous recombination event, according to the model proposed by Toleman <ce:italic>et al.</ce:italic> [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref280" refid="bib7">
                     <ce:sup loc="post">7</ce:sup>
                  </ce:cross-ref>], which suggests that the IS<ce:italic>CR1</ce:italic> element is responsible for the transposition of those genes, leading to the formation of a complex class 1 integron.</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para90" view="all">The genes found in this complex structure may explain the resistance profile observed for trimethoprim–sulphamethoxazole (MIC ≥320 mg/L), tobramycin (MIC ≥16 mg/L), amoxycillin–clavulanic acid (MIC ≥32 mg/L), cloramphenicol (MIC ≥32 mg/L) and cephalotin and cefuroxime (MIC ≥64 mg/L). Moreover, the presence of the <ce:italic>qnrB10</ce:italic> gene may be responsible for the reduced susceptibility to ciprofloxacin (MIC ≥1 mg/L) and levofloxacin (MIC ≥0.5 mg/L). The <ce:italic>qnrB10</ce:italic> gene has been found to be linked to the IS<ce:italic>CR1</ce:italic> element in different class 1 integrons [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref290" refid="bib6">
                     <ce:sup loc="post">6</ce:sup>
                  </ce:cross-ref>] (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref300" refid="fig1">Fig. 1</ce:cross-ref>b), but in the Kp1 isolate, and with the primers described (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref310" refid="tbl1">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>), this association was not detected.</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para100" view="all">The location of the integron was investigated by pulsed-field gel electrophoresis. Genomic DNA digested with S1 (New England Biolabs, Beverly, MA, USA) was separated in a CHEF-DRIII system (Bio-Rad, Hemel Hempstead, UK), and hybridized to an <ce:italic>intI1</ce:italic> probe labelled with digoxigenin (Roche Diagnostics GmbH, Penzberg, Germany). The results revealed that the complex class 1 integron is located on a plasmid of approximately 225 kb (data not shown). Plasmid identification was attempted with the primers described by Carattoli <ce:italic>et al.</ce:italic> [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref320" refid="bib12">
                     <ce:sup loc="post">12</ce:sup>
                  </ce:cross-ref>]. Nevertheless, in the Kp1 isolate, no amplification was observed with any of the primers used, suggesting that no plasmid-specific sequences representative of the major plasmid incompatibility groups circulating among <ce:italic>Enterobacteriaceae</ce:italic> are present. As plasmids are the primary vehicles for horizontal resistance gene transfer among Gram-negative bacteria [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref330" refid="bib13">
                     <ce:sup loc="post">13</ce:sup>
                  </ce:cross-ref>], in order to evaluate their transfer capability, conjugation assays were performed with <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> J53<ce:sup loc="post">AzR</ce:sup> as the recipient strain, as described by Wang <ce:italic>et al.</ce:italic> [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref340" refid="bib14">
                     <ce:sup loc="post">14</ce:sup>
                  </ce:cross-ref>]. <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> transconjugants were selected in Mueller–Hinton agar with 200 mg/L sodium azide and 16 and 32 mg/L chloramphenicol. Under these laboratory conditions, no transconjugants were obtained. As the Kp1 isolate was collected from an inanimate surface, and also given the fact that the antibiotic resistance genes are contained in an integron-borne plasmid, this strain may be a possible reservoir of antibiotic resistance genes within the hospital environment. Given its resistance profile, this isolate could pose some risk for debilitated or immunocompromised patients.</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para110" view="all">This study describes the presence of a novel complex class 1 integron. Additionally, it highlights the fact that surveillance studies are of the utmost importance to provide the information needed for the implementation of appropriate control measures for dissemination of MDR microorganisms within these highly selective environments, as pathogenic bacteria can colonize and survive outside a host and therefore find their way into debilitated patients.</ce:para>
