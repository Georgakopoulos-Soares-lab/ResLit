                  Resistance to β-lactam/β-lactamase inhibitors in enterobacteria is a growing problem that has not been intensively studied in Argentina.
                  In the present work, 54/843 enterobacteria collected in a teaching hospital of Buenos Aires city were ampicillin-sulbactam-resistant isolates remaining susceptible to second- and third-generation cephalosporins. The enzymatic mechanisms present in the isolates, which were also amoxicillin-clavulanic acid (AMC)-resistant (18/54) were herein analyzed.
                  Sequencing revealed two different variants of bla
                     TEM-1, being bla
                     TEM-1b the most frequently detected allelle (10 Escherichia coli, 3 Klebsiella pneumoniae, 2 Proteus mirabilis and 1 Raoultella terrigena) followed by bla
                     TEM-1a (1 K. pneumoniae). Amoxicillin-clavulanate resistance seems to be mainly associated with TEM-1 overproduction (mostly in E. coli) or co-expressed with OXA-2-like and/or SHV β-lactamases (K. pneumoniae and P. mirabilis).
                  A new bla
                     TEM variant (TEM-163) was described in an E. coli strain having an AMC MIC value of 16/8μg/ml. TEM-163 contains Arg275Gln and His289Leu amino acid substitutions. On the basis of the high specific activity and low IC50 for clavulanic acid observed, the resistance pattern seems to be due to overproduction of the new variant of broad spectrum β-lactamase rather than to an inhibitor-resistant TEM (IRT)-like behavior.

                  <ce:section-title id="sect0025">Introduction</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:para id="par0005" view="all">Amoxicillin-clavulanate (AMC) is one of the most frequently prescribed antibiotic combinations in many countries, especially in ambulatory patients. For this reason, even if it is still a low occurrence event, resistance to β-lactam/β-lactamase inhibitors among enterobacteria clinical isolates is an emerging worldwide problem. Different enzymatic mechanisms are associated with AMC resistance: hyperproduction of chromosome-encoded class C β-lactamases, acquired plasmid-encoded cephalosporinases (AmpC type), hyperproduction of plasmid-mediated class A β-lactamases (such as TEM-1 and SHV-1 enzymes), production of class D oxacillinases and inhibitor-resistant TEM (IRTs or CMTs) and SHV mutants<ce:cross-ref id="crf0095" refid="bib0030">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">6</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:sup loc="post">,</ce:sup>
                     <ce:cross-ref id="crf0100" refid="bib0070">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">14</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:sup loc="post">,</ce:sup>
                     <ce:cross-ref id="crf0105" refid="bib0095">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">19</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:sup loc="post">,</ce:sup>
                     <ce:cross-ref id="crf0110" refid="bib0115">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">23</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref> (whose β-lactamase activities are poorly inhibited by clavulanate). In addition to enzymatic mechanisms, a decrease in the expression or absence of outer membrane proteins might also be involved, i.e., a deficiency in the OmpF and/or OmpC production has been associated to this phenotype in <ce:italic>Escherichia coli</ce:italic>
                     <ce:cross-ref id="crf0115" refid="bib0020">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">4</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0010" view="all">IRTs were frequently described as plasmid-encoded enzymes derived from TEM-1 or TEM-2 β-lactamases and detected mainly from European isolates. Substitutions of one or more amino-acid residues at positions 69, 244, 275 and 276 render structural changes in β-lactamases that affect affinity for inhibitors, but produce only slight modifications in the isoelectric point (pI) or activity on other β-lactam compounds. Other substitutions also found in IRT β-lactamases do not seem to be involved in the IRT phenotype<ce:cross-ref id="crf0120" refid="bib0045">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">9</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0015" view="all">Overproduction of both TEM-1 and SHV-1, or production of an IRT may increase resistance to amoxycillin, ticarcillin, amoxicillin-clavulanic acid and, frequently to piperacillin and ticarcillin-clavulanic acid, while only slightly affecting susceptibility to narrow spectrum cephalosporins, cephamycins, extended-spectrum cephalosporins, and, in most cases, to piperacillin-tazobactam<ce:cross-ref id="crf0125" refid="bib0015">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">3</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0020" view="all">Over the last years, an increase in the rate of resistance to AMC has been noted among <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> isolates in our country. According to the “Sistema Informático de Resistencia (SIR)” susceptibility to ampicillin-sulbactam in <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> has been decreasing from 62%<ce:cross-ref id="crf0130" refid="bib0110">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">22</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref> (years 2004–2005) to 50% (years 2006–2008; M. Radice, personal communication). In spite of the high use of this association, there is little information available about the prevalence of AMC resistance mechanisms in <ce:italic>Enterobacteriaceae</ce:italic>.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0025" view="all">The aim of this study was to investigate the enzymatic mechanisms of amoxicillin-clavulanate-resistant enterobacteria lacking inducible chromosomal <ce:italic>ampC</ce:italic> genes isolated from a teaching hospital of Buenos Aires city.</ce:para>
               </ce:section>

                  <ce:section-title id="sect0030">Materials and methods</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:section id="sec0015" view="all">
                     <ce:section-title id="sect0035">Microorganisms</ce:section-title>
                     <ce:para id="par0030" view="all">Isolates recovered from different clinical specimens that fulfilled a screening criteria (<ce:italic>Enterobacteriaceae</ce:italic> resistant to ampicillin-sulbactam but remaining susceptible to cefoxitin, ceftazidime and cefotaxime by a disk diffusion method) were studied. Isolates were collected within a 5-month period (May to September 2008) from patients attending the Hospital de Clínicas that belongs to UBA (University of<ce:anchor id="p222"/> Buenos Aires) “José de San Martín”, Buenos Aires, Argentina.</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="par0035" view="all">
                        <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> ATCC 35218, TEM-1 basal level producer, was used as reference.</ce:para>
                  </ce:section>
                  <ce:section id="sec0020" view="all">
                     <ce:section-title id="sect0040">Susceptibility testing</ce:section-title>
                     <ce:para id="par0040" view="all">Antimicrobial susceptibility testing was performed using the disk diffusion method and the agar dilution method as described by the Clinical Laboratory Standard Institute (CLSI). Results were interpreted according to the CLSI guidelines<ce:cross-ref id="crf0135" refid="bib0010">
                           <ce:sup loc="post">2</ce:sup>
                        </ce:cross-ref>. Tested antibiotics were ampicillin (AMP), ampicillin-sulbactam (AMS), Amoxicillin-clavulanic acid (AMC), piperacillin (PIP), piperacillin/tazobactam (PTZ), cephalothin (CTN), cefoxitin (FOX), ceftriaxone (CRO), ceftazidime (CAZ), cefepime (FEP), imipenem (IMP), meropenem (MER), ertapenem (ERT), amikacin (AMI), gentamicin (GEN), polymyxin B (POL), trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMS), ciprofloxacin (CIP), nitrofurantoin (NIT). AMS and AMC MICs were tested at a 2:1 fixed ratio. Antimicrobial agents tested and reported were obtained from Britania S.A. (disks) and Klonal laboratorios (drugs), Argentina.</ce:para>
                  </ce:section>
                  <ce:section id="sec0025" view="all">
                     <ce:section-title id="sect0045">β-lactamase assays</ce:section-title>
                     <ce:para id="par0045" view="all">Cells were harvested by centrifugation from overnight LB broth cultures and resuspended in 0.5 ml of sodium phosphate buffer (0.1 M, pH 7). β-lactamases were released by sonication (ten 1-min high-frequency discontinuous bursts on ice at output 4-5 and duty cycle: 50%) using SonicVibra Cell ultrasonic processor (Sonics &amp; Materials Inc., USA).</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="par0050" view="all">Isoelectric focusing was performed with crude extracts applied to polyacrylamide gels (pH gradient 3-10) in an LKB 2117-Multiphor II isoelectric focusing tank (LKB Produkter AB, Sweden), as previously described<ce:cross-ref id="crf0140" refid="bib0075">
                           <ce:sup loc="post">15</ce:sup>
                        </ce:cross-ref>. β-Lactamase activity was revealed by the agar iodometric system using AMP (500 mg/l) as substrate, as previously described<ce:cross-ref id="crf0145" refid="bib0125">
                           <ce:sup loc="post">25</ce:sup>
                        </ce:cross-ref>. TEM-1 (pI 5.4), SHV-2 (pI 7.6), OXA-2 (pI 7.7) and CTX-M-2 (pI 8.2) producing strains were used as standards and were run simultaneously with the samples.</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="par0055" view="all">Semi-quantification of β-lactamase production was determined as described below: total protein concentration in crude enzyme preparations was determined by the Bradford method (Bio-Rad Laboratories, USA), adjusted at the same protein concentration and diluted with 100<ce:hsp sp="0.25"/>mM sodium phosphate buffer (pH 7.0). β-Lactamase activity of each enzymatic extract and decimal dilutions (20<ce:hsp sp="0.25"/>μl) were evaluated using the iodometric method employing AMP as substrate. Clinical isolates that showed β-lactamase activity in dilutions higher than the reference strain were considered enzyme overproducers.</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="par0060" view="all">Specific activity was determined in those isolates harboring β-lactamases that focused at pI 5.4. This activity was measured three times per strain by the spectrophotometric assay using nitrocefin (482<ce:hsp sp="0.25"/>nm) as substrate at a final concentration of 100<ce:hsp sp="0.25"/>μM. TEM-1 overproduction was arbitrarily defined when specific activity was at least two-fold higher than <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> ATCC 35218.</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="par0065" view="all">Inhibition studies were performed by measuring the residual β-lactamase activity of crude extracts previously pre-incubated with clavulanic acid (0.1, 1 and 10<ce:hsp sp="0.25"/>μM) for 15<ce:hsp sp="0.25"/>min at room temperature. IC<ce:inf loc="post">50</ce:inf> was defined as the clavulanic acid concentration required to inhibit 50% of the β-lactamase activity.</ce:para>

                     <ce:section-title id="sect0075">Susceptibility testing</ce:section-title>
                     <ce:para id="par0090" view="all">Fifty-four (6.4%) out of a total of 843 documented infections were caused by different <ce:italic>Enterobacteriaceae</ce:italic> that fulfilled the proposed selection criterion. These bacteria, lacking inducible chromosomal AmpC genes, were: 38 <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic>, 8 <ce:italic>K. pneumonia</ce:italic>, 5 <ce:italic>P. mirabilis</ce:italic>, 2 <ce:italic>K. oxytoca</ce:italic> and 1 <ce:italic>Raoultella terrigena</ce:italic>, and displayed MIC values of AMS higher than 16/8<ce:hsp sp="0.25"/>μg/ml.</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="par0095" view="all">As not all AMS-resistant isolates are also AMC-resistant<ce:cross-ref id="crf0165" refid="bib0080">
                           <ce:sup loc="post">16</ce:sup>
                        </ce:cross-ref>
                        <ce:sup loc="post">,</ce:sup>
                        <ce:cross-ref id="crf0170" refid="bib0090">
                           <ce:sup loc="post">18</ce:sup>
                        </ce:cross-ref>; therefore, AMC susceptibility was first determined by the agar disk diffusion method. Taking this feature into account, 18/54 (33.3%) isolates were finally selected to characterize the β-lactamase/s involved: 7 of them were clearly AMC-resistant with inhibition zone diameters &lt;14<ce:hsp sp="0.25"/>mm (2 <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic>, 1 <ce:italic>R. terrigena</ce:italic>, 2 <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> and 2 <ce:italic>P. mirabilis</ce:italic>), 8 had intermediate susceptibility (all <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic>, with inhibition zone diameters between 14-17<ce:hsp sp="0.25"/>mm) and 3 had border line susceptibility (1 <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> and 2 <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic>, inhibition zone = 18<ce:hsp sp="0.25"/>mm).</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="par0100" view="all">Resistance profiles were further explored by MIC determination to a large set of antibiotics. Relevant results are shown in <ce:cross-ref id="crf0175" refid="tbl0010">Table 2</ce:cross-ref>
                        <ce:float-anchor refid="tbl0010"/>. All of them were sensitive to FOX but PIP and CTN susceptibility was variable. All isolates remained susceptible to PTZ, extended-spectrum cephalosporins (CRO, CAZ, FEP) and carbapenems (IMP, MER, ERT) tested (data not shown). Thus, chromosomal or plasmid AmpC hyper-producing and CMT-producing isolates were ruled out.</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="par0105" view="all">Regarding susceptibility to non β-lactam antibiotics, 9/11 <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> were resistant to TMS, 4/11 to CIP and 3/11 to NIT; 4/4 <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> were resistant to TMS and NIT, 2/4 were resistant or had intermediate susceptibility to GEN and AMK, and 1/4 was resistant to CIP; and finally 2/2 <ce:italic>P. mirabilis</ce:italic> were resistant to GEN and NIT, and 1/2 was resistant to CIP. Except for natural resistance in <ce:italic>P. mirabilis</ce:italic>, all isolates were susceptible to POL.<ce:anchor id="p224"/>
                     </ce:para>

                  <ce:section id="sec0060" view="all">
                     <ce:section-title id="sect0080">Resistance mechanisms</ce:section-title>
                     <ce:para id="par0110" view="all">All crude extracts showed β-lactamase activity when analyzed by the iodometric assay employing AMP as substrate. Characterization of the β-lactamases revealed that all extracts possessed, at least, an enzyme that focused at pI 5.4 and 7/18 co-produced other enzymes that focused at different pI (<ce:cross-ref id="crf0180" refid="tbl0015">Table 3</ce:cross-ref>
                        <ce:float-anchor refid="tbl0015"/>). Ten of eleven <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> isolates only displayed the pI 5.4 enzyme.</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="par0115" view="all">All isolates were positive for <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">TEM</ce:inf> and PCR products were sequenced subsequently. Variants of the <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">TEM-1</ce:inf> gene were detected, being <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">TEM-1b</ce:inf> described in 16 isolates. The <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">TEM-1a</ce:inf> allele was only noted in one <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> strain (<ce:cross-ref id="crf0185" refid="tbl0015">Table 3</ce:cross-ref>) and no other variants of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">TEM-1</ce:inf> gene were detected. An <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> isolate (strain 3343) produced a new variant of the <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">TEM</ce:inf> gene (named <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">TEM-163</ce:inf>), which differed from <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">TEM-1b</ce:inf> in 3 bp (see below for more details).</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="par0120" view="all">When tested for other β-lactamase genes by PCR, only one <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic>, three <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> and two <ce:italic>P. mirabilis</ce:italic> isolates were positive for <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-2</ce:inf>-group genes (<ce:cross-ref id="crf0190" refid="tbl0015">Table 3</ce:cross-ref>). The <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">SHV</ce:inf> gene was only detected in all <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> isolates. Not a single isolate rendered a positive amplification for <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-1</ce:inf> or <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-10</ce:inf> group. These results agreed with pI values previously obtained.</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="par0125" view="all">Most isolates that only harbored the <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">TEM-1</ce:inf> determinant (7 <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> and 1 <ce:italic>R. terrigena)</ce:italic> showed a higher level of TEM-1 production, and could be considered as probable TEM-1 overproducers since they showed an enzyme production 2-12-fold higher than <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> ATCC 35218 (<ce:cross-ref id="crf0195" refid="tbl0015">Table 3</ce:cross-ref>). The remaining two <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> strains, with intermediate susceptibility to AMC, displayed a basal or slightly increased level of TEM-1, suggesting that other mechanisms would be involved in the susceptibility decrease observed. Results achieved by the spectrophotometer method were coincident with those obtained by the iodometric semi-quantitative assay.</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="par0130" view="all">
                        <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> 3343, in which the new TEM-163 was present, also showed a profile compatible with TEM overproduction (with a specific activity that was 6-fold higher than <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> ATCC 35218). In this case, IC<ce:inf loc="post">50</ce:inf> values for clavulanic acid were similar to the IC<ce:inf loc="post">50</ce:inf>value measured on the reference TEM-1-producing strain (IC<ce:inf loc="post">50</ce:inf> around of 1μM). Because of these characteristics, rather than thinking of TEM-163 as a probable IRT β-lactamase, the AMC-resistance phenotype is likely due to overproduction of this new β-lactamase.</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="par0135" view="all">As expected from isolates that were mostly obtained from urine samples with no strong epidemiological link, even those producing only a TEM β-lactamase, the different <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> isolates are assumed as the different clones displayed a dissimilar banding profile by REP-PCR (data not shown).</ce:para>

               <ce:section id="sec0070" view="all">
                  <ce:section-title id="sect0090">Discussion</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:para id="par0145" view="all">We have focused the study on β-lactamases present in different <ce:italic>Enterobacteriaceae</ce:italic> with phenotypic resistance to β-lactam/β-lactamase inhibitors but remaining susceptible to cefoxitin and extended-spectrum cephalosporins. During the 5-month period of our study the overall frequency of ampicillin-sulbactam-resistant isolates (all non-inducible AmpC producing enterobacteria) remained at about 6.4%. Among them, we selected those that also showed a reduced inhibition zone to AMC by the disk diffusion test to describe the enzymatic mechanisms that are poorly known in our country. AMC resistance in <ce:italic>Enterobacteriaceae</ce:italic> is a complex phenomenon with heterogeneous clinical implications.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0150" view="all">The overproduction of TEM-like enzymes followed by TEM-1 production in combination with OXA-2-type and/or SHV-type β-lactamases were the main mechanisms identified.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0155" view="all">In agreement with literature data, genes encoding TEM-1 were detected in the majority of the isolates examined, being <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">TEM-1b</ce:inf> the predominant allelic variant (16/18) identified so far, and only one <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">TEM-1a</ce:inf> harboring strain (<ce:italic>K. pneumonia</ce:italic> 3400) was described. Consistently, other <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">TEM-1</ce:inf> variants (i.e. <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">TEM-1c</ce:inf>, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">TEM-1d</ce:inf>, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">TEM-1e</ce:inf>), rarely detected in enterobacteria<ce:cross-ref id="crf0220" refid="bib0065">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">13</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:sup loc="post">,</ce:sup>
                     <ce:cross-ref id="crf0225" refid="bib0085">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">17</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>, were not found.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0160" view="all">The presumed overproduction of TEM-1 β-lactamase in <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic>, which was present in 7/11 isolates with reduced susceptibility to AMC, was the main mechanism responsible for that resistance profile. These data are similar to those detected in <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> by Pérez-Moreno <ce:italic>et al.</ce:italic> in Spain<ce:cross-ref id="crf0230" refid="bib0100">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">20</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>, although other researchers have observed a lower proportion<ce:cross-ref id="crf0235" refid="bib0095">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">19</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:sup loc="post">,</ce:sup>
                     <ce:cross-ref id="crf0240" refid="bib0120">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">24</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0165" view="all">At least two mechanisms may lead to TEM overproduction: 1- multiple copies of the <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">TEM</ce:inf> gene due to the presence of multiple copies of a plasmid per cell have been reported to cause overproduction of β-lactamase or, 2- different promoter regions have been widely reported in various isolates. Even though <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">TEM-1a</ce:inf> and <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">TEM-1b</ce:inf> alleles were initially associated with a weak promoter<ce:cross-ref id="crf0245" refid="bib0035">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">7</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref> (called <ce:italic>P3</ce:italic>), it is known that stronger promoters could be present (i.e., <ce:italic>Pa/Pb</ce:italic>, <ce:italic>P4</ce:italic>, <ce:italic>P5</ce:italic>) upstream of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">TEM</ce:inf>-like genes, which would explain the higher levels of production of TEM enzymes<ce:cross-refs id="crfs0005" refid="bib0055 bib0060 bib0065">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">11–13</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-refs>.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0170" view="all">Two <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> isolates producing a basal or slightly increased level of TEM-1 as a single resistance mechanism showed reduced susceptibility to AMC and CTN, being the reduced permeability or increased efflux to β-lactam antibiotics the possible resistance mechanisms involved<ce:cross-ref id="crf0250" refid="bib0020">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">4</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:sup loc="post">,</ce:sup>
                     <ce:cross-ref id="crf0255" refid="bib0100">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">20</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0175" view="all">In the present study, OXA-2-type enzymes were mainly associated with <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> and <ce:italic>P. mirabilis</ce:italic>, while the OXA-1 and OXA-10 groups were not detected. OXA-2-like enzymes, known to be more poorly inhibited by clavulanic acid than class A β-lactamases, accounted for 33% (6/18) of the isolates analyzed, but when <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> determinants were compared with other studies<ce:cross-ref id="crf0260" refid="bib0065">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">13</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:sup loc="post">,</ce:sup>
                     <ce:cross-ref id="crf0265" refid="bib0095">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">19</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:sup loc="post">,</ce:sup>
                     <ce:cross-ref id="crf0270" refid="bib0100">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">20</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:sup loc="post">,</ce:sup>
                     <ce:cross-ref id="crf0275" refid="bib0120">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">24</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>, the frequency of OXA-producing <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> remained low (1/11).</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0180" view="all">Genes encoding SHV-1 and other close related enzymes have been shown to be natural in <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> chromosome<ce:cross-ref id="crf0280" refid="bib0045">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">9</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>; therefore <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">SHV</ce:inf> detection in this species is always expectable.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0185" view="all">Contribution of IRT β-lactamases to AMC resistance in <ce:italic>Enterobacteriaceae</ce:italic> appear to be different in isolates from diverse countries. In this study, IRT-like enzymes were not detected among isolates having intermediate susceptibility and also among those that were clearly resistant. To the best of our knowledge, the presence of IRT-producing isolates appears to be much less common in the American continent than in Europe<ce:cross-ref id="crf0285" refid="bib0070">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">14</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>. Nevertheless, amplification and direct sequencing of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">TEM</ce:inf> has contributed to discover a new enzymatic variant called TEM-163 present in an <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> strain having intermediate susceptibility to AMC (MIC= 16/8 mg/l). The predicted amino acid sequence of the latter contained the same substitution present in TEM-122<ce:cross-ref id="crf0290" refid="bib0050">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">10</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref> (Arg<ce:inf loc="post">275</ce:inf>Gln), and also provided an extra mutation (His<ce:inf loc="post">289</ce:inf>Leu), which has not been previously described.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0190" view="all">An <ce:italic>in silico</ce:italic> model of TEM coevolution network and its communities was constructed by Guthrie <ce:italic>et al.</ce:italic>, and the two mutant positions described in this work (275, 289) are included within the inhibitor-resistant β-lactamase community. Interestingly, position 289 is located at the interface between the two communities<ce:cross-ref id="crf0295" refid="bib0040">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">8</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref> (extended-spectrum and inhibitor resistance).</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0195" view="all">The amino acid substitution at position 275 (located at the C-terminal of the α-11 helix) was previously considered important to confer resistance to β-lactamase inhibitors. However, the same substitution (Arg<ce:inf loc="post">275</ce:inf>Gln) was involved in TEM-45 (IRT-14), TEM-82 and TEM-83 but these enzymes contained at least a second mutation in residue 69<ce:cross-ref id="crf0300" refid="bib0050">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">10</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:sup loc="post">,</ce:sup>
                     <ce:cross-ref id="crf0305" refid="bib0065">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">13</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>, which was highly associated with IRT enzymes<ce:cross-ref id="crf0310" refid="bib0015">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">3</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:sup loc="post">,</ce:sup>
                     <ce:cross-ref id="crf0315" refid="bib0045">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">9</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0200" view="all">The absence of a mutation in residue 69 on this new β-lactamase in agreement with its low IC<ce:inf loc="post">50</ce:inf> to clavulanic<ce:anchor id="p226"/> acid allow us to speculate that TEM-163 is more clearly a new variant of broad spectrum β-lactamase than an IRT enzyme (IC<ce:inf loc="post">50</ce:inf>&gt;10<ce:hsp sp="0.25"/>μM is considered a probable IRT enzyme<ce:cross-ref id="crf0320" refid="bib0100">
                        <ce:sup loc="post">20</ce:sup>
                     </ce:cross-ref>). In conclusion, amoxicillin-clavulanate resistance in different species of non-inducible AmpC <ce:italic>Enterobacteriaceae</ce:italic> isolated in a teaching hospital from Buenos Aires seems to be mainly associated with TEM-1 overproduction or TEM-1, OXA-2-like and/or SHV-like β-lactamase co-production.</ce:para>
