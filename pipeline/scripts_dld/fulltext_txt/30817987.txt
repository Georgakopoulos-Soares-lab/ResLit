                  The bacterial pathogen Klebsiella pneumoniae comprises several phylogenetic groups (Kp1 to Kp7), two of which (Kp5 and Kp7) have no taxonomic status. Here we show that group Kp5 is closely related to Klebsiella variicola (Kp3), with an average nucleotide identity (ANI) of 96.4%, and that group Kp7 has an ANI of 94.7% with Kp1 (K. pneumoniae sensu stricto). Biochemical characteristics and chromosomal beta-lactamase genes also distinguish groups Kp5 and Kp7 from other Klebsiella taxa. We propose the names Klebsiella africanensis for Kp7 (type strain, 200023T = CIP 111653T) and K. variicola subsp. tropicalensis for Kp5 (type strain, 1266T = CIP 111654T).

            <ce:abstract xml:lang="en" id="abs0010" view="all" class="author"><ce:abstract-sec id="abssec0010" view="all">
                  <ce:simple-para id="abspara0010" view="all">The bacterial pathogen <ce:italic>Klebsiella pneumoniae</ce:italic> comprises several phylogenetic groups (Kp1 to Kp7), two of which (Kp5 and Kp7) have no taxonomic status. Here we show that group Kp5 is closely related to <ce:italic>Klebsiella variicola</ce:italic> (Kp3), with an average nucleotide identity (ANI) of 96.4%, and that group Kp7 has an ANI of 94.7% with Kp1 (<ce:italic>K. pneumoniae sensu stricto</ce:italic>). Biochemical characteristics and chromosomal beta-lactamase genes also distinguish groups Kp5 and Kp7 from other <ce:italic>Klebsiella</ce:italic> taxa. We propose the names <ce:italic>Klebsiella africanensis</ce:italic> for Kp7 (type strain, 200023<ce:sup loc="post">T</ce:sup> = CIP 111653<ce:sup loc="post">T</ce:sup>) and <ce:italic>K. variicola</ce:italic> subsp. <ce:italic>tropicalensis</ce:italic> for Kp5 (type strain, 1266<ce:sup loc="post">T</ce:sup> = CIP 111654<ce:sup loc="post">T</ce:sup>).</ce:simple-para>
               </ce:abstract-sec></ce:abstract>

                  <ce:label>1</ce:label>
                  <ce:section-title id="sectitle0025">Introduction</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:para id="p0010" view="all">The genus <ce:italic>Klebsiella</ce:italic> includes important human and animal pathogens and is largely distributed in animal carriage and the environment <ce:cross-ref refid="bib1" id="crosref0055">[1]</ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:italic>. Klebsiella pneumoniae</ce:italic> is the most common species isolated from infections of humans and other hosts. Brisse &amp; Verhoef <ce:cross-ref refid="bib2" id="crosref0060">[2]</ce:cross-ref> reported the existence of <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> phylogroups Kp1 to Kp4, and Blin et al. <ce:cross-ref refid="bib3" id="crosref0065">[3]</ce:cross-ref> reported two additional phylogroups, Kp5 and Kp6. Over the years, phylogenetic studies have demonstrated that <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> phylogroups Kp1, Kp2, Kp4 and Kp3 correspond to different taxa, respectively <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> (<ce:italic>sensu stricto</ce:italic>), <ce:italic>Klebsiella quasipneumoniae</ce:italic> subsp<ce:italic>. quasipneumoniae</ce:italic> and subsp<ce:italic>. similipneumoniae</ce:italic>, and <ce:italic>Klebsiella variicola</ce:italic> 
                     <ce:cross-refs refid="bib4 bib5" id="crosrefs0010">[4,5]</ce:cross-refs>. Phylogenetic analyses of Kp6 genomic sequences showed that this phylogroup corresponds to a recently proposed taxon, ‘<ce:italic>Klebsiella quasivariicola’</ce:italic> 
                     <ce:cross-ref refid="bib6" id="crosref0070">[6]</ce:cross-ref>, which remains to be validly published. While screening for <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> fecal carriage in Senegal, we identified strain 200023 (internal strain bank identifier, SB5857), which was not associated with any of the previous phylogroups or species, and which we here call Kp7. Together, <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae sensu stricto</ce:italic> and the other above referenced taxa and phylogroups constitute the <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> complex. Typically, all members of this species complex are misidentified as <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> or as <ce:italic>K. variicola</ce:italic> using standard laboratory methods, which masks their actual clinical and epidemiological significance <ce:cross-refs refid="bib7 bib8 bib9" id="crosrefs0015">[7–9]</ce:cross-refs>. The members of the <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> complex can be correctly distinguished based on whole-genome sequencing (WGS) or by sequencing taxonomic marker genes such as <ce:italic>rpoB.</ce:italic> The chromosomal beta-lactamase gene also varies according to phylogroup: Kp1, Kp2, Kp3 and Kp4 harbor <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">SHV</ce:inf>, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">OKP-A</ce:inf>, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">LEN</ce:inf> and <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">OKP-B</ce:inf> beta-lactamase genes, respectively <ce:cross-refs refid="bib10 bib11" id="crosrefs0020">[10,11]</ce:cross-refs>. Recently, MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry protein biomarkers were described for the identification of <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> complex members (Kp1 to Kp6) <ce:cross-ref refid="bib12" id="crosref0075">[12]</ce:cross-ref>. The aim of the present work was to define the taxonomic status of <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> phylogroups Kp5 and Kp7.</ce:para>
               </ce:section>
               <ce:section id="sec2" role="materials-methods" view="all">
                  <ce:label>2</ce:label>
                  <ce:section-title id="sectitle0030">Materials and methods</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:para id="p0015" view="all">A total of thirty-seven strains of species, subspecies and phylogroups related to <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> were included (<ce:cross-ref refid="tbl1" id="crosref0080">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:float-anchor refid="tbl1"/>, <ce:cross-ref refid="fig1" id="crosref0085">Fig. 1</ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:float-anchor refid="fig1"/>). Strain 1266<ce:sup loc="post">T</ce:sup> (internal strain bank identifier, SB5531) was chosen as reference strain for phylogroup Kp5, whereas strain 200023<ce:sup loc="post">T</ce:sup> (internal strain bank identifier, SB5857) was included as reference strain of Kp7 phylogroup. The genome (Accession Number <ce:inter-ref xlink:href="ncbi-p:ERS214332" id="intref0010" xlink:type="simple">ERS214332</ce:inter-ref>) of the strain 38679 <ce:cross-ref refid="bib13" id="crosref0090">[13]</ce:cross-ref> was included for phylogenetic comparisons even though the strain was not available at the time of this study. Strains were grown in tryptocasein soy agar (TSA) (BioRad, Marnes-La-Coquette, France). The biochemical characterization of the isolates was performed using API20E strips (BioMérieux) and Biolog phenotype microarrays plates PM1 and PM2 (Hayward, CA) following the protocol described by Blin et al. <ce:cross-ref refid="bib3" id="crosref0095">[3]</ce:cross-ref>. To extract the DNA, bacteria were plated on TSA and one colony was grown overnight with shaking at 37 °C in 10 ml tryptocasein soy broth (TSB). DNA was extracted using the Wizard Genomic DNA purification kit (Promega, Charbonnières-les-Bains, France) and stored at −20 °C. Genomic sequencing was performed using NextSeq-500 instrument (Illumina, San Diego, USA) with a 2 × 150 nt paired-end protocol.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="p0020" view="all">Contig sequences were assembled using SPAdes v3.10.0 <ce:cross-ref refid="bib14" id="crosref0100">[14]</ce:cross-ref> and annotated with Prokka v1.12 <ce:cross-ref refid="bib15" id="crosref0105">[15]</ce:cross-ref>. JSpeciesWS <ce:cross-ref refid="bib16" id="crosref0110">[16]</ce:cross-ref> was used to calculate the BLAST-based average nucleotide identity (ANI). The <ce:italic>in silico</ce:italic> DNA–DNA hybridization (isDDH) was estimated using the GGDC tool (<ce:inter-ref xlink:href="http://ggdc.dsmz.de" id="intref0015" xlink:type="simple">http://ggdc.dsmz.de</ce:inter-ref>
                     <ce:underline>;</ce:underline> formula 2) <ce:cross-ref refid="bib17" id="crosref0115">[17]</ce:cross-ref>. Barrnap (<ce:inter-ref xlink:href="https://github.com/tseemann/barrnap" id="intref0020" xlink:type="simple">https://github.com/tseemann/barrnap</ce:inter-ref>) was used to detect and extract 16S rRNA sequences from genome assemblies, whereas internal portions of genetic markers (<ce:italic>fusA</ce:italic>, <ce:italic>gapA</ce:italic>, <ce:italic>gyrA</ce:italic>, <ce:italic>leuS</ce:italic>, <ce:italic>rpoB</ce:italic>) were detected and extracted using BLAST and compared with previously reported sequences <ce:cross-ref refid="bib4" id="crosref0120">[4]</ce:cross-ref>. Chromosomal beta-lactamases were also extracted, and the new LEN and OKP amino-acid sequence variants were submitted to the Institut Pasteur MLST nomenclature database (<ce:inter-ref xlink:href="https://bigsdb.pasteur.fr/klebsiella" id="intref0025" xlink:type="simple">https://bigsdb.pasteur.fr/klebsiella</ce:inter-ref>) for variant number attribution, and to NCBI for accession number attribution. Phylogenetic analyses based on the 16S rRNA gene sequence, concatenation of five housekeeping genes (2,609 aligned bp in total) and chromosomal beta-lactamases were performed using MEGA v7.0 with the neighbor-joining method. Genetic distances were estimated using the Jukes-Cantor substitution model in the case of nucleotide sequences and using the Jones-Taylor-Thornton (JTT) model for beta-lactamase protein sequences. For genome-based phylogenetic analysis, a phylogenetic tree was reconstructed from the concatenation of 1,703 genes defined as core genes using Roary v3.12 <ce:cross-ref refid="bib18" id="crosref0125">[18]</ce:cross-ref> with a BLASTP identity cut-off of 90%. <ce:italic>Klebsiella oxytoca</ce:italic> ATCC 13182<ce:sup loc="post">T</ce:sup> (<ce:inter-ref xlink:href="ncbi-p:ERS2016112" id="intref0125a" xlink:type="simple">ERS2016112</ce:inter-ref>) was used as outgroup. Aligned characters affected by recombination events were detected and removed from the core gene alignment using Gubbins v2.2.0 <ce:cross-ref refid="bib19" id="crosref0130">[19]</ce:cross-ref>. A maximum-likelihood phylogenetic tree was inferred using FastTree v2.1.7 <ce:cross-ref refid="bib20" id="crosref0135">[20]</ce:cross-ref>.</ce:para>

               <ce:section id="sec3" role="results" view="all">
                  <ce:label>3</ce:label>
                  <ce:section-title id="sectitle0035">Results and discussion</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:para id="p0025" view="all">The nearly complete (1,462 nt) <ce:italic>rrs</ce:italic> gene sequence coding for 16S rRNA was compared among <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> strains. Only a few sites showed reliable variation (<ce:cross-ref refid="appsec1" id="crosref0140">Fig. S1</ce:cross-ref>), and all sequences were &gt;98.8% similar. The Kp5 reference strain 1266<ce:sup loc="post">T</ce:sup> differed from F2R9<ce:sup loc="post">T</ce:sup> (<ce:italic>K. variicola</ce:italic> type strain) by 6 (0.41%) nucleotide positions, whereas Kp7 strain 200023<ce:sup loc="post">T</ce:sup> differed from KPN1705 (Kp6 reference strain) and DSM 30104<ce:sup loc="post">T</ce:sup> (<ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> type strain) by 12 (0.82%) nucleotide positions in both cases. The phylogenetic information content of the highly conserved 16S rRNA sequence did not allow to reliably infer a phylogeny of 16S sequences (<ce:cross-ref refid="appsec1" id="crosref0145">Fig. S1</ce:cross-ref>) as previously reported <ce:cross-refs refid="bib21 bib22" id="crosrefs0025">[21,22]</ce:cross-refs>.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="p0030" view="all">Maximum-likelihood phylogeny based on the recombination-purged nucleotide sequence alignments of 1,703 core genes (<ce:cross-ref refid="fig1" id="crosref0150">Fig. 1</ce:cross-ref>) showed seven highly supported branches. Phylogroup Kp5 was strongly associated with Kp3 (<ce:italic>K. variicola</ce:italic>), whereas Kp7 was associated with Kp6 and Kp1 (<ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic>) groups. The latter result suggests that Kp6 and Kp7 share a common ancestor with <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> and may therefore represent the closest relatives of the clinically most significant member of the species complex. Phylogenies based on the five concatenated housekeeping genes <ce:italic>fusA</ce:italic>, <ce:italic>gapA, gyrA, leuS</ce:italic> and <ce:italic>rpoB,</ce:italic> and on chromosomal beta-lactamases, both corroborated the distinction among the seven groups (<ce:cross-ref refid="appsec1" id="crosref0155">Fig. S2 and Fig. S3</ce:cross-ref>). The phylogeny of chromosomal beta-lactamases was highly concordant with the core gene phylogeny, with the remarkable exception of phylogroup Kp6, which harbored two different types of beta-lactamases, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">LEN</ce:inf> and <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">OKP-D</ce:inf>, depending on the strain. This is consistent with frequent homologous recombination observed in the Kp6 phylogroup using Gubbins (data not shown) and as previously reported <ce:cross-ref refid="bib23" id="crosref0160">[23]</ce:cross-ref>. In order to estimate the genome-wide divergence among phylogroups, average nucleotide identity (ANI) was used. The ANI values of Kp5 (strain 1266<ce:sup loc="post">T</ce:sup>) with <ce:italic>K. variicola</ce:italic> F2R9<ce:sup loc="post">T</ce:sup> (Kp3) was 96.4%, above the cut-off value (approximately 95–96%) proposed for species distinction <ce:cross-refs refid="bib24 bib25" id="crosrefs0030">[24,25]</ce:cross-refs>. In contrast, the ANI values of Kp7 strain 200023<ce:sup loc="post">T</ce:sup> with ‘<ce:italic>K. quasivariicola’</ce:italic> KPN1705 (Kp6) and <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> DSM30104<ce:sup loc="post">T</ce:sup> (Kp1) were 94.9% and 94.7%, respectively (<ce:cross-ref refid="tbl2" id="crosref0165">Table 2</ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:float-anchor refid="tbl2"/>). The isDDH relatedness of 1266<ce:sup loc="post">T</ce:sup> (Kp5) with Kp3 strains ranged from 75.0% to 75.5%, but ranged from 51.2% to 56.0% with the other phylogroups. For 200023<ce:sup loc="post">T</ce:sup> the isDDH relatedness with Kp1 to Kp6 phylogroups was between 50.5% and 61.9%, well below the ∼70% cut-off used to define bacterial species. Therefore, both ANI and isDDH analyses lead us to consider that 1266<ce:sup loc="post">T</ce:sup> is a subspecies of <ce:italic>K. variicola</ce:italic> (Kp3) and that 200023<ce:sup loc="post">T</ce:sup> represents a novel <ce:italic>Klebsiella</ce:italic> species.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="p0035" view="all">The phenotypic characteristics of the strains were compared (<ce:cross-ref refid="appsec1" id="crosref0170">Table S1</ce:cross-ref>, <ce:cross-ref refid="fig1" id="crosref0175">Fig. 1</ce:cross-ref>). Using API20E strips we confirmed that all Kp5 strains and 200023<ce:sup loc="post">T</ce:sup> were positive for the utilization of lactose and mannitol, for the urease, malonate, lysine decarboxylase, Voges–Proskauer and ONPG tests, and reduced nitrate to nitrite, whereas they were negative for indole and ornithine decarboxylase. Furthermore, microscopy confirmed that all strains were non-motile. The use of phenotype microarrays provided insights into the metabolic profiles of the seven phylogroups and identified carbon sources that differentiate Kp5 and Kp7 strains form other groups (<ce:cross-ref refid="fig1" id="crosref0180">Fig. 1</ce:cross-ref>). The inability to metabolize mono-methyl succinate, <ce:small-caps>d</ce:small-caps>-lactic acid methyl ester and 3-O-(ß-<ce:small-caps>d</ce:small-caps>-galactopyranosyl)-<ce:small-caps>d</ce:small-caps>-arabinose, and the ability to metabolize <ce:small-caps>d</ce:small-caps>-psicose differentiated Kp5 strains from <ce:italic>K. variicola</ce:italic>. Kp7 strain 200023<ce:sup loc="post">T</ce:sup> was unique in being unable to metabolize <ce:small-caps>d</ce:small-caps>-arabitol and was also differentiated from ‘<ce:italic>K. quasivariicola</ce:italic>’ and <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae,</ce:italic> its closest phylogenetic neighbors, by the ability to metabolize <ce:small-caps>d</ce:small-caps>,<ce:small-caps>l</ce:small-caps>-carnitine and 4-hydroxy-<ce:small-caps>l</ce:small-caps>-proline, respectively.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="p0040" view="all">Based on the above genomic and phenotypic characteristics, we propose that Kp5 be considered as a subspecies of <ce:italic>K. variicola</ce:italic>, which we propose to name <ce:italic>K. variicola</ce:italic> subsp. <ce:italic>tropicalensis</ce:italic>; whereas Kp7 represents a novel species, which we propose to name <ce:italic>Klebsiella africanensis.</ce:italic>
                  </ce:para>
