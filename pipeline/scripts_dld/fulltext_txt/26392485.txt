LETTER 

Aminoglycoside antibiotics such as amikacin and gentamicin, often along with β-lactams, have been used for the treatment of serious infections caused by Gram-negative and Gram-positive pathogenic bacteria in clinical settings ( 1 ). However, bacteria have been furnished with various resistance mechanisms to become resistant to aminoglycosides ( 2 ). Recently, the spread of 16S rRNA methyltransferase (16S-RMTase), responsible for very-high-level resistance to various aminoglycosides (MIC > 256 μg/ml), has become apparent in both human and veterinary medicine ( 1 ). Since the first report in 2003, 10 16S-RMTase-encoding genes, named rmtA , rmtB , rmtC , rmtD , rmtE , rmtF , rmtG , rmtH , armA , and npmA , have been identified ( 3 ). Of the known 16S RMTases, rmtE has been reported in only two Escherichia coli strains, which were identified from a calf and a patient in the United States, respectively ( 4 , 5 ). Here, we reported the first rmtE -producing E. coli strain isolated from swine in China. 

A total of 963 nonduplicate E. coli isolates (479 from avian and 484 from swine), isolated from diseased food-producing animals between 2002 and 2012 in Guangdong, China, were used in this research. Among them, 277 swine isolates and 183 avian strains were derived from farms, while the other isolates were recovered from diagnostic laboratories. E. coli S68 and E. coli 87 were isolated from diseased pigs in 2005 and 2010, respectively. The two strains were isolated in two farms located in one district. Both strains exhibited high-level resistance to amikacin (>256 μg/ml) and gentamicin (>256 μg/ml). PCR detection and sequencing of 16S-RMTase-encoding genes then indicated that rmtE was present in both strains. However, a single base mutation of T→C was detected at nucleotide 20 of rmtE , which causes a replacement of Val ( 6 ) by Ala in the gene product. According to the suggestions of the nomenclature rules, we would like to designate it rmtE2 ( 7 ). 

Pulsed-field gel electrophoresis (PFGE) with XbaI was performed as described previously ( 6 ). Two isolates shared identical PFGE types (data not shown), which suggested that the spread of rmtE2 was probably due to a specific E. coli clone. Phylogenetic typing and multilocus sequence typing (MLST) were conducted as described previously ( 8 , 9 ). The results assigned the two strains to phylogenetic group A and sequence type 898 (ST898), in contrast to the rmtE -positive E. coli YDC637 isolate of human origin, which is of phylogenetic group D and sequence type 354 (ST354) ( 5 ). 

Conjugation experiments performed by the broth-mating method were conducted as previously described ( 10 ). E. coli C600, highly resistant to streptomycin, was used as the recipient in the conjugation experiments. Two transconjugants with high-level resistance to amikacin and gentamicin were successfully obtained ( Table 1 ). The two plasmids showed identical EcoRI restriction digestion profiles ( Fig. 1b ). The size of the plasmids carrying rmtE2 was estimated to be approximately 110 kb by S1 nuclease pulsed-field gel electrophoresis (S1-PFGE) and Southern blotting ( 11 ) ( Fig. 1a ), and the replicon type (determined by PCR-based replicon typing [PBRT]) was determined to be IncI1 ( 12 ), while rmtE -carrying pYDC637 was characterized to be an IncA/C plasmid ( 13 ). This plasmid conferred resistance only to aminoglycosides and not to other classes of agents ( Table 1 ). PCR mapping according to the sequences of the pYDC637 plasmid suggested that an IS CR20 -like transposase was located upstream of rmtE2 but that the plasmid harbored a 697-bp region in the interval. Sequences downstream of rmtE2 could not be determined due to lack of amplification. We then confirmed the results by random extension-based two-step PCR (RETS-PCR) ( 14 ), and the result indicated that an IS Vs1 -like transposase was located downstream of rmtE2 .

ST898 has been reported only in two pigs and one dog in Germany ( http://mlst.warwick.ac.uk/mlst/dbs/Ecoli/GetTableInfo_html ). And according to recent reports, IncI1 plasmids, ranged from 80 kb to 130 kb in size, appear to have a close association with extended-spectrum β-lactamase, especially with CTX-Ms ( 15 ). Among the 16S rRNA methyltransferase genes, rmtB and armA are commonly identified in E. coli , while rmtE is rarely detected; therefore, routine surveillance of rmtE in aminoglycoside-resistant Enterobacteriaceae is warranted. When MICs of amikacin and gentamicin are found to be above 256 μg/ml, both rmtB and armA and other rarely seen16S rRNA methyltransferase genes (such as rmtE ) should be detected. On the other hand, amikacin has been widely used in food-producing animals in China. Based on the differences in the sequences, origins of the strains, STs, plasmid incompatibility groups, and genetic environments of the genes ( Fig. 2 ), rmtE2 may have been acquired by E. coli in China in an event separate from acquisition of rmtE1 in the United States.

In conclusion, here we report two rmtE2 -positive E. coli strains of swine origin belonging to phylogenetic group A and ST898. Of note, it may be speculated that the clone and the plasmid carrying the rmtE2 gene could spread by both vertical and horizontal transfer. To our knowledge, this is the first report of rmtE2 in China. Further characterization of the IncI1 plasmid is to be done.
