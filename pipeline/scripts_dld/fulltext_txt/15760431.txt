                  <ce:simple-para id="spara30" view="all">Carbapenem resistance associated with class D β-lactamases is an increasing problem in <ce:italic>Acinetobacter baumannii</ce:italic>. Most enzymes of this class reported so far belong to two subgroups, 1 and 2; however, a novel class D carbapenemase (OXA-51) has been reported recently which shares 56% and &lt;63% amino-acid identity with subgroups 1 and 2, respectively, and which belongs to a third subgroup. This study describes a further seven novel subgroup 3 β-lactamases in carbapenem-resistant <ce:italic>A. baumannii</ce:italic> isolates from four continents.</ce:simple-para>

               <ce:para id="para10" view="all">
                  <ce:italic>Acinetobacter baumannii</ce:italic> is an opportunistic nosocomial pathogen associated with a range of infections, particularly in ventilated patients in intensive therapy units [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref30" refid="bib1">1</ce:cross-ref>,<ce:cross-ref id="cecref40" refid="bib2">2</ce:cross-ref>]. An alarming trend has been the emergence of multiresistant isolates that are resistant to one or more of the carbapenems, with the result that pan-drug resistance is now emerging [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref50" refid="bib3">3</ce:cross-ref>]. β-Lactamase-mediated carbapenem resistance is being reported with increasing frequency. Some isolates from Asia, southern Europe and the UK have IMP and VIM molecular class B metallo-β-lactamases [<ce:cross-refs id="cecrefs10" refid="bib4 bib5 bib6 bib7">4–7</ce:cross-refs>], but carbapenem-hydrolysing β-lactamases that belong to class D have also been described. The first class D carbapenemase (OXA-23) [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref60" refid="bib8">8</ce:cross-ref>] was detected in an imipenem-resistant isolate from Edinburgh in 1985 [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref70" refid="bib9">9</ce:cross-ref>] that belongs to subgroup 1, along with OXA-27 (Singapore) [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref80" refid="bib10">10</ce:cross-ref>] and OXA-49 (China) (GenBank accession number AY288523). OXA-24, -25, -26 and -40, identified in isolates from Spain, Belgium, France and Portugal, differ by one or two amino-acids, and belong to subgroup 2 [<ce:cross-refs id="cecrefs20" refid="bib10 bib11 bib12 bib13">10–13</ce:cross-refs>]. The two subgroups share 60% amino-acid homology, but all of these enzymes possess a tyrosine-to-phenylalanine substitution in the conserved Y-G-N motif of the class D protein [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref90" refid="bib14">14</ce:cross-ref>]. A novel class D carbapenemase (OXA-51) has been characterised recently in two distinct clones of <ce:italic>A. baumannii</ce:italic> from Argentina. OXA-51 shares 56% and 61–62% amino-acid homology with subgroups 1 and 2, respectively, and thus represents a new subgroup of carbapenemases in <ce:italic>Acinetobacter</ce:italic> [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref100" refid="bib15">15</ce:cross-ref>]. The present report describes a further seven novel class D β-lactamases belonging to this new subgroup in carbapenem-resistant <ce:italic>A. baumannii</ce:italic> isolates collected worldwide.</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para20" view="all">Multiresistant <ce:italic>A. baumannii</ce:italic> clinical isolates were collected between 1996 and 2000 from centres in Argentina, South Africa, Hong Kong, Spain, Singapore and Turkey. Ten isolates from Argentina included in this study belonged to two clones (designated as pulsed-field gel electrophoresis genotypes B and C) that were identified previously in an outbreak of carbapenem resistant isolates from two hospitals in Buenos Aires (39th Interscience Conference on Antimicrobial Agents and Chemotherapy, abstract 1463); clones B and C were distinct from two other clones of carbapenem-resistant <ce:italic>A. baumannii</ce:italic> that produced the OXA-51 carbapenemase [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref110" refid="bib15">15</ce:cross-ref>]. The isolates from Turkey included six related multiresistant <ce:italic>A. baumannii</ce:italic> isolates (designated as pulsed-field gel electrophoresis genotype D) that were collected from the surgical intensive care unit of Dokuz Eylul University Hospital in Izmir (39th Interscience Conference on Antimicrobial Agents and Chemotherapy, abstract 1463). The remaining isolates in this study included two from South Africa collected from the Chris Hani Baragwanath Hospital, Soweto, three isolates from the KK Women's and Children's Hospital, Singapore, two isolates from a hospital clinic in Barcelona, and two isolates from the Prince of Wales Hospital, Hong Kong. Clinical <ce:italic>A. baumannii</ce:italic> strains 789 (carbapenem-susceptible) and 788 (OXA-51 producer) were included as controls for PCRanalysis.</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para30" view="all">Imipenem and meropenem MICs were determined according to published guidelines [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref120" refid="bib16">16</ce:cross-ref>,<ce:cross-ref id="cecref130" refid="bib17">17</ce:cross-ref>]. DNA was extracted from isolates for PCR analysis by boiling one to three colonies in 100 µL of sterile ultra-pure water for 10 min and centrifuging briefly. PCRs (50-µL final volumes) contained 2 µL of DNA extract, 20 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8.8), 10 mM KCl, 10 mM (NH<ce:inf loc="post">4</ce:inf>)<ce:inf loc="post">2</ce:inf>SO<ce:inf loc="post">4</ce:inf>, 2 mM MgSO<ce:inf loc="post">4</ce:inf>, 0.8 mM dNTPs and 1.25 U of <ce:italic>Pfu</ce:italic> DNA polymerase (Promega, Southampton, UK). To determine the presence of <ce:italic>bla<ce:inf loc="post">OXA-51</ce:inf>
                  </ce:italic>-related sequences, primers designed to amplify the OXA-51 gene [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref140" refid="bib15">15</ce:cross-ref>] were used. PCR conditions on a Techne Px2 Gradient Thermal Cycler (Thermo Hybaid, Ashford, UK) comprised 95°C for 5 min, followed by 30 cycles of 95°C for 1 min, 50°C for 1 min and 72°C for 2 min, with a final extension period of 72°C for 10 min. Purified amplicons from three separate PCRs were sequenced in both directions on an ABI Prism Automated Sequencer (Perkin-Elmer, Beaconsfield, UK). Protein sequences were aligned by hierarchical clustering [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref150" refid="bib18">18</ce:cross-ref>].</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para40" view="all">The isolates studied had imipenem and meropenem MICs of 4–128 mg/L, suggesting that additional factors, such as outer-membrane impermeability, may also be involved in the carbapenem resistance of these isolates. Sequencing of an amplified 1000-bp product identified a gene with an 825-bp open reading frame that encoded a protein with 274 amino-acids. Analysis of the protein from Argentinian strains 884 (clone B) and 790 (clone C) identified a novel β-lactamase (designated OXA-65) that shared 98% amino-acid identity with OXA-51. A further six novel sequences with 97–98% amino-acid homology with OXA-51 (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref160" refid="fig1">Fig. 1xd</ce:cross-ref>
                  <ce:float-anchor refid="fig1"/>) were identified, namely OXA-64 in isolate 889 (South Africa), OXA-66 in isolates 806 (Spain), 811 (Hong Kong) and 864 (Singapore), OXA-68 in isolate 809 (Spain), OXA-69 in isolates 877, 881 (both Singapore) and 823 (clone D, Turkey), OXA-70 in isolate 812 (Hong Kong), and OXA-71 in isolate 888 (South Africa).</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para50" view="all">Comparison of these enzymes with OXA-51 identified between three and eight amino-acid substitutions (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref170" refid="tbl1">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>
                  <ce:float-anchor refid="tbl1"/>). All had an A → G change at nucleotide position 13, resulting in a threonineto-alanine substitution at amino-acid 5. In addition, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-71</ce:inf> had an A → C change at the third position in this codon (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref180" refid="tbl1">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>). Each of these genes also had a T → C change at nucleotide postion 143, causing a valine-to-alanine substitution at amino-acid 48. As observed with OXA-51, all enzymes had a valine-to-isoleucine substitution in the S-X-V class D motif (amino-acids 127–129 in OXA-51) and, in contrast to carbapenemases in subgroups 1 and 2 [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref190" refid="bib8">8</ce:cross-ref>,<ce:cross-refs id="cecrefs30" refid="bib10 bib11 bib12">10–12</ce:cross-refs>], a tyrosine of the Y-GN motif (amino-acids 153–155) was also retained (data not shown). However, subgroup 3 enzymes were similar to those in subgroup 2 in that the K-T-G motif (amino-acids 217–219 in OXA-51) was replaced by K-S-G [<ce:cross-refs id="cecrefs40" refid="bib10 bib11 bib12">10–12</ce:cross-refs>]. Identical silent changes were present at 11 nucleotide positions in at least two different OXA genes (data not shown).</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para60" view="all">In conclusion, this study identified seven novel OXA-type β-lactamases related to OXA-51 in carbapenem-resistant <ce:italic>A. baumannii</ce:italic> isolates from six countries, with two of these enzymes found in isolates from more than one continent. Carbapenemases in subgroup 2 have been identified so far only in strains from Europe [<ce:cross-refs id="cecrefs50" refid="bib10 bib11 bib12 bib13">10–13</ce:cross-refs>,<ce:cross-ref id="cecref200" refid="bib19">19</ce:cross-ref>], and OXA-23 is the only enzyme in subgroup 1 to have been found in more than one country (GenBank accession number AY288523) [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref210" refid="bib20">20</ce:cross-ref>,<ce:cross-ref id="cecref220" refid="bib21">21</ce:cross-ref>]. Consequently, subgroup 3 now represents the largest and most diverse collection of class D carbapenemases in <ce:italic>A. baumannii</ce:italic>, and may become the major mechanism of carbapenem resistance in this species.</ce:para>
