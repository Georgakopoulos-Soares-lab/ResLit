Abstract 

Three Acinetobacter baumannii isolates that possess OXA-40 group carbapenemase genes were identified. They belonged to novel sequence types (ST122, ST123, and ST124) and harbored bla OXA-160 , bla OXA-72 , and bla OXA-40 , respectively. OXA-160 is a novel variant of OXA-40 with a P227S substitution. An isogenic Escherichia coli clone producing OXA-160 was more susceptible to carbapenems than a clone producing OXA-40. The genetic environment of bla OXA-160 and bla OXA-40 beyond the putative XerC/XerD recombination sites was distinct from the scaffold reported previously. 

Acinetobacter baumannii is emerging as a major nosocomial pathogen worldwide ( 14 ). Four groups of OXA-type plasmid-mediated carbapenemases have been identified in A. baumannii : OXA-23, -40, -58, and -143. Among them, OXA-23 is the most prevalent enzyme ( 13 , 14 ). The aim of the present study was to characterize the genetic contexts of three carbapenem-resistant A. baumannii isolates, AB195, AB199, and AB285, which were recovered from epidemiologically unrelated patients at the University of Pittsburgh Medical Center and positive for a bla OXA-40 group gene. 

AB195 was identified from the bronchus of a donor lung which was transplanted to a 69-year-old female with severe emphysema in 2008. The recipient was treated with intravenous ampicillin-sulbactam and nebulized colistimethate with favorable clinical outcomes. AB199 was identified along with Enterococcus faecalis in the blood of a 56-year-old male with a history of stroke, scrotal ulcers, and end-stage kidney disease who had been admitted with altered mental status in 2008. He was not treated with antimicrobials, as this was felt to represent contamination. The patient subsequently improved with supportive therapy. AB285 was recovered from the bronchus of donor lungs, which were transplanted to a 47-year-old female with sarcoidosis and pulmonary hypertension in 2009. The recipient was treated with intravenous ampicillin-sulbactam, doripenem, colistimethate, and nebulized colistimethate and eventually had a favorable outcome as well. A. baumannii AB1204 is a representative isolate from the outbreak of OXA-40-producing A. baumannii that took place in Chicago in 2005 ( 8 ). 

The four clinical isolates were resistant to all β-lactams, including carbapenems, aminoglycosides, and fluoroquinolones. The MICs of imipenem, meropenem, and doripenem were all ≥32 μg/ml, except for AB195, for which the MICs ranged between 8 and 16 μg/ml, as determined by the agar dilution method (Table 1 ).

All four isolates were positive for a bla OXA-40 group gene by PCR analysis. Of them, AB195 carried bla OXA-160 , a novel variant of bla OXA-40 with a P227S substitution in the deduced amino acid sequence according to the Ambler numbering scheme. AB199 encoded bla OXA-72 , a variant of bla OXA-40 with a G224P substitution in the amino acid sequence, which has been reported in East Asian, Southern European, and South American countries ( 3 , 6 , 7 , 9 , 17 ). Both AB285 and AB1204 carried bla OXA-40 . bla OXA-40 has been reported from Chicago and Houston ( 4 , 8 ), whereas this is the first report of its variants in the United States. bla OXA-23 and bla OXA-58 , the other plasmid-mediated carbapenemase genes frequently reported in A. baumannii , were not detected in any of the isolates. bla ADC group and bla OXA-51 group genes were detected in all of the isolates, as expected. In addition, AB195 and AB285 were highly resistant to amikacin (MIC, >256 μg/ml) and possessed armA , encoding 16S rRNA methylase, as well as aac(6 ′ )-Ib , encoding aminoglycoside acetyltransferase. aph(3 ′ )-VI , which encodes aminoglycoside phosphotransferase and confers resistance to amikacin, was identified in all isolates except AB195. 

Pulsed-field gel electrophoresis (PFGE) was performed using ApaI as the restriction enzyme ( 1 ). The four A. baumannii isolates showed distinct macrodigestion patterns (Fig. 1 ). The isolates were then subjected to multilocus sequence typing (MLST) ( 2 ), with modification of the primers to obtain consistent amplification of the loci ( 10 ). The isolates belonged to 3 novel sequence types (STs) (ST122 for AB195 and AB285, ST123 for AB199, and ST124 for AB1204) with two novel gdhB alleles (60 in AB1204 and 61 in AB195, AB199, and AB285) and one novel gpi66 allele in AB1204. ST122 belongs to clonal complex (CC) 92 and is a single-locus variant of ST92, which is an internationally disseminated ST belonging to the European clone II group. ST123 is a triple-locus variant of ST92. ST124 is distantly related to CC113, whose STs have primarily been deposited from Argentina but also Brazil and Spain. These findings suggested that the isolates identified at our hospital represented sporadic cases and were distinct from the strain that caused an outbreak in Chicago.

Escherichia coli DH10B and A. baumannii HE130, a clinical isolate susceptible to carbapenems and other antimicrobials, were used as the recipients for transformation experiments ( 1 ). The plasmids were extracted using the standard alkaline lysis method ( 15 ). The transformants were selected on Luria-Bertani (LB) agar plates containing ampicillin (50 μg/ml) or meropenem (0.5 μg/ml). All four clinical isolates yielded transformants which were positive for a bla OXA-40 group gene with A. baumannii HE130 as the recipient but not with E. coli DH10B as the recipient. All of the A. baumannii transformants demonstrated a ≥32-fold increase of carbapenem MICs compared to those of the recipient strain (Table 1 ). These results suggested plasmidic location of the bla OXA-40 group genes and their contribution to carbapenem resistance. Resistance to non-β-lactam antimicrobials, including tetracycline, gentamicin, amikacin, nalidixic acid, and ciprofloxacin, was not cotransferred to any of the transformants. 

To assess whether the amino acid substitution observed in bla OXA-160 affected the level of resistance to carbapenems and other β-lactams, isogenic E. coli clones harboring bla OXA-40 , bla OXA-72 , or bla OXA-160 were constructed. The structural genes corresponding to the enzymes were amplified with primers bla OXA-40 -F-XbaI (5′-CGGC TCTAGA ATGAAAAAATTTATACTTCCTATATTCAGC-3′) and bla OXA-40 -R-BamHI (5′-CGGC GGATCC TTAAATGATTCCAAGATTTTCTAGCG-3′) (underlining indicates the recognition sites for the restriction enzymes [XbaI and BamHI, respectively]). The PCR products as well as pBC-SK(−) (Stratagene, La Jolla, CA) were digested with XbaI and BamHI, ligated, and used to transform E. coli DH10B by electroporation. Transformants harboring the vector with the bla OXA-40 group genes were selected on LB agar plates containing chloramphenicol (30 μg/ml) and ampicillin (50 μg/ml) or meropenem (0.25 μg/ml). The MICs of imipenem, meropenem, and doripenem for E. coli DH10B(pOXA-160) and E. coli DH10B(pOXA-72) were generally lower than those for E. coli DH10B(pOXA-40) (Table 1 ). G224 and P227 are located in proximity to the KSG active domain and have been implicated as residues potentially crucial for hydrolysis of carbapenems ( 16 ). Detailed kinetic studies are required to confirm the role of these residues. 

To investigate the genetic environments of bla OXA-40 , bla OXA-72 , and bla OXA-160 , various DNA inserts were sequenced using custom sequencing primers. The sequence surrounding bla OXA-72 in AB199 showed >99% nucleotide identity to those reported for bla OXA-40 identified in Italy and Spain (pABVA01 and pMMCU3; the sequence of bla OXA-24 has been updated to that of bla OXA-40 , thus they represent the same gene, referred to as bla OXA-40 in this work) ( 5 , 12 ). Sequence analysis of the genetic environment surrounding bla OXA-40 and bla OXA-160 revealed 100% identity among AB195, AB285, and AB1204 (Fig. 2 ), which was distinct from that of bla OXA-72 in AB199. In all isolates, the conserved inverted repeat sequences homologous to the XerC/XerD binding sites were present upstream and downstream of bla OXA-40 , bla OXA-72 , and bla OXA-160 , beyond which the sequences diverged. The sequences adjacent to the XerC/XerD binding sites of bla OXA-40 and bla OXA-160 showed a high degree of similarity with part of plasmid pAV2 in Acinetobacter venetianus strain VE-C3 ( 11 ). XerC/XerD binding sites are presumed to act as site-specific recombination targets responsible for the mobilization of discrete DNA modules in Acinetobacter spp. ( 5 ). These findings suggested that multiple genetic motifs exist for the bla OXA-40 group genes, likely as a result of XerC/XerD-mediated transpositional events.

In conclusion, we identified carbapenem-resistant A. baumannii isolates producing diverse OXA-40 group carbapenemases, including OXA-40, OXA-72, and a new variant OXA-160. The bla OXA-40 group genes were located on multiple genetic structures among isolates belonging to diverse sequence types. Our findings suggest that the bla OXA-40 group genes were likely acquired by A. baumannii through independent events. 

Nucleotide sequence accession numbers. 

The nucleotide sequences reported in this paper have been submitted to the GenBank database under accession no. GU199038 , GU199039 , and GU199040 for bla OXA-160 , bla OXA-72 , and bla OXA-40 , respectively.
