Title: Exploring the third-generation tetracycline resistance of multidrug-resistant livestock-associated methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus ST9 across healthcare settings in China.

## ABSTRACT
Abstract Background The overuse of antibiotics in livestock is contributing to the burden of antimicrobial resistance in humans, representing a One Health challenge. Livestock-associated methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (LA-MRSA) has recently become a growing concern, and ST9 is the major LA-MRSA lineage in China and has emerged in clinical settings. Methods Antimicrobial susceptibility testing was used to evaluate the tetracycline resistance of ST9 MRSA collections, and gene cloning experiments were performed to explore the resistance mechanisms. Whole-genome sequencing and comparative genomics were used to analyse the genetic features of clinical ST9 isolates. A phylogenetic tree was constructed to investigate the relationship of human- and livestock-derived ST9 isolates. Results Clinical ST9 isolates were found to possess several types of resistance genes and resistance-related mutations and were multidrug-resistant. Notably, all clinical ST9 isolates were resistant to third-generation tetracyclines. Cloning experiments showed that both the acquisition of the tetracycline resistance gene tet (L)/ tet (63) and a mutation in the rpsJ gene contributed to third-generation tetracycline resistance. Phylogenetic analysis showed that the ST9 isolates collected in healthcare systems were probably transmitted from livestock. The ST9 lineage underwent multiple interspecies recombination events and gained many resistance elements. Furthermore, the resistance to third-generation tetracyclines may have evolved under tetracycline pressure in livestock. Conclusions The evolution of ST9 MRSA in livestock and transmission of this clone between humans and livestock highlight the importance of establishing control strategies with the One Health approach to reduce the burden of antibiotic resistance.

## INTRODUCTION
The emergence, evolution and spread of antibiotic resistance occur across boundaries between different settings and may represent a risk to human health, which is a One Health problem. 1 MRSA is one of the most important multidrug-resistant pathogens of humans and various species of agricultural, companion and wild animals. 2 , 3 The ever-changing epidemiology of MRSA has raised global concerns after MRSA colonization and infections appeared in farm workers and pigs in the Netherlands in the early 2000s. 4 , 5 Unlike healthcare-associated MRSA and community-associated MRSA, this unique MRSA clone was designated livestock-associated MRSA (LA-MRSA). Since then, LA-MRSA has raised considerable concern due to its threat to public health. ST398 is the most dominant LA-MRSA lineage worldwide, especially in Europe and North America. 2 , 6 Many studies have reported the emergence of ST398 in the clinic. 7 Further research confirmed that ST398 represented two distinct phylogenetic clades, one of which was associated with animals, while the other was with humans. 8 , 9 Although ST398 has also emerged in Asia, ST9 MRSA clone appears to be more prevalent in most Asian countries. 10 Recently, the molecular characteristics of ST9 isolates from livestock in China were elucidated via whole-genome sequencing. 11 , 12 Nevertheless, the mechanism underlying the multidrug resistance of ST9 MRSA is not fully understood. In our previous study of patients with S. aureus infections, 13 several ST9 MRSA isolates were collected. However, the relationship and evolution of ST9 MRSA from livestock and the healthcare system were not investigated. Tetracycline antibiotics, which interact with the ribosome, represent a large and diverse group of compounds with a long history. 14 Currently, tetracyclines are frequently used in both human medicine and agriculture. First-generation tetracyclines, such as chlortetracycline and oxytetracycline, are used as veterinary antibiotics worldwide. 15 The first modern tetracycline introduced into clinic was tigecycline, followed by eravacycline and omadacycline. 16 These third-generation tetracycline antibiotics have the advantage of superior potency against multidrug-resistant bacteria, including MRSA. 16–19 Moreover, resistance to third-generation tetracycline antibiotics in MRSA is rare. However, following the discovery of the tet (X) gene, 20 it has been observed that the use of ‘older’ tetracyclines in food animals may be a driving force behind the dissemination of resistance to the ‘newer’ third-generation tetracyclines. Hence, an epidemiological study of resistance to third-generation tetracyclines in MRSA, especially LA-MRSA, is necessary. In this study, we reported the emergence of third-generation tetracycline-resistant ST9 MRSA before eravacycline and omadacycline were launched on the Chinese market. The resistance mechanisms to the third-generation tetracyclines in these ST9 MRSA were investigated, and a phylogenetic tree was constructed to investigate the isolates in a global context and to identify possible livestock to human transmission events.

## MATERIALS AND METHODS
Bacterial isolates S. aureus isolates and clinical data were collected in a multicentre prospective study, 13 in which 471 clinical MRSA isolates were subjected to whole-genome sequencing. Among 471 MRSA, ST9 and ST398 isolates were tested for the in vitro susceptibility to third-generation tetracyclines. ST59, ST239 and ST5 MRSA isolates were chosen for comparison using the ‘RANDBETWEEN’ function in Microsoft Excel. Antimicrobial susceptibility testing MICs of tetracycline antibiotics were determined using a microbroth dilution method. Susceptibility to other antibiotics was evaluated using agar dilution method. Tetracycline antibiotic activity was interpreted based on EUCAST 21 and Antibacterial Susceptibility Test Interpretive Criteria ( https://www.fda.gov/ ), whereas the activities of other antibiotics were interpreted in accordance with CLSI. 22 S. aureus ATCC 29213 was used as a quality control strain. Whole-genome sequencing and bioinformatics analysis Three isolates were sequenced using a MinION sequencer (Oxford Nanopore Technologies, Oxford, UK). Hybrid assembly was performed using Unicycler with Illumina and Nanopore reads. The assembled contigs were annotated using the RAST server. The resistance genes were identified using ABRicate ( https://github.com/tseemann/abricate ). The mobile genetic elements were characterized and compared with those in other species via Blastn and comparison maps were generated using Easyfig 2.1. The plasmid map was generated using BRIG. The SCC mec type was confirmed via SCC mec Finder ( https://cge.food.dtu.dk/services/SCCmecFinder/ ). The mutations of chromosomal genes were confirmed via an allele gene table in cgMLST generated in SeqSphere + software version 4.1.9 (Ridom GmbH). Phylogenetic analysis The assembled genomes ( n = 180) of ST9 S. aureus were downloaded from the GenBank database at the time of the study (May 2021). Additional raw reads of 17 strains from the research by Yu et al. 23 were retrieved from the Sequence Read Archive and assembled via Shovill ( https://github.com/tseemann/shovill ). The maximum likelihood phylogenetic tree was constructed from a core genome alignment using Panaroo 24 with the tree generated by IQ-TREE 2 25 using the bootstrap method for testing with 1000 replicates. The core genome alignment was analysed for single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) using snp-dists ( https://github.com/tseemann/snp-dists ).The phylogenetic tree was annotated using iTOL ( https://itol.embl.de/login.cgi ). Information on the public genomes is shown in Table S1 (available as Supplementary data at JAC Online). Cloning experiments A description of the strains and plasmids used in the cloning experiment is presented in Table S2 . pKSSCm and pT(63)SSCm were prepared by modifying the shuttle vector pTSSCm 26 by replacing tet (L) with the kanamycin resistance gene kanR and tetracycline resistance gene tet (63), respectively. The efflux genes tet (L), tet (63) and mepA N369Y and the ribosomal S10 protein-encoding gene rpsJ Y58D from strain N29CSA11 were amplified using a pair of primers (Table S3 ). The PCR products were ligated to the linearized vector pKSSCm via homologous recombination. The wild-type mepA and rpsJ from strain RN4220 were cloned as controls. rpsJ Y58D and rpsJ wt were cloned into pTSSCm and pT(63)SSCm via homologous recombination. Agricultural data Data on the output of livestock products and number of feeding livestock were collected from the National Bureau of Statistics of China. Data on antimicrobial consumption in animals were consulted from the official veterinary bulletin published by The Ministry of Agriculture of the People's Republic of China. 27–29 Statistics Statistical analysis of MICs was performed using GraphPad Prism v.9.0. Data were analysed using the Kruskal–Wallis test for comparison of multiple groups. Heatmaps and geographic distribution maps were generated using R. The mode picture was constructed via bioRender ( https://app.biorender.com/ ). Ethics The study received project approval from the local ethics committees (number 20150115-1) as previously reported. 13

The clinical ST9 MRSA isolates were multidrug resistance (Table 1 ). These isolates were all resistant to cefoxitin, gentamicin, erythromycin, clindamycin, ciprofloxacin and levofloxacin. Four of the ST9 isolates were resistant to moxifloxacin, whereas the others exhibited intermediate resistance. All the isolates were susceptible to linezolid, vancomycin and rifampicin. All the isolates harboured mecA , tet (L), erm (C), aadD , aph(2’’)-Ia , ant(6)-Ia and dfrG genes (Table 1 ), which confer resistance to cefoxitin, tetracycline, macrolides, aminoglycosides and trimethoprim, respectively. Two isolates carried the tetracycline resistance gene tet (63). The mutations in gyrA and parC in all the isolates accounted for the resistance to quinolones. Composite SCC mec structures in clinical ST9 strains SCC mec type XII was found in six of the nine clinical ST9 MRSA isolates while two new variants of the SCC mec type XII were identified in the other three isolates. Both new variants of the SCC mec type XII harboured three regions separated by four direct repeats and contained two ccr gene complexes. We designated these two new SCC mec variants SCC mec XII Y1 and SCC mec XII Y2 as they harboured ccrC2 and the type C2 mec gene complex identical to these of SCC mec XII. In addition, SCC mec XII Y1 harboured ccrA1 and ccrB3, while SCC mec XII Y2 harboured ccrB3 and a novel ccrA gene, tentatively named ccrA* . The total length of SCC mec XII Y1 was 63 460 bp. Two regions about 10 kbp (positions 654–11 479 and 6359–16 683) in the left region of SCC mec XII Y1 shared 93% identity with sequences found in Staphylococcus pseudintermedius HSP240 from Canis and Staphylococcus lugdunensis strain VISLISI_27 from human, respectively (Figure 1B ). The ∼20 kbp left part of SCC mec XII Y2 (positions 577–20 628) shared 74% coverage and 95% identity with the sequence found in the chromosome of Staphylococcus saprophyticus GDY8P168P isolated from pig (Figure 1C ). Further comparison revealed that the left and middle parts of SCC mec XII Y2 (positions 2075–44 897) were similar to the SCC mec type V variant identified in one porcine MRSA ST398 30 (Figure 1C ). These findings may indicate that these two composite SCC mec variants originated in livestock and evolved via cross-species recombination. All the ST9 isolates harboured the tetracycline efflux pump gene tet (L), while two strains also carried tet (63). Moreover, we identified mutations in two chromosomal genes in all the clinical ST9 isolates. An N369Y amino acid change was identified in the MATE family efflux pump gene mepA and a Y58D amino acid substitution was identified in the ribosomal S10 protein-encoding gene rpsJ . We cloned these genes to investigate the resistance mechanisms in these ST9 isolates. As shown in Table 2 , the transformant RN-pK- tet (L), strain RN4220 with a plasmid-borne copy of tet (L), exhibited increased MICs for tetracycline (512-fold), oxytetracycline (512-fold), chlortetracycline (256-fold), doxycycline (64-fold), minocycline (4-fold), tigecycline (2-fold) and eravacycline (2-fold). Moreover, the transformant RN-pK- tet (63), RN4220 with a plasmid-borne copy of tet (63), also exhibited increased resistance to tetracyclines. However, tet (63) conferred higher MICs of oxytetracycline (>512-fold), chlortetracycline (512-fold), doxycycline (256-fold) and minocycline (8-fold). These results indicated that these tetracycline efflux pump genes could confer resistance to almost all tetracyclines (except omadacycline). The transformant RN-pK- rpsJ Y58D strain RN4220 with a plasmid-borne copy of rpsJ Y58D had increased MICs of all tetracyclines by 2- or 4-fold, while RN-pK- rpsJ wt , RN4220 with a plasmid-borne copy of wild-type rpsJ , exhibited no change in the tetracyclines MICs. However, the transformant RN-pK- mepA N369Y , RN4220 with a plasmid-borne copy of mepA N369Y , presented the same level of MICs of tetracyclines as RN4220. These data suggested that rpsJ with a Y58D substitution contributed to the resistance to the third-generation tetracycline in ST9 strains. Furthermore, the combined effect of the efflux pump genes tet (L) and tet (63) and mutated rpsJ on tetracycline resistance was investigated. For transformant strains RN-pT- rpsJ Y58D and RN-pT(63)- rpsJ Y58D , the MICs of tetracyclines (apart from tetracycline and oxytetracycline) increased 2- and 4-fold, compared with those for RN-pT- rpsJ wt and RN-pT(63)- rpsJ wt (Table 2 ). These results indicated that multiple factors contributed to the tetracycline resistance in the clinical ST9 isolates. Phylogenetic analysis of livestock-associated and clinical ST9 isolates To elucidate the transmission pattern and evolution of ST9 isolates, we performed global phylogenetic analysis on ST9 S. aureus with our 9 ST9 isolates and 197 ST9 S. aureus isolates collected worldwide. Two-thirds of the ST9 strains ( n = 150) were collected from China, while 34, 20 and 1 strains were collected from America, Europe and Oceania, respectively. Most (136/206) of the isolates were collected from livestock. In addition to our clinical isolates, 53 strains were collected from infected or colonized people (Table S4 ). The global phylogenetic tree showed that the ST9 clone was classified into several clades with distinct geographic aggregation (Figure 3 ). Despite their different hosts, most ST9 isolates from China were clustered into one clade, indicating that ST9 in China had the same origin. Possible transmission events between clinical and livestock settings were highlighted in the tree, with branches in blue. The number of SNPs in these subclades ranged from 17 to 209, proving that our ST9 isolates from patients were closely related to strains of livestock origin. Evolution of tetracycline resistance in the Chinese ST9 lineage In China, ∼100 000 tons of chlortetracycline and oxytetracycline were used as growth accelerators and true treatment in veterinary per year during the period 2018–2020 (Figure S2 ). Furthermore, MRSA lineage ST9 is predominantly isolated in swine, which is the major livestock produced in China (Figure S3 ). Hence, ST9 MRSA in China was under tetracycline selection pressure. The resistance genes and mutations related to tetracycline resistance in global ST9 strains were presented in Figure 3 and Figure S4 , showing the evolution of tetracycline resistance in the Chinese ST9 strains. The multidrug resistance cluster MDR_ST9, where the tet (L) gene was located, was present in nearly all isolates (148/150) from China (Figure 3 ). Moreover, the tet (63) gene was only found in two clinical isolates in our study. mepA N369Y seemed to be present in all global ST9 isolates. rpsJ Y58D was identified in almost all the Chinese ST9 isolates, while ST9 isolates from other countries harboured rpsJwt . ## PHYLOGENETIC ANALYSIS OF LIVESTOCK-ASSOCIATED AND CLINICAL ST9 ISOLATES
To elucidate the transmission pattern and evolution of ST9 isolates, we performed global phylogenetic analysis on ST9 S. aureus with our 9 ST9 isolates and 197 ST9 S. aureus isolates collected worldwide. Two-thirds of the ST9 strains ( n = 150) were collected from China, while 34, 20 and 1 strains were collected from America, Europe and Oceania, respectively. Most (136/206) of the isolates were collected from livestock. In addition to our clinical isolates, 53 strains were collected from infected or colonized people (Table S4 ). The global phylogenetic tree showed that the ST9 clone was classified into several clades with distinct geographic aggregation (Figure 3 ). Despite their different hosts, most ST9 isolates from China were clustered into one clade, indicating that ST9 in China had the same origin. Possible transmission events between clinical and livestock settings were highlighted in the tree, with branches in blue. The number of SNPs in these subclades ranged from 17 to 209, proving that our ST9 isolates from patients were closely related to strains of livestock origin. ## DISCUSSION
The One Health approach is an integrated, unifying approach that aims to sustainably balance and optimize the health of people, animals and ecosystems, as stated recently by the Tripartite (FAO, OIE, WHO) and UNEP. Antimicrobial resistance is a health challenge linked to humans, animals and environmental factors. 1 The resistance genes cfr , optrA , mcr-1 and tet (X) were all initially identified in livestock. 20 , 32–34 These resistance genes were transmitted from livestock to the healthcare environment, leading to drug resistance and posing a severe threat. Due to the frequent use of antibiotics in the agricultural industry, livestock have become a reservoir of antimicrobial-resistant bacteria, including MRSA. 1 , 3 In this study, the transmission and evolution of multidrug-resistant MRSA lineage ST9 in livestock and the healthcare environment was investigated. To the best of our knowledge, tigecycline has remained an effective inhibitor of MRSA. 35 , 36 The recently developed antibiotic eravacycline and omadacycline both exhibited excellent in vitro activity against MRSA. 17–19 Omadacycline has been approved by the FDA for use in the treatment of acute bacterial skin and skin structure infections and community-acquired bacterial pneumonia in adults 37 and has been introduced into the Chinese market in 2022. However, our clinical ST9 isolates, collected between 2015 and 2017, all exhibited resistance to tigecycline, eravacycline and omadacycline. In other words, the resistance to third-generation tetracyclines in ST9 isolates developed before the use of antibiotics in the clinical setting in China. ST9 is the most frequently detected LA-MRSA in Asian livestock. 10 Colonization by ST9 in farm workers or butchers has been reported 38 and several cases of human infection have occurred. 39 , 40 However, a recent survey of Chinese pig farms and human healthcare isolates, which confirmed the dominant status of ST9 in pig farms, did not find ST9 isolates in hospitals due to the limited scale of the survey. 41 In our national epidemiological study, ST9 isolates were identified and their multidrug resistance phenotype was uncovered, demonstrating the presence of ST9 in the human healthcare system in China. The resistance elements carried by patient-derived ST9 were presumably generated via multiple interspecies recombination events in livestock. Meanwhile the degree of phylogenetic relatedness of patient- and swine-derived ST9 isolates in the transmission cluster was presented. Hence, we considered that the possible transmission of the multidrug-resistant ST9 lineage was emerged from livestock to human. Carrying multi-antibiotic resistance genesis one of the characteristics of Chinese ST9 isolates, among the ARGs, tet (L) and tet (63) could decrease the susceptibility to all tetracyclines except omadacycline. In addition to the acquired tetracycline-resistant genes, most Chinese ST9 isolates have the Y58D amino acid substitution in rpsJ . Mutations in rpsJ contributing to reduced susceptibility to tigecycline and eravacycline have been observed in many clinical infections. 42–44 In this study, cloning experiments confirmed that the mutation in rpsJ could reduce the susceptibility to all tetracyclines, including omadacycline. Although mutations in chromosomes cannot be transmitted through horizontal transfer, the dissemination of ST9 isolates that have acquired the rpsJ mutation poses a public health threat. Previous studies have reported that tetracyclines are among the most likely selective drivers implicated in the spread of LA-MRSA 45 and induce tet gene accumulation in LA-MRSA. For instance, the tet (L) gene is uncommon in all S. aureus lineages except LA-MRSA, 46 and the tet (63) gene was first reported in chicken-derived MRSA. 31 After reviewing the data on the consumption of veterinary antibiotics in China, we hypothesized that Chinese ST9 was under the selection pressure of tetracyclines, which promoted the accumulation of resistance genes such as tet (L) and tet (63) and mutation in rpsJ. It is of concern that the first-generation tetracyclines used in livestock could facilitate the emergence of resistance to third-generation tetracyclines before they are used in the clinic, presenting a challenge for the development of new antibiotics. Animal-to-human transmission of LA-MRSA could occur via different routes. 2 Humans in direct contact with livestock are at increased risk for colonization with LA-MRSA. 47 Recently, an increasing number of reports have described the isolation of MRSA from food production animals. 48 , 49 Thus, the food animals themselves or the handling of an infected animal products could be the part of the transmission chain. Although how the ST9 isolates in this study were introduced into human healthcare environments remains uncertain, the transmission may have occurred via the routes described previously. In conclusion, the resistance phenotypes and genomic characteristics of ST9 MRSA isolated from patients were revealed, which showed that multidrug-resistant ST9 MRSA likely originated in livestock. Furthermore, we showed that multiple mechanisms were involved in the resistance to third-generation tetracyclines in ST9 MRSA. The evolution of ST9 MRSA in livestock under the selection pressure of tetracyclines and the transmission of ST9 MRSA into the healthcare system highlight the importance of establishing control strategies with the One Health approach to reduce the burden of antibiotic resistance.
