            <ce:sections>
               <ce:para id="para10" view="all">Carbapenem-hydrolyzing class D <ce:italic>β</ce:italic>-lactamases (CHDLs) hydrolyze carbapenems at low levels but do not have activity against extended-spectrum cephalosporins (ESCs) and aztreonam, which hinder their detection at the clinical microbiology laboratory [<ce:cross-refs id="cecrefs10" refid="bib1 bib2">1,2</ce:cross-refs>]. Nowadays, CHDLs such as OXA-48-type enzymes are circulating among Mediterranean countries and are progressively disseminating to other geographical areas [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref130" refid="bib3">3</ce:cross-ref>]. OXA-163 is an OXA-48-related enzyme with a weaker carpabenem activity but a higher hydrolytic activity against ESCs that has recently been reported in Argentina and later in Egypt (22nd European Congress of Clinical Microbiology and Infectious Diseases, Abstr. P1699) [<ce:cross-refs id="cecrefs20" refid="bib4 bib5">4,5</ce:cross-refs>]. In Argentina, OXA-163 was first described in <ce:italic>Klebsiella pneumoniae</ce:italic> and <ce:italic>Enterobacter cloacae</ce:italic> [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref140" refid="bib4">4</ce:cross-ref>]. Later, we described the broad spread of multiple clones of OXA-163-producing <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> and <ce:italic>E. cloacae</ce:italic> from nine hospitals (22nd European Congress of Clinical Microbiology and Infectious Diseases, Abstr. P1699).</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para20" view="all">Here, we report the characterization of two <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> clinical strains, isolated 1 month apart from the same patient, that belonged to the same clonal type but harboured different CHDLs: <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-163</ce:inf> and a new variant of this enzyme, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-247</ce:inf>.</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para30" view="all">A 15-year-old female diagnosed with acute lymphoid leukemia at the age of 3, was hospitalized in August 2010 and was subjected to a decolonization treatment with penicillin and colistin as part of a bone marrow transplant (BMT) protocol. Four days after BMT the patient developed febrile syndrome (FS) and sinusitis, and received empirical treatment with piperacillin-tazobactam, amikacin and vancomycin. Six days after, a positive blood culture was obtained with an expanded-spectrum-<ce:italic>β</ce:italic>-lactamase (ESBL)-producing <ce:italic>E. cloacae</ce:italic> resistant to amikacin; therefore the treatment was replaced by meropenem, colistin and amphotericin B. Twelve days later a new blood culture yielded a <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> (Kpn163) suspicious for carbapenemase production. Susceptibility testing of Kpn163 (disk diffusion method and microdilution, Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute-CLSI 2012 [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref150" refid="bib6">6</ce:cross-ref>]), showed resistance to all <ce:italic>β</ce:italic>-lactams (including carbapenems), fosfomycin i.v. (European Committee on Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing-EUCAST criteria) (<ce:inter-ref id="interrefs10" xlink:href="http://www.eucast.org/clinical_breakpoints/" xlink:type="simple">http://www.eucast.org/clinical_breakpoints/</ce:inter-ref>), colistin, trimethoprim/sulphamethoxazole, chloramphenicol, tetracyclines and rifampin. Kpn163 remained susceptible to aminoglycosides, tigecycline (EUCAST) and quinolones. The treatment was replaced by gentamicin and ciprofloxacin with clinical success. A month later, a second BMT was performed and FS developed again. The patient was empirically treated with colistin and vancomycin but a week later, a second <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> (Kpn247) was recovered from the paranasal sinuses. The susceptibility profile of Kpn247 was similar to that of Kpn163 with exceptions: the aminoglycosides and quinolones became resistant, and unexpectedly, Kpn247 restored the susceptibility to ESCs and aztreonam (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref160" refid="tbl1">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>
                  <ce:float-anchor refid="tbl1"/>). The patient died a day after Kpn247 isolation due to a massive infection with cytomegalovirus and graft vs. host disease. Both isolates were referred to the National Reference Laboratory (INEI-Malbrán) for their characterization.</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para40" view="all">
                  <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">TEM</ce:inf>, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">SHV</ce:inf>, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-163</ce:inf> and <ce:italic>aac(6)-Ib-cr</ce:italic> were amplified by PCR in both Kpn163 and Kpn247 (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref170" refid="tbl1">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>). Locally prevalent <ce:italic>β</ce:italic>-lactamase (i.e. <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">KPC</ce:inf>, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">PER</ce:inf>, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">CTX-M</ce:inf> and <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">GES</ce:inf>) were PCR negative. <ce:italic>Pst</ce:italic>I-digested plasmidic DNA was ligated and amplified with outward primers to obtain the complete <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA</ce:inf> genes. Kpn163 harboured <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-163</ce:inf> and Kpn247 a new variant with two amino acid substitutions (Y219S and D220N, DBL numbering) assigned <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-247</ce:inf> (GenBank JX893517). The immediate genetic surroundings of both <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA</ce:inf> alleles were identical to that previously described [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref180" refid="bib4">4</ce:cross-ref>] (i.e. bracketed upstream by the insertion sequence IS4321 and downstream by a truncated IS4-like element; data not shown).</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para50" view="all">Both genes were transferred by biparental conjugation to <ce:italic>Escherichia coli</ce:italic> J53 (sodium azide-resistant) [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref190" refid="bib7">7</ce:cross-ref>], rendering TC163 and TC247 from Kpn163 and Kpn247, respectively. Imipenem MICs of both transconjugants were eight times higher than that of the acceptor strain but the MICs of meropenem or ertapenem remained the same (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref200" refid="tbl1">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>). A modified Hodge test (MHT) to carbapenems [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref210" refid="bib8">8</ce:cross-ref>] was positive for both transconjugants (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref220" refid="tbl1">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>).</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para60" view="all">Isoelectric focusing (IEF) [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref230" refid="bib9">9</ce:cross-ref>] gave bands at pIs 5.6 (TEM) and 7.6 (SHV) in both strains, and pI 6.7 (OXA-163-Kpn163) and pI 7.0 (OXA-247-Kpn247) (<ce:cross-ref id="cecref240" refid="tbl1">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>). The shift of the pI from 6.7 to 7.0 when comparing OXA-163 and OXA-247 agrees with the substitution of the acidic aspartic acid residue to the neutral asparagine (D220N).</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para70" view="all">S1 nuclease digestion [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref250" refid="bib10">10</ce:cross-ref>] showed that Kpn163 carried two plasmids of 70 Kb and 190 Kb while Kpn247 carried three plasmids of 70, 170 and 190 Kb in both isolates, the band of 70 Kb hybridized with the <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-163/247</ce:inf> probe. TC163 and TC247 carried a 70 Kb non-typeable plasmid (PCR-based replicon typing [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref260" refid="bib11">11</ce:cross-ref>]) that hybridized with the <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-163/247</ce:inf> probe. These results suggest that both enzymes might be carried in the same plasmid.</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para80" view="all">Outer membrane protein-encoding genes, <ce:italic>ompK35</ce:italic> and <ce:italic>ompK36,</ce:italic> were amplified by PCR [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref270" refid="bib12">12</ce:cross-ref>], sequenced and compared with <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> MGH78578 [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref280" refid="bib5">5</ce:cross-ref>]. <ce:italic>ompK</ce:italic>35 of both isolates were identical and showed five synonymous mutations and a 4-nucleotide insertion (CCAC) after codon 196, giving a deduced truncated product of 213 amino-acids. Kpn163 harboured a wild-type <ce:italic>ompK36</ce:italic> sequence while Kpn247 had a 5-nucleotide deletion at codons 44 and 45 that produced a premature termination codon with a deduced truncated product of 43 amino acids.</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para90" view="all">Pulsed-field gel electrophoresis (PFGE) studies revealed that Kpn163 and Kpn247 were clonally related, with only one band of difference in the macrorestriction pattern. Multilocus sequence typing (MLST) was performed according to the MLST Database (<ce:inter-ref id="interrefs20" xlink:href="http://www.pasteur.fr/recherche/genopole/PF8/mlst/" xlink:type="simple">http://www.pasteur.fr/recherche/genopole/PF8/mlst/</ce:inter-ref>) and both isolates belonged to ST37, also reported in a clinical isolate from Egypt expressing <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-163</ce:inf> [<ce:cross-ref id="cecref290" refid="bib5">5</ce:cross-ref>].</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="para100" view="all">In this case report we found the novel variant <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-247</ce:inf> in an immunosuppressed patient who received several antibiotic treatments and who was previously infected with a genetically related <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> harbouring <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-163</ce:inf>. Of note, to the best of our knowledge, the patient had not had any admissions to other health institutions from Buenos Aires City. In addition, this was the only report of OXA-type enzymes in the hospital involved. This epidemiological setting in conjunction with the results of PFGE, plasmid content, genetic environment and porin analysis are consistent with the hypothesis of an intrapatient emergence of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-247</ce:inf> from the previously established <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                  <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-163</ce:inf>. The implications of OXA-247 in the local epidemiology remain to be determined.</ce:para>
