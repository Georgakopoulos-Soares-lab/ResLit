                  Objectives
                  To describe the case of a patient with infection due to a KPC-producing Klebsiella pneumoniae (K. pneumoniae) isolate developing ceftazidime-avibactam resistance with restored carbapenem susceptibility during ceftazidime-avibactam therapy. To review the clinical/microbiological cure and survival rates using carbapenems in other similar case reports and case series.
               
                  Patients and methods
                  A patient with an intra-abdominal infection due to K. pneumoniae producing the KPC-48 variant (L169P-A172T) (resistant to ceftazidime/avibactam and susceptible to carbapenems) who was treated with imipenem-cilastatin in combination with tigecycline and gentamicin. The literature was reviewed in order to summarise the in vivo (clinical/microbiological cure and survival rate) use of carbapenems in this emerging scenario.
               
                  Results
                  The patient was successfully treated with the indicated regimen. In other reported cases (mostly with pneumonia) all-cause mortality was 50% and clinical cure was 62.5%. Meropenem-vaborbactam has been successful used in an additional case.
               
                  Conclusions
                  A carbapenem-based regimen of combination therapy seems to be an option for treating patients infected with K. pneumoniae resistant to ceftazidime/avibactam and susceptible to carbapenems, at least when the risk of mortality is low.

                  <ce:section-title id="sect0015">Objectives</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:simple-para id="spar0045" view="all">To describe the case of a patient with infection due to a KPC-producing <ce:italic>Klebsiella pneumoniae</ce:italic> (<ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic>) isolate developing ceftazidime-avibactam resistance with restored carbapenem susceptibility during ceftazidime-avibactam therapy. To review the clinical/microbiological cure and survival rates using carbapenems in other similar case reports and case series.</ce:simple-para>
               </ce:abstract-sec><ce:abstract-sec id="abst0015" role="materials-methods" view="all">
                  <ce:section-title id="sect0020">Patients and methods</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:simple-para id="spar0050" view="all">A patient with an intra-abdominal infection due to <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> producing the KPC-48 variant (L169P-A172T) (resistant to ceftazidime/avibactam and susceptible to carbapenems) who was treated with imipenem-cilastatin in combination with tigecycline and gentamicin. The literature was reviewed in order to summarise the in vivo (clinical/microbiological cure and survival rate) use of carbapenems in this emerging scenario.</ce:simple-para>
               </ce:abstract-sec><ce:abstract-sec id="abst0020" role="results" view="all">
                  <ce:section-title id="sect0025">Results</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:simple-para id="spar0055" view="all">The patient was successfully treated with the indicated regimen. In other reported cases (mostly with pneumonia) all-cause mortality was 50% and clinical cure was 62.5%. Meropenem-vaborbactam has been successful used in an additional case.</ce:simple-para>
               </ce:abstract-sec><ce:abstract-sec id="abst0025" role="conclusion" view="all">
                  <ce:section-title id="sect0030">Conclusions</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:simple-para id="spar0060" view="all">A carbapenem-based regimen of combination therapy seems to be an option for treating patients infected with <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> resistant to ceftazidime/avibactam and susceptible to carbapenems, at least when the risk of mortality is low.</ce:simple-para>

                  <ce:label>1</ce:label>
                  <ce:section-title id="sect0040">Introduction</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:para id="par0020" view="all">Avibactam is a non-β-lactam β-lactamase inhibitor that inactivates class A (including KPC), class C and some class D (i.e. OXA-48) carbapenemases <ce:cross-ref id="crf0160" refid="bib0005">[1]</ce:cross-ref>. Ceftazidime/avibactam has been clinically useful for the treatment of infections caused by KPC-producing <ce:italic>Klebsiella pneumoniae</ce:italic> (<ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic>) (KPC-Kp) <ce:cross-ref id="crf0165" refid="bib0005">[1]</ce:cross-ref>. Emerging cases caused by ceftazidime/avibactam-resistant strains that produce variants of KPC genes have already been reported <ce:cross-refs id="crfs0005" refid="bib0010 bib0015 bib0020 bib0025 bib0030 bib0035">[2–7]</ce:cross-refs>. Interestingly, these organisms present an extended-spectrum β-lactamase (ESBL)-like phenotype with restoration of carbapenem susceptibility, suggesting the potential therapeutic usefulness of these drugs <ce:cross-refs id="crfs0010" refid="bib0010 bib0015 bib0020 bib0025 bib0030 bib0035">[2–7]</ce:cross-refs>. However, in vitro, pressure with carbapenems can select mutants that are also resistant to these agents <ce:cross-ref id="crf0170" refid="bib0040">[8]</ce:cross-ref>.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0025" view="all">This study presents a case of a patient with infection caused by a KPC-Kp isolate developing resistance to ceftazidime/avibactam-resistant with restored carbapenem susceptibility during ceftazidime/avibactam treatment, who was treated with an imipenem-cilastatin-based regimen of combination therapy. Additionally, it reviewed the literature in order to summarise the rate of clinical/microbiological cure and survival when using carbapenems in this emerging scenario.</ce:para>
               </ce:section>
               <ce:section id="sec0010" view="all">
                  <ce:label>2</ce:label>
                  <ce:section-title id="sect0045">Case presentation and microbiological methods</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:para id="par0030" view="all">A 47-year-old male with pancreatic cancer underwent a pancreatectomy. The postoperative course is summarised in <ce:cross-ref id="crf0175" refid="tbl0005">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:float-anchor refid="tbl0005"/>. The patient developed a KPC-producing <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> intra-abdominal infection. <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> was identified using MALDI Biotyper CA System (BrukerDaltonics, Madrid, Spain). Antimicrobial susceptibility testing was performed using Sensititre panels (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Madrid, Spain) and MICs were interpreted using EUCAST breakpoints <ce:cross-ref id="crf0180" refid="bib0045">[9]</ce:cross-ref>. The organism was resistant to ertapenem, imipenem and meropenem (MIC values: &gt;2 mg/L, &gt;16 mg/L and &gt;16 mg/L, respectively) and susceptible to ceftazidime/avibactam (MIC 2 mg/L) and tigecycline (MIC 1 mg/L) and intermediate to gentamicin (MIC 4 mg/L) (<ce:cross-ref id="crf0185" refid="tbl0005">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>). An immunochromatography assay (NG-Test CARBA 5, RAL, Barcelona, Spain) detected a KPC enzyme, and the <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">KPC-3</ce:inf> was identified by PCR with specific primers (KPC-F: 5'-CCCATGAACGCCGACCTGAT-3', KPC-R: 5'-GGTTGGTAATCCATGCCGCC-3') and sequencing (<ce:cross-ref id="crf0190" refid="tbl0005">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>). Ceftazidime/avibactam (2.5 g iv over 2 h every 8 h) was indicated on day 12. On day 24, KPC-39-producing <ce:italic>K. pneumonia</ce:italic>e <ce:cross-ref id="crf0195" refid="bib0050">[10]</ce:cross-ref> (mutation A172T) resistant to ceftazidime/avibactam (MIC &gt; 16 mg/L) and carbapenems but still susceptible to tigecycline and intermediate to gentamicin was isolated. Tigecycline (iv 200 mg, loading dose, followed by 100 mg every 12 h) and gentamicin (iv 5 mg/kg/day controlling blood levels) were added to the therapeutic regimen. The antibiotic treatment was discontinued on day 32 with clinical response. However, <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> producing the KPC-48 variant (mutation L169P-A172T), which was resistant to ceftazidime-avibactam and susceptible to meropenem (MIC 1 mg/L) and imipenem (MIC 1 mg/L), was isolated from the abdominal drains (microbiological failure).</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0035" view="all">One week after the antibiotic regimen was discontinued, fever reappeared and an abdominal collection that was percutaneously drained (day 39). Ceftazidime/avibactam-resistant KPC-31-producing <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> 
                     <ce:cross-ref id="crf0200" refid="bib0050">[10]</ce:cross-ref> (mutation D179Y) was cultured among other isolates (<ce:cross-ref id="crf0205" refid="tbl0005">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>). It was susceptible to imipenem (MIC ≤ 0.5 mg/L), meropenem (MIC 2 mg/L), tigecycline (MIC 1 mg/L), and intermediate to gentamicin (MIC 4 mg/L). The patient was treated with imipenem-cilastatin (1 g iv over 2 h every 8 h) plus tigecycline (iv 200 mg loading dose, followed by 100 mg every 12 h) and gentamicin (iv 5 mg/kg/day controlling blood levels). After a 2-week course of treatment, the patient showed clinical and microbiological responses. A CT scan showed resolution of all abdominal collections. <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> isolates producing the KPC-47 (mutation A172T,T243A) were isolated from rectal swabs on days 54 and 98. On day 115 (&gt;3 months after treatment with imipenem/cilastatin), KPC-31-producing <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> still susceptible to imipenem and resistant to ceftazidime/avibactam was isolated from a rectal swab. The patient received five cycles of chemotherapy with gemcitabine between days 113 and 241 without evidence of active infection.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0040" view="all">A complete description of the antibiotic susceptibility phenotypes of the several <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> obtained from the patient is included in <ce:cross-ref id="crf0210" refid="tbl0010">Table 2</ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:float-anchor refid="tbl0010"/>.</ce:para>
               </ce:section>
               <ce:section id="sec0015" view="all">
                  <ce:label>3</ce:label>
                  <ce:section-title id="sect0050">Review of the literature</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:para id="par0045" view="all">PubMed was searched from 01 January 2009 to 31 March 2019 using the terms “<ce:italic>Klebsiella pneumoniae</ce:italic>”, “ceftazidime/avibactam” and “resistant”. Seven additional cases were found of ceftazidime/avibactam-resistant, carbapenem-susceptible KPC-producing <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> active infections (excluding colonisations) treated with meropenem <ce:cross-refs id="crfs0015" refid="bib0010 bib0015 bib0020 bib0025 bib0030 bib0035">[2–7]</ce:cross-refs>. Other cases with insufficient information to be assessed were not included. <ce:cross-ref id="crf0215" refid="tbl0015">Table 3</ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:float-anchor refid="tbl0015"/> describes the characteristics of the nine cases that emerged after receiving ceftazidime/avibactam, including the current one, and another one treated with meropenem-vaborbactam <ce:cross-ref id="crf0220" refid="bib0035">[7]</ce:cross-ref>.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0050" view="all">The most frequent infection was pneumonia (6/8 cases, 75%). Patients usually received combined treatment (7/8 cases, 87.5%). The clinical response was 62.5% (5/8 cases). The all-cause mortality was 50% (4/8 cases). The three patients who had clinical failure died. An additional patient died due to the progression of his underlying disease. One patient (Case 2) developed a breakthrough bloodstream infection. The available information was insufficient to assess the microbiological response.</ce:para>
               </ce:section>
               <ce:section id="sec0020" role="discussion" view="all">
                  <ce:label>4</ce:label>
                  <ce:section-title id="sect0055">Discussion</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:para id="par0055" view="all">The efficacy of ceftazidime/avibactam in the treatment of KPC-producing <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> infections can be limited by the development of resistance (ca. 10% of treated patients) <ce:cross-refs id="crfs0020" refid="bib0005 bib0010 bib0015 bib0020 bib0025 bib0030 bib0035 bib0055 bib0060">[1–7,11,12]</ce:cross-refs>. Several mutations in the Ω-loop of the KPC-carbapenemase translate into a decrease in the carbapenem MICs, which are frequently below the susceptibility breakpoint <ce:cross-refs id="crfs0025" refid="bib0015 bib0020 bib0025">[3–5]</ce:cross-refs>. Microbiology laboratories should be alert to this phenomenon in patients treated with ceftazidime/avibactam because the strain can phenotypically simulate an ESBL-producing <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic>. Some KPC variants are not detected by immunochromatographic assays (i.e. KPC-31) or by the commonly used carbapenem inactivation method (in this case, KPC-31, KPC-39 and KPC-48, as shown in <ce:cross-ref id="crf0225" refid="tbl0010">Table 2</ce:cross-ref>).</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0060" view="all">Previous studies have shown that carbapenems are effective for treating infections caused by ESBL-producing <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> and that carbapenemase-producing <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> can be treated with meropenem-imipenem when their MIC is ≤8 mg/L <ce:cross-ref id="crf0230" refid="bib0005">[1]</ce:cross-ref>. With these antecedents, infections caused by ceftazidime-avibactam-resistant and carbapenem-susceptible <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> could also be, theoretically, treated with carbapenems. However, in vitro, studies have shown that under selective pressure with carbapenems, the MICs of these compounds can increase, while the organism maintains its resistance to ceftazidime/avibactam <ce:cross-ref id="crf0235" refid="bib0040">[8]</ce:cross-ref>. Based on these observations, there is a risk that in vivo carbapenem therapy can fail in some patients, as already reported (<ce:cross-ref id="crf0240" refid="tbl0015">Table 3</ce:cross-ref>) <ce:cross-ref id="crf0245" refid="bib0015">[3]</ce:cross-ref>. Reviewing the reported cases, the all-cause mortality associated with carbapenem treatment is 50% (usually related to clinical failure). This mortality is much higher than that observed in ESBL-producing <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> infections and similar to that observed in KPC-producing <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> infections not treated with ceftazidime/avibactam <ce:cross-refs id="crfs0030" refid="bib0055 bib0060">[11,12]</ce:cross-refs>.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0065" view="all">The treatment of this emerging infection due to carbapenemase-susceptible and ceftazidime/avibactam-resistant <ce:italic>Klebsiella pneumoniae</ce:italic> should possibly be based on the risk of death that can be predicted by the INCREMENT-CPE score <ce:cross-refs id="crfs0035" refid="bib0065 bib0070">[13,14]</ce:cross-refs>. It seems safe to use combined regimens based on carbapenems in low-risk patients (INCREMENT score &lt;8). Carbapenems could be combined with active drugs such as aminoglycosides or fosfomycin in urinary infections or tigecycline in abdominal infections. Combination therapy (that is concordant with the results of susceptibility testing) could be recommended because the risk of in vivo selection of carbapenem resistance is still poorly known. The INCREMENT score provided evidence (although just observational) pointing towards a benefit of ‘old’ combinations in patients at a higher risk of death. It is believed that there is currently no firm evidence to indicate whether novel (and fully active) anti-KPC-Kp agents should be used alone or combined. Consequently, in patients at high risk of death (score INCREMENT ≥ 8) and infected with a strain producing a class A carbapenemase (e.g. KPC), the use of meropenem-vaborbactam, alone or combined with other active agents, can be considered for meropenem-vaborbactam susceptible strains. Imipenem-relebactam, cefiderocol, plazomicin and other active agents under clinical development could be alternatives in the future <ce:cross-ref id="crf0250" refid="bib0005">[1]</ce:cross-ref>. When new antibiotics are unavailable, the use of a polymyxin-based combination not including carbapenems can be an alternative, and when the strain is resistant to colistin, the only alternative is to use a carbapenem at high-dose with extended perfusion combined with other active antibiotics.</ce:para>
