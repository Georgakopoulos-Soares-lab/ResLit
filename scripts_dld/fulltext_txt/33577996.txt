                  Objectives
                  
                     Pseudomonas is a Gram-negative bacterial genus with numerous member species. In this study, using whole-genome sequencing, we characterized a novel Pseudomonas sp. strain TUM18999, isolated as a pathogen from a human patient.
               
                  Methods
                  The TUM18999 strain was isolated from a patient’s burn wound. Minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) were determined using the broth microdilution method. The whole-genome sequence was obtained using Miseq and MinION, and we conducted phylogenetic analysis based on single nucleotide polymorphisms of the core genome.
               
                  Results
                  Antimicrobial susceptibility testing revealed a high ceftazidime MIC (32 mg/L). Moreover, carbapenemase production was confirmed using the modified carbapenem inactivation method. We found that the complete genome of TUM18999 was 6,826,062 bp long, with 6175 coding sequences (CDS) and a DNA G+C content (non-plasmid) of 66.4 mol%. Consistent with the high similarities with the 16S rRNA sequences of P. otitidis MCC10330 (98.6%) and P. alcaligenes NBRC 14159 (99.2%), similarities (&lt;90%) were also observed with the gyrB genes of both strains. The average nucleotide identities for P. alcaligenes NBRC 14159 and P. otitidis MCC10330 were also &lt;90%. The core-genome single nucleotide polymorphism phylogenetic tree indicated that the TUM18999 strain was most closely related to P. otitidis MCC10330. In addition, the TUM18999 strain carried the novel gene, species-specific subclass B3 metallo-β-lactamase (MBL), and its similarities with P. alcaligenes metallo-β-lactamase-1 (PAM-1) and P. otitidis metallo-β-lactamase-1 (POM-1) were 90.24% and 73.14%, respectively.
               
                  Conclusion
                  We characterized the complete whole genome sequence of the novel Pseudomonas sp. TUM18999 carrying the novel gene species-specific subclass B3 MBL.

            <ce:abstract id="abs0005" view="all" class="author"><ce:abstract-sec id="abst0005" view="all">
                  <ce:section-title id="sect0010">Objectives</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:simple-para id="spar0020" view="all">
                     <ce:italic>Pseudomonas</ce:italic> is a Gram-negative bacterial genus with numerous member species. In this study, using whole-genome sequencing, we characterized a novel <ce:italic>Pseudomonas</ce:italic> sp. strain TUM18999, isolated as a pathogen from a human patient.</ce:simple-para>
               </ce:abstract-sec><ce:abstract-sec id="abst0010" role="materials-methods" view="all">
                  <ce:section-title id="sect0015">Methods</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:simple-para id="spar0025" view="all">The TUM18999 strain was isolated from a patient’s burn wound. Minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) were determined using the broth microdilution method. The whole-genome sequence was obtained using Miseq and MinION, and we conducted phylogenetic analysis based on single nucleotide polymorphisms of the core genome.</ce:simple-para>
               </ce:abstract-sec><ce:abstract-sec id="abst0015" role="results" view="all">
                  <ce:section-title id="sect0020">Results</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:simple-para id="spar0030" view="all">Antimicrobial susceptibility testing revealed a high ceftazidime MIC (32 mg/L). Moreover, carbapenemase production was confirmed using the modified carbapenem inactivation method. We found that the complete genome of TUM18999 was 6,826,062 bp long, with 6175 coding sequences (CDS) and a DNA G+C content (non-plasmid) of 66.4 mol%. Consistent with the high similarities with the 16S rRNA sequences of <ce:italic>P. otitidis</ce:italic> MCC10330 (98.6%) and <ce:italic>P. alcaligenes</ce:italic> NBRC 14159 (99.2%), similarities (&lt;90%) were also observed with the <ce:italic>gyrB</ce:italic> genes of both strains. The average nucleotide identities for <ce:italic>P. alcaligenes</ce:italic> NBRC 14159 and <ce:italic>P. otitidis</ce:italic> MCC10330 were also &lt;90%. The core-genome single nucleotide polymorphism phylogenetic tree indicated that the TUM18999 strain was most closely related to <ce:italic>P. otitidis</ce:italic> MCC10330. In addition, the TUM18999 strain carried the novel gene, species-specific subclass B3 metallo-β-lactamase (MBL), and its similarities with <ce:italic>P. alcaligenes</ce:italic> metallo-β-lactamase-1 (PAM-1) and <ce:italic>P. otitidis</ce:italic> metallo-β-lactamase-1 (POM-1) were 90.24% and 73.14%, respectively.</ce:simple-para>
               </ce:abstract-sec><ce:abstract-sec id="abst0020" role="conclusion" view="all">
                  <ce:section-title id="sect0025">Conclusion</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:simple-para id="spar0035" view="all">We characterized the complete whole genome sequence of the novel <ce:italic>Pseudomonas</ce:italic> sp. TUM18999 carrying the novel gene species-specific subclass B3 MBL.</ce:simple-para>
               </ce:abstract-sec></ce:abstract>

               <ce:para id="par0005" view="all">
                  <ce:italic>Pseudomonas</ce:italic> is a Gram-negative bacterial genus that is widely distributed in animals, plants, soil, and aquatic environments and comprises many species <ce:cross-ref id="crf0070" refid="bib0005">[1]</ce:cross-ref>. <ce:italic>Pseudomonas</ce:italic> is a clinically important pathogen as it causes a wide range of severe nosocomial infections, mainly affecting immunocompromised patients. Because of the development of various molecular species identification techniques, such as multi-locus sequence analysis and whole-genome sequencing, the number of new species registrations is increasing <ce:cross-ref id="crf0075" refid="bib0010">[2]</ce:cross-ref>. Metallo-β-lactamases (MBLs), belonging to the broad-spectrum class B β-lactamases, hydrolyse most β-lactam families, including carbapenems <ce:cross-ref id="crf0080" refid="bib0015">[3]</ce:cross-ref>. Therefore, detection of these potent β-lactamases is important to understand antimicrobial resistance and improve medical treatment.</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="par0010" view="all">Herein, we report the complete whole-genome sequence of a novel <ce:italic>Pseudomonas</ce:italic> species strain TUM18999 belonging to the <ce:italic>Pseudomonas aeruginosa</ce:italic> group, which includes <ce:italic>P. aeruginosa</ce:italic>, <ce:italic>Pseudomonas alcaligenes</ce:italic>, and <ce:italic>Pseudomonas otitidis</ce:italic>.</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="par0015" view="all">The TUM18999 strain was isolated as a pathogen from a skin swab of the burn wound of a patient admitted to the Toho University Omori Medical Center in 2018. The strain was incubated overnight on 5% sheep blood agar at 35 °C under 5% CO<ce:inf loc="post">2</ce:inf>. The colonies showed rough, pastel-green oxidase-positive cells, which were similar to those of the <ce:italic>P. aeruginosa</ce:italic> group. Primary species identification, including carbohydrate utilization, using the VITEK®2 compact system (bioMérieux, Marcy-l'Étoile, Rhône, France), tentatively identified the strain as <ce:italic>Pseudomonas fluorescens</ce:italic>. Then, the antimicrobial susceptibility of TUM18999 was assessed using the broth microdilution method according to the Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute methodology (document M07-A10). The minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) (mg/L) were determined as follows: piperacillin, 16; cephalothin, &gt;128; ceftazidime, 32; cefepime, 1; imipenem, 0.5; meropenem, 0.25; aztreonam, &lt;0.125. We attempted to detect the production of carbapenemases using the modified carbapenem inactivation method (mCIM) and sodium mercaptoacetate-combination mCIM (SMA-mCIM) <ce:cross-ref id="crf0085" refid="bib0020">[4]</ce:cross-ref>. Both mCIM and SMA-mCIM were positive, and therefore, phenotypic MBL production in TUM18999 was confirmed.</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="par0020" view="all">The whole-genome sequence (WGS) was determined using Miseq (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA) and MinION (Oxford Nanopore Technologies, Oxford, UK), and genomic DNA was extracted using the NucleoBond︎® AXG Column (TaKaRa Bio, Shiga, Japan) for MinION and NucleoSpin︎® Microbial DNA (TaKaRa Bio, Shiga, Japan) for Miseq. DNA libraries were generated using a 1D Rapid Barcoding Kit (Oxford Nanopore Technologies, Oxford, UK) and QIAseq FX DNA Library Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany) and sequenced using an R9.4 flow cell (Oxford Nanopore Technologies, Oxford, UK) and Miseq Reagent Kit v3 with 300-bp paired-ends for MinION and Miseq, respectively. A hybrid de novo assembly was conducted using Unicycler v0.4.8-beta. The contigs were polished thrice with Pilon. The assembly genome was obtained with one circular chromosome with an average sequence depth of 304x. Gene annotation was performed using the DDBJ Fast Annotation and Submission Tool (DFAST version 1.1.15; <ce:inter-ref id="intr0005" xlink:href="https://dfast.nig.ac.jp/" xlink:type="simple">https://dfast.nig.ac.jp/</ce:inter-ref>). The TUM18999 strain was found to have a 66.4 mol% G+C circular chromosome of 6,826,062 bp with 6175 CDSs and four copies of ribosomal RNA operons. Additionally, the TUM18999 strain did not carry any plasmid.</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="par0025" view="all">The partial 16S rRNA gene (1459 bp) similarity check was conducted using the EzBioCloud database (<ce:inter-ref id="intr0010" xlink:href="https://www.ezbiocloud.net/" xlink:type="simple">https://www.ezbiocloud.net/</ce:inter-ref>). We obtained high similarities of 99.2% and 98.6% with <ce:italic>P. alcaligenes</ce:italic> NBRC 14159 and <ce:italic>P. otitidis</ce:italic> MCC10330, respectively. In contrast, sequence similarity values of the <ce:italic>gyrB</ce:italic> genes with those in <ce:italic>P. alcaligenes</ce:italic> NBRC 14159 and <ce:italic>P. otitidis</ce:italic> MCC10330 were determined to be &lt;90% using pairwise alignment by EMBOSS Water (<ce:inter-ref id="intr0015" xlink:href="https://www.ebi.ac.uk/Tools/psa/emboss_water/" xlink:type="simple">https://www.ebi.ac.uk/Tools/psa/emboss_water/</ce:inter-ref>). Additionally, the average nucleotide identities against <ce:italic>P. otitidis</ce:italic> MCC10330, <ce:italic>P. alcaligenes</ce:italic> NBRC 14159, <ce:italic>Pseudomonas resinovorans</ce:italic> NBRC 106553, <ce:italic>Pseudomonas furukawaii</ce:italic> KF707, <ce:italic>Pseudomonas citronellolis</ce:italic> DSM 50332, <ce:italic>Pseudomonas oryzae</ce:italic> KCTC 32247, <ce:italic>Pseudomonas knackmussii</ce:italic> B13, and <ce:italic>P. aeruginosa</ce:italic> DSM 5071 were all &lt;90%. We also conducted phylogenetic analysis based on core genome single nucleotide polymorphisms using WGS. Briefly, WGS of <ce:italic>Pseudomonas</ce:italic> sp. type strains were aligned to the WGS of the TUM18999 strain, using a Burrows-Wheeler Aligner (BWA) with the MEM option. We constructed a core-genome alignment using SAMtools (version 1.11) mpileup and VarScan (2.3.9) mpileup2cns, and then constructed a maximum-likelihood phylogenetic tree using RAxML. This identified the TUM18999 strain as being most closely related to <ce:italic>P. otitidis</ce:italic> MCC10330 (<ce:cross-ref id="crf0090" refid="fig0005">Fig. 1</ce:cross-ref>
                  <ce:float-anchor refid="fig0005"/>). These results suggest that TUM18999 is a novel <ce:italic>Pseudomonas</ce:italic> species.</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="par0030" view="all">In this study, we identified a novel MBL gene (located at 2,672,031 to 2,672,894 with GenBank accession no. AP023189) in the TUM18999 chromosome. The amino acid similarities of MBL with PAM-1 and POM-1 were 90.24% and 73.14%, respectively, indicating that the novel MBL in the TUM18999 strain belongs to subclass B3 MBL (<ce:cross-ref id="crf0095" refid="fig0010">Fig. 2</ce:cross-ref>
                  <ce:float-anchor refid="fig0010"/>). Moreover, we compared three <ce:italic>Pseudomonas</ce:italic> chromosomes with peripheral MBL genes using EasyFig, version 2.2.2 (<ce:cross-ref id="crf0100" refid="fig0015">Fig. 3</ce:cross-ref>
                  <ce:float-anchor refid="fig0015"/>). The peripheral sequences with MBL genes were well conserved between the TUM18999 strain and closely related PAM-1-producing <ce:italic>Pseudomonas</ce:italic> sp., and transfer factors such as integrase and transposase were not found in these contigs. These results suggest that the novel MBL was a species-specific subclass B3 MBL. Because MBL is a broad-spectrum β-lactamase <ce:cross-ref id="crf0105" refid="bib0015">[3]</ce:cross-ref>, it confers major β-lactam resistance and is thus therapeutically important in human infections.</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="par0035" view="all">In conclusion, we characterized the complete WGS of the novel <ce:italic>Pseudomonas</ce:italic> sp. TUM18999 carrying the novel subclass B3 MBL gene. Further studies are needed to determine its phenotype for new species registration as well as the enzymological characteristics of its novel MBL.</ce:para>
               <ce:para id="par0040" view="all">The genome sequence of the <ce:italic>Pseudomonas</ce:italic> species strain TUM18999 was deposited at DDBJ/ENA/GenBank under BioProject number PRJDB9669 and accession number AP023189. Raw sequence data for this strain were deposited under SRA accession numbers DRR236258 (MinION) and DRR236257 (Miseq).</ce:para>
