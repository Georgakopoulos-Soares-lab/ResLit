                  Objectives
                  
                     Ralstonia pickettii has been increasingly recognised as an emerging opportunistic pathogen in hospital settings in recent years, especially in patients with prolonged hospital stay. Clinical manifestations associated with R. pickettii infection range from mild infections to severe invasive life-threatening infections. Here we report the genome sequence of a clinical R. pickettii strain (PSLESD1) carrying two novel bla
                     OXA allelic variants in China.
               
                  Methods
                  Whole-genome sequencing of strain PSLESD1 was performed using an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform. Antimicrobial resistance genes were identified using the BacWGSTdb server. The phylogenetic relationship betweenR. pickettii PSLESD1 and a total of 17 R. pickettii strains deposited in the NCBI GenBank database was analysed using NJ (neighbour joining)/UPGMA (unweighted pair-group method with arithmetic mean) phylogeny (MAFFT v.7) based on core genome single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) data.
               
                  Results
                  The draft genome sequence of R. pickettii strain PSLESD1 consists of 25 contigs comprising 5 267 333 bp. Three antimicrobial resistance genes were identified in the genome, including bla
                     OXA-898, bla
                     OXA-899 and sul2. Strain PSLESD1 was resistant to aminoglycosides and carbapenems including meropenem. Phylogenetic analysis showed that all strains retrieved from the NCBI GenBank database were epidemiologically unrelated. The closest relative of strain PSLESD1 was H2Cu2, which differed by 2908 SNPs.
               
                  Conclusion
                  In summary, we report the first genome sequence of a clinicalR. pickettii strain harbouring two novel class D β-lactamase genes (bla
                     OXA-898 and bla
                     OXA-899) recovered from a bloodstream infection in China. These data may help to understand the genomic features and antimicrobial resistance mechanisms of this bacterial pathogen.

               <ce:section id="sec0005" role="introduction" view="all">
                  <ce:label>1</ce:label>
                  <ce:section-title id="sect0035">Introduction</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:para id="par0005" view="all">
                     <ce:italic>Ralstonia pickettii</ce:italic> is a non-fermentative, Gram-negative, aerobic bacillus that often causes contamination of pharmaceutical solutions and results in nosocomial infections and outbreaks. It can be isolated from a variety of clinical samples, including sputum, blood, urine, nasopharyngeal swab and cerebrospinal fluid. Clinical manifestations associated with <ce:italic>R. pickettii</ce:italic> infection range from mild infections to severe and sometimes life-threatening invasive infections. <ce:italic>Ralstonia pickettii</ce:italic> has been increasingly recognised as an emerging opportunistic pathogen in hospital settings in recent years, especially in patients with prolonged hospital stay <ce:cross-refs id="crfs0005" refid="bib0005 bib0010">[1,2]</ce:cross-refs>. Class D β-lactamases, also known as oxacillinases or OXA-type β-lactamases (OXAs), can contribute to a high level of carbapenem resistance in Gram-negative bacilli <ce:cross-refs id="crfs0010" refid="bib0015 bib0020">[3,4]</ce:cross-refs>. Here we report the first genome sequence of a clinical <ce:italic>R. pickettii</ce:italic> strain isolated from a bloodstream infection carrying two novel <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">OXA</ce:inf> genes in China. Genotypic and phenotypic characterisation of the strain was further analysed.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0010" view="all">An 86-year-old female patient was hospitalised in the intensive care unit of a tertiary hospital in Zhejiang Province in August 2019. She was diagnosed with chronic renal failure and aspiration pneumonia. The patient also had other co-morbidities including hypertension and hyperlipidaemia. Sputum cultures recovered several bacterial isolates, i.e. carbapenem-resistant <ce:italic>Pseudomonas aeruginosa</ce:italic> and carbapenem-resistant <ce:italic>Acinetobacter baumannii</ce:italic>. During her hospitalisation, the patient received multiple broad-spectrum antibiotics including ceftazidime, aztreonam, fosfomycin, tigecycline and colistin. In September 2019, a <ce:italic>R. pickettii</ce:italic> strain (PSLESD1) was collected from a blood sample of the patient.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0015" view="all">The isolate was preliminarily identified using the VITEK® MS system (bioMérieux, Marcy-l’Étoile, France). Antimicrobial susceptibility testing was performed using a VITEK®2 system (bioMérieux) with Gram-negative antimicrobial susceptibility testing card (AST-GN16) and by Etest following the guidelines of the Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI). Whole-genome sequencing) of the strain was performed using an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform (Illumina Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). The generated short reads were de novo assembled into contigs using SPAdes v.3.13.0. The whole genome sequence was automatically annotated by the NCBI Prokaryotic Genome Annotation Pipeline (PGAP). Antimicrobial resistance genes were identified using the BacWGSTdb server <ce:cross-refs id="crfs0015" refid="bib0025 bib0030">[5,6]</ce:cross-refs>.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0020" view="all">
                     <ce:italic>Ralstonia pickettii</ce:italic> strain PSLESD1 was further confirmed by calculating the average nucleotide identity (ANI), which compares the submitted genome sequence against the genomes of type strains and proxytype strains that are already in GenBank. The ANI results revealed that the genome of <ce:italic>R. pickettii</ce:italic> strain PSLESD1 is &gt;99.9% identical to the type strain <ce:italic>R. pickettii</ce:italic> ATCC 27511 <ce:cross-ref id="crf0035" refid="bib0035">[7]</ce:cross-ref>. The draft genome sequence of <ce:italic>R. pickettii</ce:italic> strain PSLESD1 consists of 25 contigs comprising 5 267 333 bp, and the overall GC content amounts to 63.8%. In total, 49 tRNA genes, 3 rRNA operons and 4774 protein-coding sequences were identified by the PGAP server. Three antimicrobial resistance genes were identified in the genome, including the β-lactam resistance genes <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-899</ce:inf> (located in contig 00001: 353 171–353 998) and <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-898</ce:inf> (located in contig 00001: 543 716–544 531) and the sulfonamide resistance gene <ce:italic>sul2</ce:italic>. The <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-898</ce:inf> gene is a novel variant allele of the OXA-60 family class D β-lactamases and the <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-899</ce:inf> gene is a novel variant allele of the OXA-22 family class D β-lactamases. They are first reported in this study and have been submitted to GenBank with accession nos. <ce:bold>
                        <ce:underline>MN968728.1 and</ce:underline>
                        <ce:underline>MN968729.1, respectively. The minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs)</ce:underline>
                     </ce:bold> of antimicrobial agents tested against <ce:italic>R. pickettii</ce:italic> PSLESD1 are presented in Supplementary Table S1. Strain PSLESD1 was resistant to aminoglycosides and carbapenems including meropenem, but remained susceptible to third-generation cephalosporins, piperacillin/tazobactam, cefepime (fourth-generation cephalosporin) and fluoroquinolones.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0025" view="all">The phylogenetic relationship between PSLESD1 and a total of 17 <ce:italic>R. pickettii</ce:italic> strains deposited in the NCBI GenBank database was analysed using NJ (neighbour joining)/UPGMA (unweighted pair-group method with arithmetic mean) phylogeny (MAFFT v.7) based on core genome single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) analysis. The phylogenetic tree is presented in <ce:cross-ref id="crf0040" refid="fig0005">Fig. 1</ce:cross-ref>
                     <ce:float-anchor refid="fig0005"/>. Seven strains were isolated from the USA, three strains from Japan, two strains each from Portugal and UK, and one strain each from China, Canada and Australia. Four strains were isolated from humans, eleven strains from the environment and two strains with unknown isolation source. One strain (namely 52) was isolated from a water sample in Zhejiang Province in 2016, which differed by 9299 SNPs compared to strain PSLESD1. Phylogenetic analysis showed that these strains were epidemiologically unrelated. The closest relative of strain PSLESD1 was H2Cu2, which differed by 2908 SNPs. Most of these strains produce OXA-22 family and OXA-60 family class D β-lactamases. It appears that chromosomally-encoded OXA-22 family and OXA-60 family class D β-lactamases are commonly identified in <ce:italic>R. pickettii</ce:italic>. Among them, OXA-60 family class D β-lactamase is an inducible oxacillinase with carbapenem-hydrolysing ability, making clinical treatment difficult <ce:cross-ref id="crf0045" refid="bib0040">[8]</ce:cross-ref>.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0030" view="all">In summary, we report the first genome sequence of a clinical <ce:italic>R. pickettii</ce:italic> strain harbouring two novel class D β-lactamases (<ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-898</ce:inf> and <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">OXA-899</ce:inf>) recovered from a bloodstream infection in China. These data may help to understand the genomic features and antimicrobial resistance mechanisms of this bacterial pathogen. The genome sequence of <ce:italic>R. pickettii</ce:italic> PSLESD1 can be used as a reference sequence for comparative genomic analysis in the future.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="par0035" view="all">This Whole Genome Shotgun project has been deposited at DDBJ/EMBL/GenBank under the accession no. <ce:bold>
                        <ce:underline>WJYN00000000.</ce:underline>
                     </ce:bold> The version described in this paper is the first version (<ce:bold>
                        <ce:underline>WJYN00000000.1)</ce:underline>
                     </ce:bold>.</ce:para>
