                  Objectives
                  To evaluate the molecular mechanisms of ceftazidime/avibactam (CAZ/AVI) resistance in six Klebsiella pneumoniae strains that co-produce K. pneumoniae carbapenemase (KPC)-2 and a novel variant of CMY cephalosporinase in a Chinese hospital.
               
                  Methods
                  Antimicrobial susceptibility was determined by broth microdilution. Whole-genome sequencing (WGS) was performed to investigate potential resistance determinants. Plasmid conjugation, electroporation, S1 nuclease pulsed-field gel electrophoresis (S1-PFGE) hybridization and cloning experiment were carried out to investigate the resistance plasmids and genes.
               
                  Results
                  A high level of CAZ/AVI resistance was observed in six KPC-Kp strains (MIC 128 mg/L). Five strains were isolated in 2015 and one in 2016, before the approval of CAZ/AVI in China. Sequence analysis indicated that all the strains belonged to sequence type (ST) 11 and uniformly carried a novel CMY AmpC β-lactamase gene, designated bla
                     CMY-172. When compared with CMY-2, CMY-172 has a deletion of three consecutive amino acids (K290, V291 and A292) in the R2-loop region and a non-synonymous amino acid substitution at position 346 (N346I). The bla
                     CMY-172-bearing plasmid, pKPCZA02_4, was 93.3 Kb, IncI1-I type, and conjugative; bla
                     CMY-172 was located in an IS1294-mediated transposon. Plasmid conjugation and DNA fragment cloning proved that bla
                     CMY-172 was responsible for CAZ/AVI resistance.
               
                  Conclusions
                  Our study identified conjugative plasmid-mediated bla
                     CMY-172 as a new mechanism for CAZ/AVI resistance in clinical KPC-Kp strains. Careful monitoring of CAZ/AVI susceptibility is imperative for preventing the spread of the resistance gene.

            <ce:abstract id="abs0010" xml:lang="en" view="all" class="author"><ce:abstract-sec id="abssec0010" view="all">
                  <ce:section-title id="sectitle0015">Objectives</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:simple-para id="abspara0010" view="all">To evaluate the molecular mechanisms of ceftazidime/avibactam (CAZ/AVI) resistance in six <ce:italic>Klebsiella pneumoniae</ce:italic> strains that co-produce <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> carbapenemase (KPC)-2 and a novel variant of CMY cephalosporinase in a Chinese hospital.</ce:simple-para>
               </ce:abstract-sec><ce:abstract-sec id="abssec0015" view="all">
                  <ce:section-title id="sectitle0020">Methods</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:simple-para id="abspara0015" view="all">Antimicrobial susceptibility was determined by broth microdilution. Whole-genome sequencing (WGS) was performed to investigate potential resistance determinants. Plasmid conjugation, electroporation, S1 nuclease pulsed-field gel electrophoresis (S1-PFGE) hybridization and cloning experiment were carried out to investigate the resistance plasmids and genes.</ce:simple-para>
               </ce:abstract-sec><ce:abstract-sec id="abssec0020" role="results" view="all">
                  <ce:section-title id="sectitle0025">Results</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:simple-para id="abspara0020" view="all">A high level of CAZ/AVI resistance was observed in six KPC-Kp strains (MIC 128 mg/L). Five strains were isolated in 2015 and one in 2016, before the approval of CAZ/AVI in China. Sequence analysis indicated that all the strains belonged to sequence type (ST) 11 and uniformly carried a novel CMY AmpC β-lactamase gene, designated <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf>. When compared with CMY-2, CMY-172 has a deletion of three consecutive amino acids (K290, V291 and A292) in the R2-loop region and a non-synonymous amino acid substitution at position 346 (N<ce:sup loc="post">346</ce:sup>I). The <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf>-bearing plasmid, pKPCZA02_4, was 93.3 Kb, IncI1-I type, and conjugative; <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf> was located in an IS<ce:italic>1294</ce:italic>-mediated transposon. Plasmid conjugation and DNA fragment cloning proved that <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf> was responsible for CAZ/AVI resistance.</ce:simple-para>
               </ce:abstract-sec><ce:abstract-sec id="abssec0025" role="conclusion" view="all">
                  <ce:section-title id="sectitle0030">Conclusions</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:simple-para id="abspara0025" view="all">Our study identified conjugative plasmid-mediated <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf> as a new mechanism for CAZ/AVI resistance in clinical KPC-Kp strains. Careful monitoring of CAZ/AVI susceptibility is imperative for preventing the spread of the resistance gene.</ce:simple-para>
               </ce:abstract-sec></ce:abstract>

               <ce:section id="sec1" role="introduction" view="all">
                  <ce:section-title id="sectitle0040">Introduction</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:para id="p0010" view="all">
                     <ce:italic>Klebsiella pneumoniae</ce:italic> carbapenemase-producing <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> (KPC-Kp) continues to be a global health concern [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0055" refid="bib1">1</ce:cross-ref>]. Due to limited effective therapeutic options, serious infections caused by KPC-Kp are associated with high mortality [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0060" refid="bib2">2</ce:cross-ref>].</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="p0015" view="all">Avibactam is a promising novel β-lactamase inhibitor which is active against most serine β-lactamases, including class A extended-spectrum β-lactamases (ESBLs), class C cephalosporinases, and KPCs [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0065" refid="bib3">3</ce:cross-ref>]. The combination agent, ceftazidime/avibactam (CAZ/AVI), decreased the all-cause hospital mortality rate of KPC-producing carbapenem-resistant Enterobacteriaceae (CRE) [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0070" refid="bib4">4</ce:cross-ref>,<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0075" refid="bib5">5</ce:cross-ref>]. However, resistance to CAZ/AVI has been reported among KPC-Kp strains. The resistance mechanism is commonly linked to variant mutations of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">KPC-2</ce:inf>/<ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">KPC-3</ce:inf> [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0080" refid="bib6">6</ce:cross-ref>,<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0085" refid="bib7">7</ce:cross-ref>]. Additionally, increased expression of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">KPC</ce:inf> with alterations of Ompk35/36 porins [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0090" refid="bib8">8</ce:cross-ref>] and the newly emerged Vietnamese extended-spectrum β-lactamase (VEB)-25 [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0095" refid="bib9">9</ce:cross-ref>] have also been proved to cause CAZ/AVI resistance in KPC-Kp.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="p0020" view="all">CMY is a plasmid-mediated class C cephalosporinase conferring resistance to the expanded-spectrum oxyimino-cephalosporins, and also carbapenems, with the loss of outer-membrane porin proteins [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0100" refid="bib10">10</ce:cross-ref>]. <ce:italic>In vitro</ce:italic> selection of aztreonam/avibactam resistance due to mutations of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">CMY</ce:inf> has been reported in an NDM-1-, OXA-48-, CTX-M-15- and CMY-16-positive <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> strain [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0105" refid="bib11">11</ce:cross-ref>]. Therefore, it is worthwhile to investigate whether a similar resistance mechanism exists in the clinical setting.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="p0025" view="all">In the present study, we described a novel variant of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">CMY</ce:inf>, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf>, that was responsible for a high level of resistance to CAZ/AVI in six clinical KPC-Kp strains in China. The genetic environment and transferable element of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf> were identified.</ce:para>
               </ce:section>
               <ce:section id="sec2" role="materials-methods" view="all">
                  <ce:section-title id="sectitle0045">Materials and methods</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:section id="sec2.1" view="all">
                     <ce:section-title id="sectitle0050">Bacterial isolates</ce:section-title>
                     <ce:para id="p0030" view="all">Carbapenem-resistant <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> (CRKP) strains were selected from the archived bacterial collection of the First Affiliated Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine. All the isolates were identified using matrix-assisted laser desorption ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry (MALDI-TOF MS) (Bruker, Germany). The clinical data were extracted from the hospital's electronic records. This study was approved by the ethics committee of the First Affiliated Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine.</ce:para>
                  </ce:section>
                  <ce:section id="sec2.2" view="all">
                     <ce:section-title id="sectitle0055">Antimicrobial susceptibility testing</ce:section-title>
                     <ce:para id="p0035" view="all">Antimicrobial susceptibility testing was performed by broth microdilution and interpreted according to the Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) 2020 standard [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0110" refid="bib12">12</ce:cross-ref>].</ce:para>
                  </ce:section>
                  <ce:section id="sec2.3" view="all">
                     <ce:section-title id="sectitle0060">Plasmid transformation, conjugation and southern hybridization</ce:section-title>
                     <ce:para id="p0040" view="all">
                        <ce:italic>Escherichia coli</ce:italic> DH5α and sodium azide-resistant <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> J53 were used as recipient cells for the plasmid electroporation experiment and the conjugation assay, respectively. S1 nuclease pulsed-field gel electrophoresis (PFGE) and Southern hybridization were carried out to indicate the genetic location of resistance genes [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0115" refid="bib13">13</ce:cross-ref>].</ce:para>
                  </ce:section>
                  <ce:section id="sec2.4" view="all">
                     <ce:section-title id="sectitle0065">Gene cloning</ce:section-title>
                     <ce:para id="p0045" view="all">The 1418-bp fragment surrounding <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf> and containing the upstream promoter sequence of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf> was amplified from the KPCZA02 strain using primers CMY-F (5′-AGCAAATGGGTCGCGGATCCGAATTCCAGAGTTTATTAAGTATCATT-3′) and CMY-R (5′-TGGTGCTCGAGTGCGGCCGCAAGCTTTTATTGCAGCTTTTCAAGAATG-3′). After purification, the product was digested by EcoR-I and Hind-III endonuclease, and then cloned into plasmid vector pET28a (Novagen, USA) using NovoRec® plus One step PCR Cloning Kit (Novoprotein, China), followed by electroporation into <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> DH5α. The recombinant clones were selected in Mueller–Hinton (MH) agar plates containing 10 mg/L CAZ and confirmed by PCR and sequencing.</ce:para>
                  </ce:section>
                  <ce:section id="sec2.5" view="all">
                     <ce:section-title id="sectitle0070">Whole-genome sequencing (WGS) and bioinformatic analysis</ce:section-title>
                     <ce:para id="p0050" view="all">The genomic DNA was extracted by the QIAamp DNA Midi Kit (Qiagen, Germany) and sequenced on the Illumina Hi-seq 2000 platform (Illumina, USA). In order to explore the plasmid structure, KPCZA02 was analysed by PacBio RS II sequencing. The reads were assembled by Celera Assembler v. 8.3. The resistance genes were predicted by Resistance Gene Identifier (RGI) 5.1.1 in the Comprehensive Antibiotic Resistance Database (CARD) platform (<ce:inter-ref id="intref0010" xlink:href="https://card.mcmaster.ca/" xlink:type="simple">https://card.mcmaster.ca/</ce:inter-ref>). The multilocus sequence typing (MLST), plasmid incompatibility typing, and phylogenetic relationships of the strains were predicted by MLST 2.0, PlasmidFinder 2.1 and CSI Phylogeny (Call SNPs &amp; Infer Phylogeny) 1.4 respectively, available in the Center for Genome Epidemiology (<ce:inter-ref id="intref0015" xlink:href="https://cge.cbs.dtu.dk/services/" xlink:type="simple">https://cge.cbs.dtu.dk/services/</ce:inter-ref>). In order to determine the capsular type, the <ce:italic>wzi</ce:italic> allele was analysed using the Kaptive web tool (<ce:inter-ref id="intref0020" xlink:href="https://kaptive-web.erc.monash.edu/" xlink:type="simple">https://kaptive-web.erc.monash.edu/</ce:inter-ref>). Insertion sequences (ISs) were identified by ISFinder (<ce:inter-ref id="intref0025" xlink:href="https://www-is.biotoul.fr/" xlink:type="simple">https://www-is.biotoul.fr/</ce:inter-ref>). Comparisons of plasmid sequences were performed by the BLAST Ring Image Generator (BRIG) [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0120" refid="bib14">14</ce:cross-ref>]. The genetic environment of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf> was generated by Easyfig 2.2 [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0125" refid="bib15">15</ce:cross-ref>].</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="p0055" view="all">The amino acid numbering of CMY was determined using the SANC (structural alignment-based numbering of class C β-lactamases) scheme [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0130" refid="bib16">16</ce:cross-ref>].</ce:para>
                  </ce:section>
                  <ce:section id="sec2.6" view="all">
                     <ce:section-title id="sectitle0075">Nucleotide sequence accession numbers</ce:section-title>
                     <ce:para id="p0060" view="all">The complete sequences of the chromosome and four plasmids of KPCZA02 and the nucleotide sequence of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf> have been deposited in the GenBank database under accession numbers from CP058226 to CP058230 and MT752965, respectively.</ce:para>
                  </ce:section>

               <ce:section id="sec3" role="results" view="all">
                  <ce:section-title id="sectitle0080">Results</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:section id="sec3.1" view="all">
                     <ce:section-title id="sectitle0085">Strains and antibiotic susceptibility</ce:section-title>
                     <ce:para id="p0065" view="all">Ceftazidime/avibactam was approved by the National Medical Products Administration and marketed in China in May 2019. Before this, regular CAZ/AVI susceptibility testing was not implemented in our hospital. This was a retrospective analysis of deposited clinical <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> isolates.</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="p0070" view="all">From July 2014 to August 2018, 662 clinical CRKPs were collected from the hospital, of which 11 were resistant to CAZ/AVI (named KPCZA01 to KPCZA11) and negative for known metallo-β-lactamase (MBL) genes. WGS identified a novel <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">CMY</ce:inf> in six strains (KPCZA01 to KPCZA06). Further microbiological experiments and clinical retrospective analyses were performed for the six strains.</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="p0075" view="all">Besides displaying a high CAZ/AVI MIC (128/4 mg/L), all of the six strains showed resistance to carbapenem, aminoglycoside and fluoroquinolone (<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0135" refid="tbl1">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>
                        <ce:float-anchor refid="tbl1"/>).</ce:para>
                  </ce:section>
                  <ce:section id="sec3.2" view="all">
                     <ce:section-title id="sectitle0090">Epidemiological investigation and clinical characterization</ce:section-title>
                     <ce:para id="p0080" view="all">All the six CAZ/AVI-resistant KPC-Kp strains (KPCZA01 to KPCZA06) were isolated in the period 2nd June 2015 to 13th July 2015, with the exception of KPCZA06 (Patient 6) isolated on 2nd February 2016. Patient (Pt) 6 had stayed in the surgical intensive care unit from 4th to 8th June 2015 after a Whipple procedure, and was discharged on 30th June 2015. He was admitted to the Department of Radiotherapy due to recurrence of pancreatic cancer on 2nd December 2015. Since no other CAZ/AVI-resistant KPC-Kp strain was identified between December 2015 and February 2016, a long-term colonization by the strain in Pt 6 from June 2015 was suspected.</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="p0085" view="all">All the patients were operated before the isolation of the strains with the exception of Pt 3. Four medical wards were involved, including the kidney transplantation intensive care unit (Pt 1), the surgical intensive care unit (Pt 2, Pt 4 and Pt 6), the medical intensive care unit (Pt 3) and the hepatopancreatobiliary intensive care unit (Pt 5). Pt 2, Pt 4 and Pt 6 had overlapping hospitalized times in the surgical intensive care unit. None of the patients had previously received CAZ/AVI treatment.</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="p0090" view="all">The timeline of the six cases is shown in <ce:cross-ref id="crosref0140" refid="appsec1">Supplementary Material Fig. S1</ce:cross-ref>, and the clinical characterization is summarized in <ce:cross-ref id="crosref0145" refid="appsec1">Supplementary Material Table S1</ce:cross-ref>.</ce:para>
                  </ce:section>
                  <ce:section id="sec3.3" view="all">
                     <ce:section-title id="sectitle0095">WGS and analysis</ce:section-title>
                     <ce:para id="p0095" view="all">All the six strains (KPCZA01–KPCZA06) belonged to ST11 and capsular type KL47 (<ce:italic>wzi</ce:italic>-209). The numbers of single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) among the six strains were 4–19, suggesting probable nosocomial dissemination of the same clone. Besides carriage of common serine β-lactamase genes, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">KPC-2</ce:inf>, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">TEM-1B</ce:inf>, and <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">CTX-M-65</ce:inf>, they were uniformly found to harbour a novel variant of the class C β-lactamase CMY gene, designated <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf> (GenBank accession number: MT752965). When compared with CMY-2, CMY-172 showed a deletion of three consecutive amino acids at positions 290 (Lys), 291 (Val) and 292 (Ala), as well as a non-synonymous amino acid substitution at position 346 (Asn<ce:sup loc="post">346</ce:sup>Ile) (<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0150" refid="appsec1">Supplementary Material Fig. S2</ce:cross-ref>).</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="p0100" view="all">A 16S rRNA methylase gene, <ce:italic>rmtB</ce:italic>, that can mediate high-level resistance to various aminoglycosides, was present in all the six strains.</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="p0105" view="all">In all six strains, OmpK35 was truncated (AA89-STOP) due to a frameshift mutation.</ce:para>
                  </ce:section>
                  <ce:section id="sec3.4" view="all">
                     <ce:section-title id="sectitle0100">Transferability of CAZ/AVI resistance and the role of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf>
                     </ce:section-title>
                     <ce:para id="p0110" view="all">S1-PFGE and Southern hybridization confirmed that <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf> and <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">KPC-2</ce:inf> were carried by two distinct plasmids (<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0155" refid="appsec1">Supplementary Material Fig. S3</ce:cross-ref>). The CAZ/AVI MIC of the <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> DH5α recipient increased from ≤0.25 mg/L to 16 mg/L (at least &gt;64-fold) after electroporation of the <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf>-harbouring plasmid; however, it was not affected by the <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">KPC-2</ce:inf>-harbouring plasmid (<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0160" refid="tbl1">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>).</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="p0115" view="all">Conjugation experiments showed that KPCZA02 was able to transfer its CAZ/AVI resistance to sodium azide-resistant <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> J53, the MIC of CAZ/AVI increasing from ≤0.25 mg/L to 32 mg/L. The presence of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf> in the transconjugants was confirmed by PCR and sequencing. It suggested that <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf> was carried by a conjugative plasmid.</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="p0120" view="all">Furthermore, cloning of the 1418-bp <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf>-surrounding fragment also indicated that the recombinant plasmid in <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> DH5α established resistance to CAZ/AVI (from ≤0.25 mg/L to 32 mg/L) (<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0165" refid="tbl1">Table 1</ce:cross-ref>).</ce:para>
                  </ce:section>
                  <ce:section id="sec3.5" view="all">
                     <ce:section-title id="sectitle0105">Structure of the <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf>-bearing plasmid</ce:section-title>
                     <ce:para id="p0125" view="all">Long-read sequencing revealed that the genome of KPCZA02 consisted of a 5 380 741-bp chromosome and four circular plasmids designated pKPCZA02_1 (10 059 bp), pKPCZA02_2 (149 200 bp), pKPCZA02_3 (5595 bp) and pKPCZA02_4 (93 354 bp), respectively (<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0170" refid="appsec1">Supplementary Material Table S2</ce:cross-ref>).</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="p0130" view="all">
                        <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf> was the only antibiotic resistant gene of pKPCZA02_4. The plasmid belonged to incompatibility group I1 (IncI1) and contained an intact conjugal transfer region (<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0175" refid="fig1">Fig. 1</ce:cross-ref>
                        <ce:float-anchor refid="fig1"/>A). A unique genetic environment of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf> was revealed with a gene arrangement of IS<ce:italic>1294</ce:italic>-ΔIS<ce:italic>Ecp1</ce:italic>-<ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf>-<ce:italic>blc</ce:italic>-<ce:italic>sugE</ce:italic>-<ce:italic>ecnR</ce:italic>. The fragment was entirely inserted into a colicin gene <ce:italic>cia</ce:italic> and flanked by sequences CTTG and GTTC, which is the typical target for insertion for the IS<ce:italic>91</ce:italic> family [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0180" refid="bib17">17</ce:cross-ref>,<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0185" refid="bib18">18</ce:cross-ref>] (<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0190" refid="fig1">Fig. 1</ce:cross-ref>B).</ce:para>
                     <ce:para id="p0135" view="all">BLASTN comparison showed that the sequence of pKPCZA02_4 was very similar (95% nucleotide identities) to that of pGM351-2 (GenBank accession No. CP038417), a plasmid of E. coli O157:H7 strain 3-5-1 isolated in the USA from cattle faeces, except for the intact cia gene without the insertion of IS1294-mediated blaCMY-172-surrounding fragment. pS10584 (GenBank accession No. KX058576), a <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-2</ce:inf>-harbouring plasmid of <ce:italic>Salmonella enterica</ce:italic> strain SJTUF10584 from a food sample in China [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0195" refid="bib19">19</ce:cross-ref>], was almost identical to pKPCZA02_4 (99% nucleotide identities), including the same IS<ce:italic>1294</ce:italic>-mediated <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                        <ce:inf loc="post">CMY</ce:inf>-surrounding fragment and the same insertion site in the <ce:italic>cia</ce:italic> gene (<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0200" refid="fig1">Fig. 1</ce:cross-ref>B).</ce:para>
                  </ce:section>
               </ce:section>
               <ce:section id="sec4" role="discussion" view="all">
                  <ce:section-title id="sectitle0110">Discussion</ce:section-title>
                  <ce:para id="p0140" view="all">The present study clearly indicated that a novel variant of the class C cephalosporinase CMY gene, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf>, was primarily associated with a high level of resistance to CAZ/AVI in a KPC-producing strain of <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic>.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="p0145" view="all">CMY-2 is the most common plasmid-borne AmpC β-lactamase of the Enterobacteriaceae worldwide [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0205" refid="bib10">10</ce:cross-ref>]. When compared with CMY-2, CMY-172 contained a Lys–Val–Ala deletion (K290_V291_A292del) in the R2 loop. Deletion of some residues in the R2 loop widens the R2 active site and contributes to the extended-spectrum activity of AmpC [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0210" refid="bib20">20</ce:cross-ref>]. Additionally, a recent study reported that structural changes of AmpC caused by a two-residue deletion (A294_P295del) in the R2 loop could result in increased hydrolysis of ceftazidime, and consequently decreased CAZ/AVI susceptibility, in a clinical strain of <ce:italic>Enterobacter cloacae</ce:italic> Ent385 [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0215" refid="bib21">21</ce:cross-ref>]. Therefore, it is hypothesized that a similar effect exists in CMY-172.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="p0150" view="all">However, in comparison to the moderate increase in CAZ/AVI MIC mediated by deletion mutation in the R2 loop alone (<ce:italic>E. cloacae</ce:italic> strain Ent385, 8 mg/L) [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0220" refid="bib21">21</ce:cross-ref>], a dramatically elevated CAZ/AVI MIC was found in our CMY-172-producing <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> strains (128 mg/L). Besides the deletion in the R2 loop, a non-synonymous amino acid substitution at position 346 (Asn<ce:sup loc="post">346</ce:sup>Ile) was observed in CMY-172. In the model of avibactam bound to <ce:italic>Pseudomonas aeruginosa</ce:italic> AmpC, Asn346 is one of the binding pocket residues [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0225" refid="bib22">22</ce:cross-ref>]. The sulphate group of avibactam is stabilized by the polar group of Asn346 [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0230" refid="bib22">22</ce:cross-ref>]. The substitution of Asn346 would result in a steric clash with the sulphate group of avibactam, thus influencing the binding affinity and leading to CAZ/AVI resistance [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0235" refid="bib23">23</ce:cross-ref>]. In another study, an <ce:italic>in vitro</ce:italic> selected mutation of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-16</ce:inf> (Asn<ce:sup loc="post">346</ce:sup>His) was associated with aztreonam/avibactam resistance [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0240" refid="bib11">11</ce:cross-ref>]. Overall, it is believed the amino acid deletion (L290_V291_A292del) in the R2 loop together with Asn<ce:sup loc="post">346</ce:sup>Ile mutation in <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf> lead to the high-level resistance to CAZ/AVI. Further biochemical and structural features of this novel variant of CMY remain to be characterized.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="p0155" view="all">The development of resistance-conferring <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">KPC</ce:inf> mutations in <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> during CAZ/AVI treatment has been well studied [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0245" refid="bib24">24</ce:cross-ref>,<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0250" refid="bib25">25</ce:cross-ref>]. Successful selection of aztreonam/avibactam-resistant <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> due to <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-16</ce:inf> mutation has also been reported <ce:italic>in vitro</ce:italic> [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0255" refid="bib11">11</ce:cross-ref>]. However, in this study, these KPC-2 and CMY-172 co-producing strains emerged independently of the usage of CAZ/AVI, suggesting a spontaneous mutation of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">CMY</ce:inf>. The sequence of pKPCZA02_4 in this study was very similar to that of pS10584, a <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-2</ce:inf>-habouring plasmid of <ce:italic>Salmonella enterica</ce:italic> strain SJTUF10584 from a food sample in China (GenBank accession No. <ce:inter-ref id="intref0030" xlink:href="ncbi-n:KX058576" xlink:type="simple">KX058576</ce:inter-ref>) [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0260" refid="bib19">19</ce:cross-ref>]. The two plasmids were identical in IncI1 type, conjugal transfer region and genetic environment of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">CMY</ce:inf>. CMY-2-producing <ce:italic>Salmonella</ce:italic> spp., <ce:italic>E. coli</ce:italic> and <ce:italic>K. pneumoniae</ce:italic> of animal origin were identified [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0265" refid="bib26">26</ce:cross-ref>,<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0270" refid="bib27">27</ce:cross-ref>], suggesting the possible transmission of resistance genes between human and animal populations. Therefore, further investigation of the CMY-type cephalosporinase gene is needed not only in clinical isolates but also in food animals.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="p0160" view="all">Of particular concern is the spread of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf>; 1338 CRKPs consecutively collected in our hospital between July 2014 and July 2020 were subject to detection of <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf> by PCR. However, no other CMY-172-producing CRKPs were identified apart from the six strains mentioned in this study, indicating a limited and transient spread of this special clone in our hospital. Further investigation is warranted to detect <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf> in members of the Enterobacteriaceae other than CRKPs. Since the emergence of CAZ/AVI-resistant CRKPs, it is necessary to regularly detect CAZ/AVI susceptibility before and during its usage.</ce:para>
                  <ce:para id="p0165" view="all">In conclusion, <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf> is identified as providing a new mechanism for high-level resistance to CAZ/AVI in KPC-Kp. Compared with KPC-2/KPC-3 mutation, which confers CAZ/AVI resistance but restored carbapenem susceptibility [<ce:cross-ref id="crosref0275" refid="bib28">28</ce:cross-ref>], the occurrence of CMY-172 in KPC-Kp produces concurrent CAZ/AVI and carbapenem resistance, and further narrows antibiotic options. Given that <ce:italic>bla</ce:italic>
                     <ce:inf loc="post">CMY-172</ce:inf> is located in an IS<ce:italic>1294</ce:italic>-mediated element and is associated with a conjugative plasmid, a serious concern is the potential horizontal transfer of the gene, especially under the increased use of CAZ/AVI. Hence, an effective surveillance would be imperative.</ce:para>
               </ce:section>
